# WP 11.0A — Platform Architecture & Code Quality Review

## 1. Introduction

`WP 11.0A` (commit `e8cbec9`, joint with `WP 11.0B`) is `v0.11.0`'s
opening Work Package: an independent, evidence-based architecture and
code-quality review of the complete codebase as of the `v0.10.0` tag —
600 `.cs` files, ~66,000 lines under `src/`; ~243 test files, ~42,600
lines under `tests/`. Written from the TempestOS Principal Software
Architect role, review only — no code was modified. Its sole deliverable
is `docs/releases/v0.11.0/WP11.0A Platform Architecture Review.md`, and
its ten findings (`A-1`–`A-9`, `R-1`) are the direct origin of every
other Work Package in this release.

This retrospective was written retrospectively, under `WP 16.2B`, more
than four releases after `v0.11.0` shipped — `v0.11.0` closed with zero
Academy coverage for seven of its own eight Work Packages, a gap
`WP 11.9.0`'s own sign-off disclosed as non-blocking and recommended as
an immediate `v0.12.0` fast-follow that did not happen until now. It is
grounded strictly in `WP11.0A Platform Architecture Review.md` itself,
`WorkPackages.md`'s own `WP 11.0A` row, and the joint commit `e8cbec9`.

## 2. Purpose

Answer one question with direct evidence, not accumulated retrospective
narrative: is the codebase actually as sound as four release cycles of
self-reported "0 Warnings, N/N tests passing" claims say it is, and what
stands between it and a confident `v1.0`?

## 3. Background

By `v0.10.0`, TempestOS had shipped a full Engineering Domain model, six
real engineering disciplines, and a complete Avalonia desktop
application across `v0.7.0`–`v0.10.0`, entirely without a build server —
no CI, no automated test execution, ever. Every claim in over 100 prior
Work Package retrospectives that the build was clean and the tests
passed was a manually-run, self-reported, never-machine-verified
assertion. `v0.11.0` opens by finally auditing that accumulated claim
directly against the code, rather than continuing to build on top of it
unexamined.

## 4. The Problem

Nobody had independently verified, in four release cycles, whether the
platform's foundational architectural decisions (`ADR-0023`'s four-layer
model, `ADR-0033`'s Shell-above-Host boundary) still held in practice as
the codebase grew, or whether the newest layer (`Tempest.Desktop`,
`v0.10.0`) was repeating patterns the platform core had otherwise
avoided. Nobody had checked whether the Technical Debt Register's own
characterisation of known gaps matched what the code actually did, or
whether the governance documentation describing the platform was itself
still accurate.

## 5. The Design

Direct, evidence-based review rather than a documentation audit: reading
source directly, verifying dependency direction via `.csproj` references
and `using`-graph search (not the documentation's own claim about
itself), spot-checking ADR compliance against code for the
highest-risk decisions (Event Bus failure isolation, Command Dispatcher
failure model, DI-public vs. Host-owned boundaries), and
cross-checking governance claims (Technical Debt Register, Governance
Index) against the repository's actual state. Not exhaustive — a
targeted review of every major subsystem plus deep inspection of the
files most likely to carry risk.

Ten findings resulted, each classified Critical/High/Medium/Low and each
disposed **Before v1.0** or **Defer to v1.x**: zero Critical. Two High
findings drove the rest of this release directly — `R-1` (no CI/CD
pipeline exists anywhere in the repository) and `A-2` (two independently
maintained presentation stacks, no documented decision about the console
shell's future). A third High finding, `A-1` (Desktop composition-root
God Objects — `MainWindow.cs` at 1,556 lines, `EngineeringCockpit.cs` at
1,398), was deliberately left for `v0.12.0` rather than folded into this
release. A Medium finding, `A-5` (governance/documentation verbosity —
concretely, the Governance Index's own ADR count stale by 60 entries,
"39" against an actual 99), became the direct evidentiary trigger that
reprioritised `FCR-0005` (governance health-check tooling) into
`WP 11.2A`, below.

## 6. Alternatives Considered

A line-by-line audit of all 66,000 lines was considered and rejected as
disproportionate to the review's own purpose — a targeted review plus
deep inspection of the highest-risk subsystems (composition roots,
concurrency-sensitive code, plugin/security boundaries) gives
representative, defensible coverage without the cost of exhaustiveness a
`v1.0` readiness question does not actually require.

## 7. Why This Solution Was Chosen

Empirical verification catches exactly the class of drift a purely
documentary review cannot: this review's own dependency-direction check
(via `.csproj` and `using`-graph, not the architecture documents'
prose) and its own direct file count against the Governance Index's
stale "39 ADRs" claim are both examples of a finding that only exists
because the claim was checked against the artifact it describes, not
against another document describing the same artifact.

## 8. Architectural Principles

No ADR, architecture document, or production/test file was modified —
this is a review Work Package by explicit constraint; its findings
inform architecture, they do not constitute one. The review itself
independently reconfirmed `ADR-0023`'s four-layer model and
`ADR-0017`'s Discovery/Registration/Lifecycle exclusion from the DI
container both hold in the code, not merely in the documents describing
them.

## 9. Benefits

A concrete, prioritisable list of ten findings replaces four release
cycles of unverified assumption. Two of them (`R-1`, `A-2`) directly
scope five of this release's own remaining seven Work Packages; a third
(`A-5`) triggers `WP 11.2A`; the review's own "Overall verdict —
architecturally sound, engineering-disciplined, not yet
release-engineered" gives the Product Owner a defensible basis for
sequencing `v0.11.0` through `v1.0.0` (realised immediately afterward by
`WP 11.0B`).

## 10. Trade-offs

A review this broad necessarily samples rather than exhaustively
verifies — the review's own text is explicit that ADR compliance was
"spot-checked... not exhaustive coverage of all 99." Four findings
(`A-1`, `A-6`, `A-7`, `A-8`) were disposed **Defer to v1.x** or
**Strongly Recommended**, not fixed within this release — real,
disclosed debt carried forward rather than resolved immediately.

## 11. Common Mistakes

**Treating a large, disciplined codebase's own accumulated self-reports
as evidence.** The review's own central finding is that "0 Warnings,
N/N tests passing" repeated across 100+ retrospectives was true almost
certainly, but was never once independently, machine-verified — a
distinction that mattered enough to become this release's single
highest-priority finding (`R-1`).

## 12. Future Evolution

`WP 11.0B` (below) turns these ten findings into the scoped, sequenced
roadmap governing `v0.11.0` through `v1.0.0`. That roadmap's own §1
definition of `v1.0` scope ("Definition 2") is, five releases later,
proposed as the governing decision for `v1.0.0` itself (`D-021`,
`WP 16.0A`, Proposed at the time of this retrospective) — a direct line
from this review's own findings to the project's still-open `v1.0`
scope question.

## 13. Key Takeaways

- Verify a claim against the artifact it describes, not against another
  document describing the same artifact — the ADR-count and
  dependency-direction findings both exist only because this review did
  that.
- A sound architecture and an unverified build process are two
  different questions; this review answered both, separately, and found
  the first Pass and the second the release's single biggest gap.
- Ten findings, each disposed explicitly (before v1.0 / defer), is more
  actionable than an undifferentiated list — every subsequent
  `v0.11.0` Work Package traces to exactly one of them.
