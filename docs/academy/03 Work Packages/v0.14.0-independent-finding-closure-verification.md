# Governance — Independent Finding-Closure Verification, Register Closure, False-Claim Corrections

## 1. Introduction

`ec81bf6` records a full falsification report over the closures this
engineering body of work had already claimed — `TD-58`, `TD-59`, `TD-60` —
and over every previously-Resolved register row, catching two Risk
Register false claims and re-opening two closure gaps as `TD-63`/`TD-64`
along the way. A pre-`v0.14.0` engineering-body-of-work row; see the
naming rule in this set's "architecture audit becomes tracked debt"
article.

## 2. Purpose

To verify closures independently rather than take them on the closing
session's own word — because, as the commit records, the prior session's
closure report and its original sixteen-item finding list were never
committed anywhere and are unrecoverable.

## 3. Background

`docs/governance/Quality/Finding Closure Verification (2026-08-28).md`
carries the full report: provenance (nothing assumed closed on an
unrecoverable prior session's word), six-question closure verdicts for
`TD-58`/`TD-59`/`TD-60` with mutation evidence, re-verification of every
previously-Resolved register closure, and the disposition of every new
finding from an eight-discipline re-review.

## 4. The Problem

A register row marked Resolved is a claim, and a claim nobody has checked
against the repository is not evidence — it is a repetition. Two entries
the Risk Register itself had marked Retired since 2026-07-28 were still
appearing as live in the register's own index (`R2`, `R5`, `R7`, `R8`,
`R9`), contradicting its own Source of Truth section and a false
"verified … and vice versa" cross-check sentence. The Release Register's
`v0.13.1` row carried a stale "Not yet tagged, not merged, not published"
sentence contradicting the row's own Released status.

## 5. The Design

Re-verify every previously-Resolved closure against the repository, not
against the closing report. `TD-40` and `TD-52` did not survive that
check — their test-pinning gaps became `TD-63` and `TD-64` rather than
being silently left as Resolved. Ten new findings from the re-review were
added: `TD-57` (governance-register currency drift, including the
Companion-branch numbering collision), `TD-61` (symlink containment
bypass), `TD-62` (unauthenticated OpenAPI permission-map disclosure),
`TD-63`/`TD-64` (the two closure-test gaps just found), `TD-65`
(systemic accessibility gaps), `TD-66` (the refresh-architecture debt
`TD-58` deliberately left), `TD-67` (crash-window write ordering),
`TD-68` (`AsyncKeyedLock` growth), `TD-69` (DI silent-overwrite / optional-
parameter defects). Totals re-derived by direct row count: 69 tracked —
21 Resolved, 1 Partially resolved, 47 Open.

## 6. Alternatives Considered

Trusting the prior session's closure claims at face value is the
alternative this row rejects outright — the commit records that report as
unrecoverable and treats it as absent rather than as a source.

## 7. Why This Solution Was Chosen

Because a register whose Resolved rows have never been independently
checked is a register that can accumulate false claims silently, and this
one already had — two of them, found the moment somebody looked.

## 8. Architectural Principles

A closure claim and a verified closure are different facts, and a
governance register should record which one it holds. Re-deriving totals
by direct row count, rather than carrying forward a prior total, is the
same principle applied to arithmetic.

## 9. Benefits

Two false claims in the Risk Register corrected against its own recorded
truth. Two closure-test gaps that had been silently Resolved are now
tracked debt with names. Ten new findings from the re-review are on
record rather than lost with the session that found them.

## 10. Trade-offs

The eight-discipline re-review's findings are recorded as the ten TD
rows above; the review itself, like the sixteen-item list it followed, is
not separately preserved beyond this report and the rows it produced.

## 11. Common Mistakes

Treating a register's own "Resolved" label as proof rather than as a claim
requiring the same evidence any other finding would need. Carrying a
total forward across a pass rather than re-deriving it by direct count —
this row corrects exactly that arithmetic once and states the corrected
figure.

## 12. Future Evolution

The `WP-Z1` retrospective, later in the remediation programme, revisits
this exact discipline — re-deriving register totals and normalising Status
cells that lead with "Open" while ending with their own closure — showing
the pattern this row establishes recurs rather than being a one-time
correction.

## 13. Key Takeaways

- A closure report that is never committed does not exist for governance
  purposes, however recently it happened.
- Re-verify a Resolved row against the repository before trusting it as a
  source for the next pass.
- Re-derive register totals by direct row count rather than carrying a
  prior total forward across a pass.
