## 2. Inspection — what was already there

The brief's first instruction was to inspect rather than assume. The
decisive finding is that **the platform already had the concept this
workspace was missing**, already composed in the shipped Desktop path.

- `Calculation` (`src/Tempest.Core/EngineeringDomain/Implementation/Calculations.cs`)
  derives from `EngineeringObjectBase` and therefore composes `IRenamable`,
  `IHasParent`, `IHasLifecycle`, `IHasRelationships`, `IHasRevisions`,
  `ITraceable` and `IDeletable`.
- `CalculationsWorkspaceRegistration.Register` already registers handlers for
  `CreateCalculationObjectCommand`, `RenameCalculationObjectCommand`,
  `MoveCalculationObjectCommand`, `DeleteCalculationObjectCommand` and
  `SetCalculationStatusCommand`, plus rename/delete/revise factories.
- It is reached from the Desktop by `WorkspaceHost.StartAsync` →
  `EngineeringWorkspaceComposer.RegisterEngineeringDisciplines`. This is not
  a test-only composition.
- `CalculationTemplateRegistry.CalculatedByRelationshipKind` (`"calculatedBy"`)
  is the existing relationship linking an engineering object to the
  `CalculationRecord` it produced, already mapped to
  `RelationshipCategory.Calculation`.
- `ProjectMembership.ResolveOwningProjectAsync` is declared "the single
  definition of project membership in TempestOS", resolving it by walking
  the `IHasParent` chain.
- `CalculationObjectFactoryRegistry.RegisterRehydrators` is wired into the
  shared composer, and `EngineeringObjectRehydrationService.RebuildRelationshipsAsync`
  rebuilds `calculatedBy` edges from the durable per-document references, so
  both the object and its link survive a restart.

**What was actually missing** was none of that. It was that a calculation
run from the Engineering Calculations workspace never got one of these
objects. The workspace listed bare `CalculationRecord`s — immutable
evidence with no display name, no parent and no lifecycle — so there was
nothing to name, nothing to rename, nothing to organise and nothing to
retire.

`TD-147` was inspected directly, as instructed. Its subject is
`EngineeringObjectFactory<T>.CreateAsync`'s ordering — an object whose
initial durable state write fails is still registered in the repository and
cannot be unregistered. It is unrelated to this workspace, it is untouched
by this work, and it remains the register's only Release Blocking row. It
is **not** closed here. (§7 records where this pass runs into its shape on a
different path, and opens `TD-170` for that rather than borrowing `TD-147`'s
row.)

## 4. What was implemented

One new application-layer type, `EngineeringCalculationRegister`
(`src/Tempest.App/Engineering/`), and the surface that reaches it.

- **Naming.** After a calculation has run and been recorded, the register
  creates a governed `Calculation` object carrying the engineer's chosen
  name, links it to the record by the existing `calculatedBy` relationship,
  and parents it on the open project. A blank name takes a serviceable
  default (the calculation's own name and the UTC instant, invariant
  formatted) rather than losing a calculation that has already been
  performed over an empty text box.
- **Renaming** dispatches `RenameCalculationObjectCommand`. It changes
  `DisplayName` and nothing else: identity, revision history, lifecycle
  status, lifecycle history, relationships and the record itself are all
  untouched — asserted on the domain object, not on the read model.
- **Organisation** is `ProjectMembership.ResolveOwningProjectAsync`, the
  platform's single definition. The list shows which project a calculation
  belongs to; belonging to none is a supported state and is shown as such.
- **Retirement** dispatches `SetCalculationStatusCommand` to the nearest
  *retained terminal* lifecycle state the canonical transition table
  permits, preferring `Archived` where reachable and `Cancelled` where it is
  not. The first press describes what will happen and does nothing; the
  second acts. Retired calculations remain listed under "Show retired
  calculations", remain openable, and are never deleted.
- **Reporting.** A naming failure never presents as a failed calculation:
  the calculation is recorded durably first, and the failure is reported
  alongside the full result.

Desktop-layer discipline is unchanged: no arithmetic, no `Quantity<>`, no
unit conversion and no persistence semantics entered `Tempest.Desktop`. The
register is constructed once by `WorkspaceHost` over already-resolved
services and the coordinator once by `MainWindow`, per `ADR-0103`; neither
is DI-registered and neither references `MainWindow` or a sibling back.

## 5. What was deliberately not done

The brief was explicit that this is not a PDM system, and nothing here is
one. There are **no folders, no arbitrary hierarchy, no drag-and-drop, no
permissions administration, no enterprise search, no version-control UI and
no generic object-management framework.** Organisation is the one parent
edge the platform already had.

Also unchanged, deliberately:

- The four registered calculations with no governed entry point remain
  listed as **"not available here yet"**, with the reason. That is roadmap,
  not a defect, and the Colour Review Board was instructed not to grade it
  as one.
- The bracket section check itself — its arithmetic, its governed entry
  point, its reference pinning — is untouched.
- Sample modules are untouched and remain unable to make sample data look
  authoritative.
- No material release governance is bypassed, nothing is auto-released and
  no reviewer is hard-coded. `ReferenceReviewService` remains the single
  gate, taking the reviewer from the signed-in principal and the date from
  its injected clock.
- Opening a recorded calculation remains read-only and recalculates nothing.
- The shell, rail, ribbon, palette, Cockpit, design tokens and docking are
  untouched.

## 7. Architectural constraints found, and how they were met

Two constraints were found by reading the platform, not inferred, and both
changed the design.

**7.1 `Draft` → `Archived` is not a permitted transition.**
`LifecycleTransitionTable` reaches `Archived` only from `Superseded` or
`Obsolete`, themselves reachable only from `Released`. A governed
`Calculation` starts `Draft`, so the `calculations.archive` descriptor the
platform already registers cannot succeed against a new one. From `Draft`
the permitted targets are `InReview` and `Cancelled` alone. The workspace
therefore retires to the nearest **retained** terminal state the table
actually permits, and says on screen exactly which state it reached.
Recorded as `TD-169`, not worked around.

**7.2 `IDeletable.DeleteAsync` is a one-way door.**
`EngineeringObjectBase.DeleteAsync`'s own remarks state that no writer
anywhere clears `_isDeleted`, that there is no undelete on any facet, and
that every application read model filters deleted objects out. Using it to
take a calculation out of the active list would make work that has
contributed to traceable engineering vanish from the product with no
supported way back — precisely what the brief forbids. **This pass never
calls it.**

The board correctly pressed on the corollary: `Archived` and `Cancelled` are
themselves terminal, so a retirement cannot be undone either. The
difference, and it is the whole difference, is that a retired calculation
stays visible, listable, openable and traceable while a soft-deleted one
does not. Because the act is nonetheless irreversible, it now takes a
confirmation that names the state it will reach and says it cannot be moved
back.

## 10. Colour Review Board

Nine perspectives, run against `f9553f8` by three independent reviewers, each
instructed not to manufacture findings from absent roadmap functionality and
to cite `file:line` and a concrete failure scenario for anything graded RED
or AMBER.

**One 🔴 RED, raised independently by two perspectives:** the project a
calculation belongs to was read as a single `IHasParent` hop and labelled,
rather than asked of `ProjectMembership`. A calculation filed under a
Calculation Set inside a project would have been shown under the set's name
while the project directory, reading the same edge properly, said the
project — two contradictory answers to one governed question. **Fixed:** it
now asks `ProjectMembership.ResolveOwningProjectAsync`.

**AMBER findings and their disposition:**

| Finding | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Retirement reaches a terminal state with no way back, offered in one unconfirmed click, worded as though it merely hid a row | **Fixed.** Two-press confirmation naming the state and its terminality; tooltip corrected. |
| `calculatedBy` is many-valued but was read with `FirstOrDefault`, so a twice-executed calculation could bind to a different record after a restart | **Fixed.** Ordered by `CreatedAt`, newest taken, matching `CalculationRecordReader`. |
| `EngineeringCalculationRegister` was an optional constructor parameter, creating unreachable degraded branches | **Fixed.** Required; both branches removed. |
| `NameAsync`'s create-then-link has no compensation, and the message said "could not be named" | **Fixed as far as it can be, and disclosed.** A typed `EngineeringCalculationNamingException` reports precisely what happened. The residue cannot be compensated — the platform has no unregister — and is opened as `TD-170`. |
| Rename and retire dispatched from discarded Tasks with nothing catching | **Fixed.** Both guarded; a failure is said out loud. |
| Read-only Name box showed a stale name | **Fixed.** Opening a record fills it from that record. |
| The explanatory note was invisible to assistive technology | **Fixed.** Automation name and polite live setting added. |
| Two visible controls both labelled "Name" | **Fixed.** "Calculation name" and "Rename to". |
| `DefaultedName` used `CurrentCulture` for a *durable* display name — a Windows/Linux divergence invariant-culture CI could never catch | **Fixed.** Invariant, from the UTC instant. |

**YELLOW findings fixed:** the injected lifecycle table is used instead of a
fresh one; recovery is retirement-aware; the rename box survives a refresh
that is not a change of selection; `IsRetired` is derived from `Status`
rather than supplied beside it; the class remark claiming "no mutation of
its own" is corrected to state which two operations dispatch and which two
do not, and why; the Show-retired box has a tooltip and an empty selection
now says what to do; the Namespace Register prose that read "2 files to 4"
where the row read 5 is corrected; and the two untested paths the board
named — the naming failure and the `Archived`-before-`Cancelled` preference
— now have tests, as do the input controls that were driven without a
usability assertion.

**YELLOW findings accepted and not fixed, with reasons:**

- `SetCalculationStatusCommandHandler` ignores `TargetKind` and tests only
  `is IHasLifecycle`. Pre-existing platform behaviour, not reachable from
  this surface (the listing is Kind-scoped), and not this pass's to change.
- Naming, renaming and retiring proceed with no signed-in principal,
  recording the store's "unknown" principal. Platform-wide pre-existing
  convention — `WorkspaceHost` is written to support a session with no
  principal — and changing it is a board decision.
- Two rapid Show-retired toggles issue overlapping listings with no ordering
  guard. Low harm: the next refresh corrects it, and adding a sequencer here
  would be the only one of its kind on this surface.

## 11. Residual risk

1. **`TD-147` is still open and still Release Blocking.** Nothing here
   touches it, and nothing here should be read as reducing it.
2. **`TD-170`** — a naming whose link write fails leaves a durable named
   object with no evidence. It requires a durable write to fail mid-
   operation, the calculation itself is never lost, and the partial state is
   reported precisely. It cannot be closed without `TD-147`.
3. **`TD-169`** — a freshly recorded calculation retires to `Cancelled`
   rather than `Archived`. The capability works and states which state it
   reached; the label an engineer would prefer needs a platform-wide
   lifecycle decision.
4. **Retirement is irreversible.** Confirmed before it happens, and the
   calculation remains held and openable, but there is no un-retire.
5. **Four of five registered calculations still have no input form here.**
   Listed as not available yet, with the reason. Roadmap.
6. **No Windows validation.** Automated coverage is headless Linux only.
