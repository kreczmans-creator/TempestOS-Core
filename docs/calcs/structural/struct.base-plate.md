# S6 — Column base plate, EN 1993-1-8 (`struct.base-plate@1.5.0`)

**In plain terms.** A column stands on a concrete foundation through a steel base plate, which spreads the column's load over the concrete and, when the column is bent, is pulled up on one side by holding-down bolts. The worksheet works out how much the grout and concrete can bear, how much tension the bolts take under axial load and moment, how thick the plate must be to carry that, and whether friction and the bolts resist the shear.

**Scope.** An unstiffened rectangular base plate under an I/H, RHS or CHS column on grouted concrete: bearing resistance, the force regime and holding-down bolt tension for a major-axis moment, the tension T-stub of the plate and the compression T-stub, shear by friction plus anchor bolts, and the plate thickness that satisfies them together. **Independent check demanded** (the author cannot check it, DA-12); every input outside its range is a hard FAIL, and each numeric input's hard limit equals its stated range (plate 100–1500 mm, t_p 10–100 mm, f_y 235–460 MPa, f_ck 20–50 MPa, α 1–3, α_cc 0.8–1.0, γ_c 1.2–1.8, γ_M0 1.0–1.25, γ_M2 1.1–1.5, C_f,d 0–0.45, and so on). The concrete and anchor design is **not** done here: the sheet outputs the holding-down bolt tension and the shear per anchor for Hilti PROFIS, and the PROFIS report is filed as Evidence and referenced by `profis_ref`.

**Method.**
- Bearing (cl. 6.2.5): `f_cd = α_cc f_ck/γ_c`; `f_jd = β_j α f_cd` with β_j = 2/3 fixed and shown; `c = t_p √[f_y/(3 f_jd γ_M0)]`. `α` is the user's concentration factor √(A_c1/A_c0) (EN 1992-1-1 cl. 6.7, ≤ 3); a value above 1 warns until `alpha_basis` states the foundation geometry. β_j = 2/3 assumes the grout conditions of cl. 6.2.5 (strength at least 0.2·f_ck, thickness at most 0.2 of the smaller plate dimension); enter `f_ck_g` and `t_g` to have them checked (warning `grout`), otherwise the sheet records the assumption.
- Effective area `A_eff` round the column footprint. I/H: `2(b + 2c)(t_f + 2c) + (h − 2t_f − 2c)(t_w + 2c)`; when `h − 2t_f − 2c < 0` the strips overlap and the full rectangle `(b + 2c)(h + 2c)` is used, and the sheet says so. RHS: `(b + 2c)(h + 2c) − (b − 2t − 2c)(h − 2t − 2c)`, inner terms ≥ 0. CHS: `π[(D/2 + c)² − (D/2 − t − c)²]`, inner term ≥ 0. **Where c would pass the plate edge it is cut back to the edge** (outstand `min(c, (B − b)/2)` across the width, `min(c, (L − h)/2)` along the length, `min(c, (min(B, L) − D)/2)` for CHS), so no area outside the plate is counted; the whole is also capped at B·L. This is never larger than the uncut area clipped to B·L, and equal to it when nothing is cut.
- Axial (cl. 6.2.8.2): `N_j,Rd = f_jd A_eff`; `u_N = max(N_Ed, 0)/N_j,Rd`. `t_req_N` is the smallest t_p in 10–100 mm (bisection) carrying N_c,Ed in bearing alone; shown as 10 mm when carried at the minimum, "> 100 mm" when 100 mm is not enough, "— (no compression)" when N_Ed ≤ 0.
- Moment (cl. 6.2.8.3, Table 6.7, simplified). The compression resultant acts at `z_C` from the column axis (default from the section: I/H `(h − t_f)/2 − (c − c_cut)/2`, RHS `(h − t)/2 − (c − c_cut)/2` (the bearing strip runs from c inside the flange to the outstand outside it, so when that outstand is cut back to the plate edge the strip's centroid moves inward by `(c − c_cut)/2`; with nothing cut these are `(h − t_f)/2` and `(h − t)/2`), CHS the centroid of the half annulus `4(R³ − r³)/(3π(R² − r²))`, R = D/2 + c, r = D/2 − t − c; or `z_C_in` entered); the bolt row is at `x_b` from the axis (outside the column face, inside the plate edge); `z = z_C + x_b`. Three regimes:
  1. Tension at one bolt row, compression under the other flange (`C_Ed = (M_Ed + N_Ed x_b)/z ≥ 0` and `T_Ed = C_Ed − N_Ed ≥ 0`).
  2. Both sides in compression (`N_Ed > 0`, `T_Ed` would be negative): lever arm `2 z_C`, `C = N_Ed/2 ± M_Ed/(2 z_C)`, no bolt tension.
  3. Both bolt rows in tension (`N_Ed < 0`, `C_Ed` would be negative): lever arm `2 x_b`, `T = −N_Ed/2 ± M_Ed/(2 x_b)`, `n_t` bolts in each row.
  `F_t,Ed = T_Ed/n_t` at the more loaded row is the port to PROFIS (`T_Ed_2` and `C_Ed_2` are the other row and flange; the regime is stated on the sheet). With symmetric `z_C = x_b = z/2` regime 1 reduces to `M_Ed/z ∓ N_Ed/2`. The compression T-stub is checked (only when M_Ed > 0 and the resultant is compressive, cl. 6.2.6.9, 6.2.6.10): `C_Rd = f_jd A_flange`, A_flange = `(b + 2c)(t_f + 2c)` (I/H), `(b + 2c)(t + 2c)` (RHS), half of A_eff (CHS), capped at half of A_eff.
- Plate tension T-stub (cl. 6.2.6.11, 6.2.4, Table 6.2): `M_pl,Rd = 0.25 l_eff t_p² f_y/γ_M0` (l_eff is the total for the n_t bolts of the row). **Prying is off by default**: the resistance is `min(2 M_pl,Rd/m, n_t F_t,Rd)`, the conservative case and usual for cast-in bolts. With `prying` on: mode 1 `4 M_pl,Rd/m`, mode 2 `(2 M_pl,Rd + n n_t F_t,Rd)/(m + n)`, `n = min(e_min, 1.25 m)`, mode 3 `n_t F_t,Rd`; the **prying force Q is not included in F_t,Ed and PROFIS must add it** (warning `prying`). `u_T = T_Ed/T_Rd`, `u_bt = F_t,Ed/F_t,Rd` (cl. 6.2.6.12).
- Shear (cl. 6.2.2): the anchor bolt shear is `F_2,vb,Rd = α_bc f_ub A_s/γ_M2` with `α_bc = 0.44 − 0.0003 f_yb` (f_yb in MPa, valid 235–640 MPa; 0.248 for class 8.8), not the Table 3.4 bolt shear; `F_vb,Rd = min(F_1,vb,Rd, F_2,vb,Rd)` with `F_1,vb,Rd` the bearing resistance from S4; `V_Rd = C_f,d max(N_Ed, 0) + n_b F_vb,Rd`; `u_V = V_Ed/V_Rd`; `V_b,Ed = max(V_Ed − C_f,d N_c,Ed, 0)/n_b` per anchor for PROFIS. A column in net tension has no friction.
- Required thickness `t_req`: the smallest t_p in 10–100 mm with `u_N`, `u_C` and `u_T` all ≤ 1, with the lever arm `z_C` (the strip or half-annulus centroid, which depends on `c` and so on t_p, unless `z_C_in` is entered) and the Table 6.7 forces `C_Ed`, `T_Ed` re-evaluated at every trial thickness, so the reported `t_req` passes its own checks when entered as t_p (scan in 0.05 mm steps, then bisection in the first passing step); "bolts govern" when `T_Ed > n_t F_t,Rd` at the 100 mm end of the range so no thickness helps; "> 100 mm" when 100 mm is not enough.
- `u_max` is the largest utilisation. The anchorage condition "Anchorage designed externally (PROFIS report attached as Evidence)" is made, and passes, **only when `profis_ref` carries Evidence provenance** (a report attached with "Attach external report"; the library refuses an evidence source that does not point at a file attached to the calculation, and the condition reads "external report attached: <file name>" so the issued sheet names the file); typed text, a manual or assumed reference, or nothing gives a warning (rule `anchorage`, "not attached") and no check, so it never counts as a PASS.

**Inputs.** `column` (I/H, RHS, CHS); `h`, `b` 50–1200 mm, `t_f`, `t_w`, `t` 3–100 mm, `D` 50–1200 mm (only those of the chosen section are needed); `B`, `L` 100–1500 mm, `t_p` 10–100 mm; `f_y` (record `steel.strengths`); `f_ck`; optional `f_ck_g`, `t_g`; `alpha_cc`, `gamma_c` (record `ec2.national-annex`); `gamma_M2` (record `ec3.national-annex`); `gamma_M0` (record `ec3-1-1.national-annex`); `C_fd` (record `joint.friction`); `alpha` (default 1, no concentration claimed) and optional `alpha_basis`; `N_Ed` −5000…10000 kN (**compression positive**, the opposite of S3's tension-positive `N_Ed`; from S2 take the reaction `R1_y`, not the member force `N_e1`), `M_Ed` 0–2000 kN·m, `V_Ed` 0–2000 kN; `n_t`, `n_b` (whole numbers, n_b ≥ 2 n_t), `x_b` (h/2 < x_b < L/2), optional `z_C_in`; **required, with no default,** `m` (≤ x_b − h/2) and `l_eff` (from the cl. 6.2.6.5 patterns; they follow from the geometry, so a silent default could be 30 % unsafe); `prying`, `e_min` (only with prying, ≤ L/2 − x_b), `F_t_Rd`, `F_1_vb_Rd` (from S4), `f_ub`, `f_yb`, `A_s`; optional `profis_ref`.

**Outputs.** `f_cd`, `beta_j`, `f_jd`, `c`, `A_eff`, `N_j_Rd`, `u_N`, `t_req_N`, `t_req`, `z_C`, `z`, `C_Ed`, `C_Ed_2`, `T_Ed`, `T_Ed_2`, `F_t_Ed`, `A_flange`, `C_Rd`, `u_C`, `M_pl_Rd`, `T_Rd_1`, `n_pry`, `T_Rd_2`, `T_Rd_3`, `T_Rd`, `T_mode`, `regime`, `u_T`, `u_bt`, `alpha_bc`, `F_2_vb_Rd`, `F_vb_Rd`, `F_f_Rd`, `V_Rd`, `u_V`, `V_b_Ed`, `u_max`. **Ports.** `F_t_Ed` (kN per bolt → Hilti PROFIS), `V_b_Ed` (kN per anchor → PROFIS), `N_j_Rd`, `u_max`. Links in: S1 section, S2/S5 actions, S4 (`F_t_Rd`, `A_s`, `F_1_vb_Rd`). **Checks.** concrete bearing; compression T-stub (M_Ed > 0 and compressive resultant); tension T-stub; bolt tension; shear; anchorage condition.

**Limitations.** Static loading; uniaxial (major-axis) moment; unstiffened plate; the simplified T-stub model (one compression resultant per flange and one tension resultant per bolt row; the web gets no credit in the moment check, so the compression check jumps at M_Ed = 0⁺ where the whole footprint resists); one `l_eff` for modes 1 and 2; prying is the user's `prying` switch, not a computed L_b ≤ L_b*, and Q is not included in F_t,Ed; anchor shear assumes standard clearance holes and equal sharing (oversized or slotted holes are outside this sheet); β_j = 2/3 depends on the grout conditions (checked only when entered); the CHS compression side is half the annulus (approximate); **anchorage, pull-out, breakout, edge distances and the tension–shear interaction of the anchors are designed externally in PROFIS**; no shear key, no weld design, no fatigue.

**Notices (1.3.0).** An entered `z_C_in` at or beyond `L/2` is blocked (the resultant would sit outside the plate, which understates `T_Ed`), and one that differs from the section's own `z_C` by more than the outstand `c` warns that `z_C` is the distance to the centre of the compression flange or wall, not the lever arm `z`. An `f_ub` above 800 MPa (class 10.9 and above) is blocked (rule `alpha_bc`) unless the anchor design's governing utilisation `u_profis` is entered, because α_bc is not defined above `f_yb` 640 MPa; with `u_profis` entered the sheet runs and warns (`alpha_bc_profis`) that `u_V` is not reliable and the report's utilisation counts in `u_max`. A warning says that a single `f_y` is used at every thickness when `t_p` or `t_req` is above the first EN 10025-2 step (16 mm); it names only the thicknesses that are above 16 mm. With the bearing outstand cut at the plate edge a thicker plate can raise `u_C` (the strip's lever arm shortens as `c` grows); the sheet warns when 1.25·`t_p` gives a larger `u_C` than `t_p`, because `t_req` is then the thinnest passing plate, not a lower bound for thicker ones.

**Changes in 1.5.0 (struct.base-plate-03, -R1, chain.steel-support-04, from the C-1 verification).** -03: an anchor bolt strength above 800 MPa was only a warning (`f_ub` 1000, `f_yb` 640, `V_Ed` 250 kN: α_bc 0.248, `u_V` 0.983, a pass, where the formula at the bolt's real yield of about 900 MPa gives α_bc 0.17 and `u_V` 1.29); it is now a block unless `u_profis` is entered. -R1: the `f_y_thickness` warning no longer says that `t_req` is above 16 mm when it is below (S6 with `t_p` 25 mm and `t_req` 15.423 mm). chain.steel-support-04: the anchorage check cites "PROFIS report (EN 1992-4)", not EN 1993-1-8 cl. 6.2.6.12 (anchor bolts in tension), which is the steel bolt check `u_bt`.

**Anchorage result (1.4.0).** The anchorage is designed in PROFIS and the sheet cannot compute it, so attaching a report is a filing, not a result: an attached placeholder passed with `u` 0 while no concrete failure mode of the anchors (cone, pull-out, edge, shear, interaction), which usually govern, was computed. The optional input `u_profis` takes the report's governing utilisation; entered, it is checked (`u_anchorage ≤ 1`) and counts in `u_max`; blank, the sheet warns (rule `anchorage-result`) that the anchorage is not assessed and `u_max` covers the steel parts only, whether or not a report is attached.
