# E8 — Containment fill, bend radius and cable pulling (`elec.containment@1.2.0`)

**In plain terms.** Cables laid in conduit, trunking or tray must not fill it too far, must not be bent tighter than their diameter allows, and must not be pulled so hard that they are damaged. The worksheet works out how full the containment is against the space factor you set, whether the bends are wide enough, and the tension and sidewall pressure on the cables as they are pulled along the route, in both directions.

**Scope.** The fill of a conduit, trunking or tray by the cables in it against a space factor the user enters; the bend radius of a pull route against a multiple of the largest cable diameter the user enters; and the pull of the cables through a route of straight lengths and bends, worked in both directions, with the direction that passes every check recommended (the lower maximum tension when both or neither pass). No IET or other cable and conduit factor table, no space-factor value and no bend or friction value ships (Addendum B §B5): the factor method is not offered, and every limit is a record the user enters from their own references and the cable maker's data. Standards are cited by clause number and title only.

**Method.** Fill is geometric: `A_cab = Σ nᵢ·π·dᵢ²/4`, `A_int = π·D²/4` (conduit) or `W·H` (trunking or tray, internal width × depth), `fill = A_cab/A_int`, utilisation `u_fill = fill/f_max`. Bend radius: `R_min = n_bend·d_max` with `d_max` the largest cable diameter; each bend `u_b = R_min/R`, the check is the tightest bend. Weight `w = Σ nᵢ·mᵢ` (kg/m), force per metre `w·g·w_c` (`w_c` your weight-correction factor, default 1), `g = 9.80665 m/s²`. Pulling, in pull order from a start tension `T₁`: straight length `T₂ = T₁ + w·g·w_c·μ·L` (w in kg/m times g gives N/m); bend `T₂ = T₁·e^(w_c·μ·θ)` (capstan equation, friction scaled by `w_c`, θ in radians); sidewall pressure at a bend `SWP = T₂/R` (N/m). The route is run as listed (direction a) and reversed (direction b), with the same start tension; tension never falls along a pull, so the maximum is the end tension. The recommended direction is the only one that passes every tension and SWP check, if exactly one does; otherwise the one with the lower maximum tension (equal tension: the lower SWP; equal both: either). Cable limit, load shared equally so each cable carries `T/N` and the weakest governs: `T_cab = k_n·N·min(T_max,ᵢ)` (`k_n` your derating for more than 3 cables, 1 if none entered), `u_T = max_i (T/N)/(k_n·T_max,ᵢ) = max_tension/T_cab`, with one working line per cable. Grip or pulling-eye limit `T_grip` (separate, optional): `u_grip = max_tension/T_grip`. `u_P = P_max/P_lim`. The largest cable must also fit: diameter below the conduit ID, or not above the smaller side of the trunking or tray.

**Inputs.** `cables` table (name, `od` mm, `count` whole number, `mass` kg/m each, optional `T_max` N each); `cont_type` (conduit or trunking/tray); `D_c` or `W_c` and `H_c` (mm); `f_max` space factor (record `containment.space-factor`, no default, required); `n_bend` (record `cable.bend-radius`, optional); `route` table (kind Straight or Bend; `L` m for a straight, `theta` degrees and `R` mm for a bend); `T_1` start tension (default 0 N); `mu` (record `containment.friction`, required when there is a route); `w_c` weight-correction factor (record `containment.weight-correction`, optional, blank means 1); `T_grip`, `P_lim` and `k_n` (record `cable.pulling-limit`, all optional, no values ship). Route-only values entered without a route warn that they are unused. Hard limits: diameters 0.1 to 300 mm, count 1 to 1000, mass 0 to 1000 kg/m, cable `T_max` at least 1 N, bend angle up to 720° per row, μ 0 to 2, space factor 0.01 to 1, 40 cable rows and 50 route rows. A tension above 1e12 N is refused, not shown.

**Outputs.** `A_cab`, `A_int`, `fill` (%), `u_fill`, `OD_max`, `w`; `R_min`, `u_bend`; `T_max_a`, `T_max_b`, `P_max_a`, `P_max_b`, `max_tension`, `P_max` (recommended direction), `T_cab`, `u_T`, `u_grip`, `u_P`, and the text `direction` ("Reverse: pull from segment 5 to segment 1", "As listed: …", or "Either direction"). One working step per segment and direction (`Ta_i`, `Tb_i`, `Pa_i`, `Pb_i`) and one per cable (`u_Tc_i`). **Ports.** `fill` (`cont.fill`) and `max_tension` (`cont.max_tension`); links E4 and E3 (the conductor sized in E4 and the run checked in E3 are the cables entered here; their diameters and masses come from the cable data sheet, and no port binds into the cable table). **Checks.** Fill against the space factor; the largest cable fits the containment; bend radius against the cable minimum (the cable maker's limit; BS 7671 reg. 522.8 cited for context); pulling tension against the weakest cable's limit; against the grip limit; sidewall pressure against the limit (each only when its limit is given). **Warnings.** No `n_bend` with bends in the route; no tension limit (a cable without `T_max`); no `P_lim` with bends; three equal cables in a conduit with 1.05·D/d between 2.8 and 3.2 (jam ratio, derived geometry, 5 % allowance for ovality); `k_n` entered with 3 cables or fewer (not applied).

**Limitations.** Circular cables counted by overall diameter, no packing rules, no cable or conduit factors (the jam-ratio warning is the only jamming check). Straight lengths are horizontal and the cable weight bears on the containment over the full length (enter a cradled or triangular correction as `w_c`, which scales the weight on straights and the friction in bends, so a bend is `T₁·e^(w_c·μ·θ)`); inclines, vertical runs, rollers and pulleys are not modelled (fold them into μ). The bend radius uses the individual cable diameter; for a triplexed or bundled assembly enter the assembly diameter from the cable maker. One μ for the whole route; static and running friction not distinguished. Bends use the capstan equation only (no weight term). SWP is the whole bundle's tension over the bend radius; enter the cable maker's per-cable limit unchanged (conservative for a triangular arrangement, roughly equal for a cradled one; never multiply it by the cable count). Load is shared equally between cables on one pulling head; unequal sharing is not modelled.

**Changes in 1.2.0 (elec.containment-01).** Tension round a bend in the horizontal plane now includes the cable's weight on the containment floor: `T₂ = T₁·cosh(μ_e·θ) + √(T₁² + (w·g·w_c·R)²)·sinh(μ_e·θ)`, `μ_e = w_c·μ`. A heavy cable (10 kg/m) on a 3 m radius, 90°, `μ` 0.3, `T₁` 100 N gives 263.2 N where the capstan equation gave 160.2 N, and a pull that starts at a bend from zero tension no longer reads zero. The input `bend_model` keeps the capstan equation `T₂ = T₁·e^(μ_e·θ)` as the option for a weightless pull (and the known-answer cases that were derived from it); the two agree to about 0.1 % for a light bundle on a small radius.

**Test cases.** All Confirmed and analytic, recomputed in python3: `ka-fill-3x10-in-25` (3 × 10 mm in a 25 mm conduit: 48 % exactly; with a five-segment route at μ 0.35: 154.919528 N as listed, 138.420988 N reversed, SWP 461.4812 / 284.2706 N/m, T_cab 1200 N), `ka-pull-tray-two-bends` (mixed bundle in 100 × 50 trunking from 100 N: 1074.235304 N as listed, 941.126090 N reversed; T_cab 3 × 150 = 450 N, u_T 2.0914, FAIL because the 150 N control cable carries a third of the pull), `ka-direction-passes-every-check` (the lower-tension direction, 1166.942 N, presses 1716 N/m against a 1500 N/m limit, so the reverse, 1281.142 N and 1010.413 N/m, is recommended), `ka-capstan-180` (T₂ = 200·e^(0.35π) = 600.567352 N, SWP 1501.41838 N/m), `ka-trunking-fill` (12 × 8 mm in 100 × 50: 12.0637 %, u_fill 0.2872 at an illustrative factor of 0.42), `ka-bend-too-tight` (R_min 120 mm against a 100 mm bend: u_b = 1.2). **Not pinned — owner check:** a cable maker's published pulling-guide example (straight plus bend tension and sidewall pressure); none is stated here because its inputs and answer cannot be quoted with confidence, so no Unconfirmed or Secondary case is declared. Every record value in the known-answer cases (space factor, bend multiple, friction, limits) is illustrative, not from any standard. The clause citations are from memory and should be checked against the owner's copies: BS 7671 reg. 522.8 (and its bend-radius sub-regulation), BS EN 61386-1 (cited as general), and Beer and Johnston Statics Ch. 8 (belt friction).
