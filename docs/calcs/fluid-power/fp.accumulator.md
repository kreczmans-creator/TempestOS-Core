# F2 — Accumulator sizing, gas law (`fp.accumulator@1.3.2`)

**In plain terms.** A hydraulic accumulator is a vessel with a gas cushion that stores fluid under pressure and gives it back when the pump cannot keep up. Gas gets stiffer as it is squeezed, so the volume of fluid you can take from it depends on the pressures at which it is charged and works. The worksheet works out the gas volume and pre-charge pressure needed to deliver the fluid you want between two pressures, picks the next standard size, and flags the bar-litre product against the pressure-equipment rules.

**Scope.** The pre-charge gas volume V0 of a hydraulic accumulator that delivers (or absorbs) a liquid volume ΔV between the lowest working pressure p1 and the highest p2, the pre-charge pressure at the charging temperature, the next size from the user's size list, and the bar·litre product PS·V of the vessel against the PSSR Schedule 1 250 bar·L exception. Three duties: energy storage, shock and pulsation (screening only; the pressure wave is not modelled) and emergency stroke (ΔV is the stroke volume, linked from the cylinder circuit F1). **Not a pressure-vessel code check**: no PD 5500, EN 13445 or ASME VIII calculation, no PED / UK PER or PSSR compliance statement (ADR-0170, Addendum B §B5). Every input outside its valid range is a hard FAIL (hard limit = valid range).

**Method.** Absolute pressures `pa = p + p_atm` (Gauge entry) or as entered (Absolute entry), through the shared `GasLaw` kernel (`Tempest.Calc.Engine.Physics.GasLaw`, also behind B6). **Gas process (1.3.0).** The sheet states how the gas is charged to p2 and discharged to p1 (input `process`, output `process`). *Same exponent both ways*, `GasLaw.ChargeVolume`: `V0 = ΔV / [(pa0/pa1)^(1/n) − (pa0/pa2)^(1/n)]`, n = 1 (isothermal) … 1.4 (adiabatic), for a gas charged as fast as it is discharged (a pulsation or a fast cycle). *Slow charge, discharge at n*, `GasLaw.ChargeVolumeSplit`: a held accumulator is charged slowly, so isothermally, to p2 (`V2 = V0·pa0/pa2`) and then discharged at n to p1 (`V1 = V2·(pa2/pa1)^(1/n)`), `ΔV = V1 − V2`, so `V0 = ΔV·(pa2/pa0) / [(pa2/pa1)^(1/n) − 1]`; one exponent from the pre-charge to both pressures understates the volume of a held accumulator (the same hydraulic power unit duty needs 12.7 % more gas volume). *By duty* (the default) is the slow charge in an emergency stroke and the same exponent both ways in energy storage; the shock and pulsation duty always uses one exponent. The volume from either form is multiplied by the real-gas factor `f_rg` (default 1, **at least 1**: nitrogen's compressibility factor exceeds 1 at high pressure, so a factor below 1 would make the accumulator smaller) and times the temperature factor `f_T = (T_max + 273.15)/(T_min + 273.15)`. The pre-charge p0 is defined at T_max and V0 is sized there; at the lowest operating temperature the same gas delivers only `T_min/T_max` of ΔV, so V0 grows by `f_T` (with `T_min = T_max` the factor is 1). Pre-charge at the charging temperature `p0(T_c) = pa0 · (T_c + 273.15)/(T_max + 273.15)` (the addendum writes 273; the 0.15 K changes the result by under 0.01 %); `p0_set` is that pressure on the entered basis (gauge or absolute). Rules (records), both on **absolute** pressures whichever basis the pressures are entered on: `u_r0 = pa0/(r_0·pa1)` with r_0 = 0.9 (p0 at T_max) and `u_rp = (pa2/p0_min)/r_p` with r_p = 4 and `p0_min = pa0/f_T` (p0 at T_min), both PASS/FAIL checks. The `accumulator.rules` record says to note whether the manufacturer states the ratio on gauge or absolute pressures: the Parker numbers read as gauge fail the 0.9·p1 rule by 0.11 %, read as absolute they sit exactly on it. Next size: the smallest listed size not below V0. `bar·L = PS · V` with **PS the vessel's own maximum allowable pressure (bar gauge, entered; blocked below p2 gauge)** and V the vessel volume (`V_vessel`, entered; blocked below V0) or else the selected size; without PS and a volume the `pssr` output reads "indication not available — enter the vessel's PS and volume" and never "below 250".

**Inputs.** `mode`, `dV` 0.001–1000 L, `process` (By duty / Same exponent both ways / Slow charge, discharge at n), `p_basis` (Gauge / Absolute), `p_0`, `p_1`, `p_2` 0–700 bar, `p_atm` 0.5–1.1 bar, `n` 1–1.4 (the discharge exponent; also the charge exponent with the same exponent both ways), `f_rg` 1–1.5, `T_max` −40…150 °C, `T_min` −40…150 °C (default 20; not above T_max), `T_c` −40…100 °C; optional `PS` 0–1500 bar gauge and `V_vessel` 0.01–5000 L (the PSSR indication); records `accumulator.rules` (`r_0` 0.1–1, `r_p` 1–20) and `accumulator.sizes` (optional table `sizes`, column `V`, 0.01–5000 L). Blocks: `p_0` zero or above `p_1`, or `p_2` not above `p_1` (absolute); on the Absolute basis any pressure below `p_atm` (`basis`, 1.3.1); `T_min` above `T_max`; `PS` below p2 (gauge); `V_vessel` below V0.

**Outputs.** `V0` (L), `V_sel` and `u_V` (when a size list is entered and a size is large enough), `p0` (absolute, at the charging temperature), `p0_set`, `p0_min`, `f_T`, `u_r0`, `u_rp`, `bar_litres` (only when PS and a volume are given), `pssr` (text), `process` (text, the gas process the volume is sized for). **Ports.** `V0`, `p0`, `bar_litres` (absent until PS and a volume are given) (`acc.V0`, `acc.p0`, `acc.bar_litres`). **Checks.** pre-charge within the p0/p1 rule; pressure ratio within the p2/p0 rule; a listed size covers V0 (only when a list is entered and none is large enough). **Warnings.** `realgas` above 200 bar absolute (ideal-gas volume, or your factor in force); `shock` for the screening duty; `exponent` for n below 1.3 in a shock or emergency duty; `process` when an emergency stroke is sized with one exponent both ways (the held accumulator is then undersized) or when a slow charge is chosen for the shock duty (not applied); `size`; constants not taken from a record.

**Limitations.** Ideal gas unless the user supplies `f_rg` (no nitrogen real-gas table ships); single gas pre-charge, no gas-side heat-transfer model (n is the user's), no bladder or piston friction, no oil compressibility or leakage; the shock duty is a gas-volume screening only. The size list and the pre-charge rules are the manufacturer's data and are not shipped. The 250 bar·L indication is PS times the vessel volume as entered (the nominal gas volume of the selected size unless a vessel volume is entered); applicability of PSSR (relevant fluid, definitions) is for the competent person.

**Test cases.** Analytic, recomputed in python3 (Confirmed): `ka-parker-numbers-absolute` (the Parker HY10-4004/UK numbers p0 90, p1 100, p2 210 bar, ΔV 14 L, isothermal, **read as absolute bar**, T_min = T_max: V0 = 14/(0.9 − 0.428571) = 29.69697 L, p0 81.64475 bar at 20 °C from 50 °C, u_rp 0.58333); `ka-parker-numbers-gauge` (the same numbers as gauge bar: 29.80704 L, and the p0 ≤ 0.9·p1 rule fails by 0.1 %); `ka-adiabatic` (n = 1.4, a gas charged as fast as it is discharged, so entered with *Same exponent both ways*: 36.69279 L; an analytic recompute of the one-exponent relation, not a published example); `ka-charge-temperature` (80 °C to 20 °C: 74.70905 bar abs); `ka-size-and-real-gas` (f_rg 1.05: 31.18182 L, next size 32 L, PS 230 bar gauge on the 32 L: 7360 bar·L); `ka-below-pssr` (1.25 L, PS 110 bar gauge, 2 L vessel: 220 bar·L); `ka-ps-above-working-pressure` (p2 100 bar gauge, 2.4 L vessel: 240 bar·L at the working pressure but 264 bar·L at a PS of 110 bar gauge, so the exception no longer applies); `ka-temperature-range` (20 to 50 °C: f_T 1.102337, V0 32.73606 L, next size 50 L, p0 at T_min 81.64475 bar, u_rp 0.64303). The quoted "about 29.7 to 29.8 L" fits both readings (29.697 as absolute, 29.807 as gauge + 1.01325 bar); the absolute reading puts p0 exactly on the 0.9·p1 limit, which is why it is the one used for the worked example. **Unconfirmed:** `ka-held-slow-charge-fast-discharge` (an emergency stroke, held at p2: isothermal charge and adiabatic discharge, the same numbers: 46.74304 L against 36.69279 L with one exponent, the held gas 20.03273 L at p2 and 34.03273 L at p1; the relation as manufacturers' sizing guides give it, recomputed in python3 and cited to the Parker HY10-4004/UK sizing title only, to be checked against the owner's copy of the manufacturer's sizing guide). **Unconfirmed + Secondary:** `ka-parker-hy10-4004` (V0 about 29.7 L and the catalogue's 32 L selection, as the addendum quotes the Parker example), to be checked against the owner's copy of the catalogue, including whether its pressures are gauge or absolute. **Not pinned (owner check):** the NFPA "How to Size an Accumulator" temperature example (V2 = 1347.77 cm³): its inputs are not stated in the addendum.

**Linked pressures (1.2.0).** A pressure input linked from another calculation (the cylinder's supply pressure `p`, gauge, into `p_2`) is read on this sheet's `p_basis`. In Absolute mode the linked gauge value would be read one atmosphere low, so the sheet warns (`link-basis`) for each of `p_0`, `p_1`, `p_2` that is linked. The default basis is Gauge, which the hydraulic power unit pack keeps.

**Slow charge, fast discharge (1.3.0).** Before 1.3.0 the sheet sized every duty with one exponent from the pre-charge to both working pressures, which is right only when the charge is as fast as the discharge. A held accumulator (emergency stroke) is charged slowly; its volume is larger (the hydraulic power unit F2 step from 26.70 L to 30.08 L, the actuated-linkage F2 step from 1.078 L to 1.310 L). Energy storage keeps the one-exponent default and offers the slow charge as a choice. Sheets issued under 1.2.0 for an emergency stroke should be re-run.

**Pressure basis (1.3.1).** A gauge number typed on the Absolute basis gives a smaller volume with no other sign (4, 5 and 10 bar read as absolute give 2.50 L, against 2.64 L as gauge), and a pre-charge of 0.5 bar absolute is a vacuum. On the Absolute basis a pressure below `p_atm` is now blocked (`basis`) with a message that names the input and says to switch `p_basis` to Gauge. Every pressure on the sheet prints its basis beside the unit: `p_0`, `p_1`, `p_2` follow `p_basis`, `p_atm` is "bar a", `PS` is "bar g", and the working and results show "bar a" or "bar g". The labels name the basis and convert nothing. F1 (`p`, `p_back`) and F3 (`p_op`) print "bar g".
