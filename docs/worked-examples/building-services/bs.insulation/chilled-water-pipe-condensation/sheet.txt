1. Scope and method
Calculates the insulation thickness of a pipe or flat surface that meets a heat-loss limit, a hot-surface limit or an anti-condensation requirement. For a chosen thickness it gives the heat flow, surface temperature and PASS or FAIL.
Pipe resistance per metre: R = ln(D_e/D_i)/(2π·λ) + 1/(h_se·π·D_e), D_e = D_i + 2t; with the inner film and pipe wall R_oth = 1/(h_i·π·D_p) + ln(D_i/D_p)/(2π·λ_w) added in series Flat resistance per square metre: R = t/λ + 1/h_se (+ 1/h_i + t_w/λ_w) Heat flow: q = (θ_i − θ_a)/R, positive from the process to the ambient Surface temperature: θ_se = θ_a + q/(h_se·π·D_e) (pipe), θ_a + q/h_se (flat) Surface coefficient: h_se as given, or h_se = h_c + h_r with h_r = ε·σ·(T_s² + T_a²)(T_s + T_a); h_c from free convection to air (laminar or turbulent, the larger), forced convection (cross-flow cylinder or flat plate) and their cube-law blend; solved with θ_se, which it depends on Heat-loss limit: the thickness beyond which |q| ≤ q_max; surface-temperature limit (hot): θ_se ≤ θ_se,max; anti-condensation (cold): θ_se ≥ θ_dew + allowance, θ_dew entered (for example from the psychrometrics calculation) or from the Magnus relation at θ_a and RH Thickness solution: each criterion's thickness by scanning t from 0 to 1 m for the last broken point and bisecting the bracket after it; governing t = max over the selected criteria Critical diameter: D_crit = 2λ/h_se; below it, insulation raises the heat flow until D_e passes D_crit

3. Assumptions
  A1. Heat flows in steady state, one-dimensionally, through a single homogeneous layer of constant conductivity. (Formula route of the standard.)
  A2. The insulation has no thermal bridges at supports, no joints and no moisture. (Formula route of the standard.)
  A3. The conductivity λ is the value entered, which should be the value at the mean insulation temperature on the sheet. (Stated.)
  A4. With the correlation, the outer surface coefficient is h_se = h_c + h_r. (Incropera, §7.2, §7.4, §9.6 and §9.9 (section numbers to be checked against the owner's copy).)
  A5. Radiation is h_r = ε·σ·(T_s² + T_a²)(T_s + T_a) to surroundings at the ambient air temperature. (Incropera, §1.2.)
  A6. Free convection to air uses Holman's simplified relations at atmospheric pressure, the larger of the laminar and turbulent forms. (Holman's constants: horizontal cylinder 1.32(ΔT/D)^0.25 and 1.24ΔT^(1/3), vertical surface 1.42(ΔT/L)^0.25 and 1.31ΔT^(1/3).)
  A7. Forced convection uses Churchill–Bernstein for a cylinder in cross-flow and the laminar or mixed flat-plate relations along a plate. (Incropera, §7.2 and §7.4.)
  A8. Air properties are fits at the film temperature, with Pr = 0.71. (Stated.)
  A9. Free and forced convection blend as (h_nat³ + h_forced³)^(1/3). (Stated.)
  A10. The correlation is indicative, not the standard's own. (The standard's own coefficients are not reproduced.)
  A11. The dew point is entered, or is the Magnus relation at the ambient temperature and humidity. (Derived; the Glaser condensation calculation shares the function.)
  A12. Below 0 °C the Magnus relation is over ice, so the humidity is then taken relative to ice. (Stated; enter the dew point for sub-zero air, because weather humidity is usually relative to water.)
  A13. An allowance above the dew point is the user's choice. (Stated.)
  A14. Pipes are treated as horizontal and flat surfaces as vertical for free convection. (Stated.)
  A15. Air conductivity is a fit within about 3 % over 250 to 400 K. (Stated.)
  A16. Air viscosity is a fit within about 4 % over 250 to 400 K. (Stated.)
  A17. The required thickness is the least thickness beyond which the criterion holds for every thicker layer up to 1 m. (Stated; this matters when thin insulation on a small pipe raises the heat flow (critical diameter).)
  A18. No thickness table of any standard, including BS 5422, is used or shipped. (No standard's table is reproduced.)
  A19. The conductivity is the user's input, from a library material or the maker's data. (No standard's table is reproduced.)
  A20. geometry (Surface) is taken as pipe (cylinder). (A round pipe.)
  A21. h_mode (Where the outer surface coefficient comes from) is taken as h_se as given. (A surface coefficient is given, not computed.)
  A22. L_surf (Height or flow length of the flat surface (correlation, flat)) is taken as 1 m. (Not used: a pipe.)
  A23. lambda (Thermal conductivity of the insulation) is taken as 0.035 W/(m·K). (Closed-cell elastomeric insulation; take the maker's value at the mean temperature (W3 material))
  A24. v_wind (Air speed over the surface (correlation; 0 = still air)) is taken as 0 m/s. (Not used: h_se is given.)
  A25. h_se (Outer surface coefficient (as given; generic default of 10 W/(m²K), replace with the value for your surface; a high value is not conservative against condensation on a cold surface, use the correlation there)) is taken as 8 W/(m²·K). (Still air in a plant room; replace with the project's own record.)
  A26. eps (Emissivity of the outer surface (for the correlation; with h_se as given it only judges that value against condensation on a cold surface)) is taken as 0.9. (Painted or plastic jacket (invented for this example); with h_se given it only judges that value against condensation.)

4. Working
  [11] T_dew = inverse Magnus of RH·p_sat(T_amb)    « BS EN ISO 13788 (Magnus form, as in the Glaser condensation calculation)
      = T_amb = 28 °C, RH = 70 %
      = 22.012 degC
        Dew point of the ambient air
  [12] t_req_q = t such that |q| ≤ 12 W/m for every thicker layer    « ISO 12241
      = scan 0 … 1000 mm, then bisection
      = 17.814 mm
        Required thickness, heat-gain limit
  [13] t_req_dew = t such that θ_se ≥ 23.012 °C for every thicker layer    « ISO 12241
      = scan 0 … 1000 mm, then bisection
      = 13.162 mm
        Required thickness, anti-condensation allowance
  [14] thickness = max over the selected criteria    « ISO 12241
      = t_req_q = 17.814 mm, t_req_dew = 13.162 mm
      = 17.814 mm
        Governing required thickness (heat-gain limit)
  [16] D_e = D_i + 2t
      = 90 mm + 2 × 25 mm
      = 140 mm
        Outer diameter of the insulation, chosen thickness
  [17] R_ins = ln(D_e/D_i)/(2π·λ)    « ISO 12241
      = ln(140/90)/(2π × 0.035)
      = 2.0091 m·K/W
        Resistance of the insulation
  [18] R_se = 1/(h_se·π·D_e)    « ISO 12241
      = 1/(8 × π × 0.14 m)
      = 0.28421 m·K/W
        Resistance of the outer surface
  [19] R_tot = R_oth + R_ins + R_se    « ISO 12241
      = 0 + 2.0091 + 0.28421
      = 2.2933 m·K/W
        Total resistance per metre
  [20] q_per_m = |θ_i − θ_a| / R    « ISO 12241
      = 22 K / 2.2933
      = 9.593 W/m
        Heat flow per metre of pipe (heat gain, magnitude)
  [21] T_se = θ_a + q/(h_se·π·D_e)    « ISO 12241
      = 28 °C − 9.593 / 3.5186
      = 25.274 degC
        Outer surface temperature
  [22] T_m = (θ_i + θ_se)/2
      = (6 + 25.274)/2
      = 15.637 degC
        Mean temperature of the insulation: take λ at this temperature
  [23] D_crit = 2λ/h_se at the bare surface    « ISO 12241
      = 2 × 0.035 / 8
      = 8.75 mm
        Critical diameter at the bare-surface h_se: below it, insulation raises the heat flow
  [24] u_q = |q| / q_max
      = |q| ≤ 12 W/m
      = 0.79941
        Utilisation, heat-gain limit
  [25] u_dew = (θ_a − θ_se)/(θ_a − (θ_dew + margin))
      = θ_se ≥ 23.012 °C
      = 0.54661
        Utilisation, anti-condensation allowance
  [26] dT_dep = θ_a − θ_se
      = 28 °C − 25.274 °C
      = 2.7264 K
        Fall of the surface below the ambient air
  [27] dT_dep_max = θ_a − (θ_dew + allowance)
      = 28 °C − 23.012 °C
      = 4.9878 K
        Largest fall of the surface below the ambient air
