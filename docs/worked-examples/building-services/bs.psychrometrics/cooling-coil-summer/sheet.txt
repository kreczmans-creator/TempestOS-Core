1. SCOPE AND METHOD
The state of moist air (humidity ratio, relative humidity, dew point, wet-bulb, enthalpy, specific volume, density) from dry-bulb and one other property at a stated pressure or altitude, and one air-handling process from that state with its heat and moisture balance: sensible heating or cooling, cooling with dehumidification, steam humidification, adiabatic humidification, or mixing with a second stream.
p_ws from Hyland–Wexler (over water ≥ 0 °C, over ice below); W = 0.621945 p_w/(p − p_w); φ = p_w/p_ws; h = 1.006 t + W(2501 + 1.86 t) kJ/kg_da; v = 0.287042 (t + 273.15)(1 + 1.607858 W)/p; dew point and wet-bulb by bisection (wet-bulb from the adiabatic-saturation relation). Processes on a dry-air mass flow: sensible Q = ṁ (h₂ − h₁) at constant W; cooling/dehumidification Q_total = ṁ(h₁ − h₂) − ṁ_w h_w with condensate ṁ_w = ṁ(W₁ − W₂); steam h₂ = h₁ + (W₂ − W₁) h_steam; adiabatic humidification at constant h; mixing by mass-weighted W and h.
  Ref: ASHRAE Handbook — Fundamentals Ch. 1 — Psychrometrics (ideal-gas relations, processes)
  Ref: Hyland & Wexler, ASHRAE Trans. 89(2A), 1983 — Saturation pressure of water and ice
  Ref: CIBSE Guide C Section 1 — Properties of humid air (principles)

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Moist air is an ideal mixture of ideal gases; the enhancement factor is taken as 1. (ASHRAE Fundamentals Ch. 1 ideal-gas relations.)
  A2. Condensate leaves at the coil leaving dry-bulb; steam adds enthalpy h_steam at negligible mass of dry air. (Process conventions.)
  A3. Adiabatic humidification follows a line of constant enthalpy (close to constant wet-bulb). (Evaporative process idealisation.)
  A4. altitude (Altitude (used when pressure is blank)) is taken as 0 m. (Sea-level site.)
  A5. known (Second known property) is taken as relative humidity. (RH given.)
  A6. process (Process) is taken as cooling with dehumidification. (Chilled-water coil.)
  A7. RH_2 (Leaving relative humidity (cooling, evaporative)) is taken as 95. (Off-coil 95 % RH (typical contact factor).)
