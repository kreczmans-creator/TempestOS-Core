1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Friction and fitting losses through series pipe segments at a given flow, the static head, the system curve, the duty point against a pump curve entered as points, the affinity-law shift for a speed or impeller change, and the NPSH available at the pump suction.
Design flow from the heat carried, when a heat load is given in place of a flow: Q = Φ/(ρ c_p ΔT). Mean velocity in the pipe: v = Q/(πD²/4). Reynolds number, which decides laminar or turbulent flow: Re = ρvD/μ. Friction factor for laminar flow (Re < 2300): f = 64/Re. Friction factor for turbulent flow, Colebrook–White by fixed-point iteration (at most 100 steps) from the Swamee–Jain value, which also cross-checks it: 1/√f = −2 log10(ε/3.7D + 2.51/(Re√f)). Head lost in the pipe and its fittings: h_f = f (L + L_eq)/D · v²/2g + K v²/2g. System curve, with the friction of a turbulent or transitional segment and every fitting loss scaled from the design flow as Q², and the friction of a laminar segment (Re < 2300, h_f ∝ Q) as Q (a closed circuit has Σdz = 0, the rise in the flow being recovered in the return): H_sys(Q) = Σdz + h_out + Σh_f,lam·(Q/Q₀) + Σh_f,other·(Q/Q₀)². Water held in the pipes: V_pipe = Σ π/4·D²·L. Duty point, where the pump and system curves cross (every crossing is found by a scan and refined by bisection; the stable one, where the pump curve falls through the system curve, nearest the design flow is reported): H_pump(Q) = H_sys(Q). Pump affinity laws for a change of speed n: Q ∝ n; H ∝ n². For a change of impeller diameter D, by the choice: a trimmed impeller of the same casing, Q ∝ D and H ∝ D² (the default, the usual practice for a trim); or a geometrically similar pump scaled in all dimensions, Q ∝ D³ and H ∝ D². Speed and diameter combine as Q ∝ n·D^a, H ∝ n²·D², a = 1 or 3. Net positive suction head available, to compare with what the pump requires: NPSH_a = p_atm/ρg + z_s − h_f,suction − p_v/ρg, with p_v from the water correlation or, for a custom fluid, from the vapour pressure entered. The friction h_f,suction is that of the segments marked 'suction' only, at the larger of the design flow and the duty flow the pump runs at. Shaft torque for the motor: T = P/ω with P the larger of the design-flow shaft power and the duty-point shaft power, at the speed n_pump; when a speed or diameter ratio shifts the duty, the shifted duty power at the shifted speed n_pump·n₂/n₁.
  Ref: Crane Technical Paper 410 — Flow of fluids through valves, fittings and pipe
  Ref: Çengel & Cimbala, Fluid Mechanics Ch. 8 — Internal flow: friction factor, minor losses, pump duty
  Ref: CIBSE Guide C Section 4 — Flow of fluids in pipes and ducts (principles)
  Ref: Kell, J. Chem. Eng. Data 20(1), 1975 — Density of liquid water

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Steady, incompressible, fully developed flow; all segments carry the full design flow in series. (Darcy–Weisbach.)
  A2. Fitting losses scale with velocity head (K method) or add pipe length (equivalent-length method); both may be used on one segment. (Crane TP-410 practice.)
  A3. The system curve scales the friction of turbulent and transitional segments and all fitting losses with Q² from the design flow (the friction factor is held at its design-flow value); the friction of a laminar segment scales with Q; the static part is independent of flow. (H_sys = H_static + k₁ Q + k₂ Q².)
  A4. Water properties are from published correlations at atmospheric pressure (0–100 °C); any other fluid is entered with its own density and viscosity and their provenance. (Kell 1975; Vogel fit; Hyland & Wexler 1983.)
  A5. n_ratio (Speed ratio n₂/n₁ (affinity)) is taken as 0.9. (Inverter turn-down to 90 % speed.)
  A6. D_ratio (Impeller diameter ratio D₂/D₁ (affinity; the law is chosen below)) is taken as 1. (Impeller unchanged.)
  A7. p_atm (Atmospheric pressure (NPSH)) is taken as 101.325 kPa. (Sea level.)
