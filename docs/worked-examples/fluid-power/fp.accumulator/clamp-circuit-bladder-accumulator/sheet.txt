1. Scope and method
Calculates the pre-charge gas volume V0 of a hydraulic accumulator that delivers or absorbs a liquid volume ΔV between two working pressures, with the next listed size and the PSSR bar·litre product. It is not a pressure-vessel code check.
Absolute pressures: p_a = p_gauge + p_atm, or as entered in Absolute mode Gas volume, one exponent both ways: V0 = ΔV / [(p0/p1)^(1/n_gas) − (p0/p2)^(1/n_gas)], n_gas = 1 isothermal to 1.4 adiabatic Gas volume, slow charge then discharge at n_gas: V0 = ΔV·(p2/p0) / [(p2/p1)^(1/n_gas) − 1], from V2 = V0·p0/p2 and V1 = V2·(p2/p1)^(1/n_gas) Temperature and gas corrections: V0 × f_rg × the cold-gas factor T_max/T_min, to the power 1/n_gas when one exponent holds both ways, in absolute temperatures Pre-charge rules: p0 ≤ r_0·p1 at T_max, and p2/p0(T_min) ≤ r_p with p0(T_min) = p0·T_min/T_max in absolute temperatures Pre-charge at the charging temperature: p0(T_c) = p0(T_max)·T_c/T_max in absolute temperatures Next size: the smallest listed size not below V0 PSSR bar-litre product: PS·V of the vessel against 250 bar·L, an indication that needs PS and a volume

3. Assumptions
  A1. All gas-law pressures are absolute. (p·Vⁿ = const on a fixed mass of gas.)
  A2. A gauge entry is converted with the atmospheric pressure entered on the sheet. (p·Vⁿ = const on a fixed mass of gas.)
  A3. The exponent n_gas is yours, from 1 for a slow isothermal discharge to 1.4 for a fast adiabatic one. (Gas-law exponents, closed form.)
  A4. With one exponent both ways, a larger n_gas gives a larger volume for a given ΔV. (Gas-law exponents, closed form.)
  A5. A held accumulator is charged slowly and isothermally to p_2, then discharged at n_gas. (Gas-law sizing of a held accumulator: slow charge, fast discharge.)
  A6. An emergency stroke is a held accumulator by default. (Gas-law sizing of a held accumulator: slow charge, fast discharge.)
  A7. Sizing a held accumulator with one exponent understates the volume. (Gas-law sizing of a held accumulator: slow charge, fast discharge.)
  A8. The one-exponent form suits a gas charged as fast as it is discharged. (Gas-law sizing of a held accumulator: slow charge, fast discharge.)
  A9. The shock and pulsation duty always uses one exponent. (Gas-law sizing of a held accumulator: slow charge, fast discharge.)
  A10. The gas is ideal unless a real-gas factor is entered. (Real-gas correction as a user factor.)
  A11. The real-gas factor is at least 1, because real nitrogen needs more volume than ideal gas, never less. (Real-gas correction as a user factor.)
  A12. Above 200 bar the sheet warns that nitrogen departs from the ideal-gas law. (Real-gas correction as a user factor.)
  A13. The pre-charge p0 is defined at the maximum operating temperature T_max, and V0 is sized there. (Manufacturer sizing practice and the gas law at constant mass.)
  A14. At T_min the same gas delivers only the fraction T_min/T_max of ΔV in absolute temperatures. (Manufacturer sizing practice and the gas law at constant mass.)
  A15. A held accumulator multiplies V0 by T_max/T_min. (Manufacturer sizing practice and the gas law at constant mass.)
  A16. One exponent n_gas both ways multiplies V0 by T_max/T_min to the power 1/n_gas. (Manufacturer sizing practice and the gas law at constant mass.)
  A17. The p2/p0 rule is checked with the pre-charge at T_min. (Manufacturer sizing practice and the gas law at constant mass.)
  A18. With T_min equal to T_max the temperature correction is 1. (Manufacturer sizing practice and the gas law at constant mass.)
  A19. The pre-charge rules are records for the accumulator type and make. (Rules are records, not built-in limits.)
  A20. The rules are checked on absolute pressures, whichever basis the pressures are entered on. (Rules are records, not built-in limits.)
  A21. The manufacturer gives the rule values and says whether its ratio is on gauge or absolute pressures. (Rules are records, not built-in limits.)
  A22. The bar·litre product is PS times the vessel volume V. (Regulatory gating: no pressure-vessel code check.)
  A23. PS is the vessel's maximum allowable pressure in bar gauge, never below the highest working pressure p_2. (Regulatory gating: no pressure-vessel code check.)
  A24. The vessel volume is the selected size unless you enter it. (Regulatory gating: no pressure-vessel code check.)
  A25. Without PS and a volume the PSSR indication is not available. (Regulatory gating: no pressure-vessel code check.)
  A26. The indication shows whether the 250 bar·L exception can apply, and it is not a compliance statement. (Regulatory gating: no pressure-vessel code check.)
  A27. The PED, UK PER, PSSR examination and the vessel's design code stay with the manufacturer and the competent person. (Regulatory gating: no pressure-vessel code check.)
  A28. mode (Duty) is taken as Energy storage. (Slow clamp-and-hold cycle: stored-energy duty.)
  A29. p_basis (Pressures entered as) is taken as Absolute. (Pressures entered as absolute bar, as the source numbers are quoted.)
  A30. p_atm (Atmospheric pressure (gauge to absolute)) is taken as 1.01325 bar a. (Standard atmosphere, derived constant.)
  A31. T_min (Minimum operating temperature (the gas is coldest and delivers least here)) is taken as 30 °C. (Oil temperature range assumed 30 to 50 °C in service (invented for this example); the gas is coldest at 30 °C.)
  A32. T_c (Charging temperature) is taken as 20 °C. (Charged in the workshop at 20 °C.)
  A33. n_gas (Gas exponent of the discharge, 1 isothermal (slow) to 1.4 adiabatic (fast), also the charge exponent when one exponent holds both ways) is taken as 1. (Isothermal: the clamp holds for minutes between cycles.)
  A34. f_rg (Real-gas factor on V0, the real over the ideal gas volume for this pressure window (1 = ideal gas, at least 1)) is taken as 1. (Ideal gas; the highest pressure is just above 200 bar, so the sheet warns.)
  A35. Energy storage duty: ΔV is the liquid volume delivered between p_2 and p_1 over a cycle, with n_gas from 1 for a slow discharge to 1.4 for a fast one. (Gas-law sizing for a stored-energy duty.)

  [21] V0_ideal = dV / [(pa_0/pa_1)^(1/n_gas) − (pa_0/pa_2)^(1/n_gas)]    « p·Vⁿ = const
      = 14 L / [(0.9) − (0.42857)]
      = 29.697 L
        Gas volume for the displacement, ideal gas
  [22] f_Tabs = (T_max / 1 K) / (T_min / 1 K)
      = 50 degC / 1 K / (30 degC / 1 K)
      = 1.066
        Absolute temperature ratio T_max/T_min
  [23] f_T = f_Tabs^(1 / n_gas)
      = 1.066^(1 / 1)
      = 1.066
        Temperature scaling of the gas volume: the temperature ratio to the power 1/n (one exponent both ways)
  [24] V0 = V0_ideal * f_rg * f_T
      = 29.697 L × 1 × 1.066
      = 31.656 L
        Pre-charge gas volume V0 (accumulator size required)
  [25] T_ratio = (T_c / 1 K) / (T_max / 1 K)
      = 20 degC / 1 K / (50 degC / 1 K)
      = 0.90716
        Charging over maximum absolute temperature

      = 90 bar a × 0.90716
      = 81.645 bar a
        Absolute pre-charge pressure at the charging temperature
  [27] p0_min = pa_0 / f_Tabs
      = 90 bar a / 1.066
      = 84.43 bar a

  [28] p0_set = p0

7. Applicability
  WARNING  records: tabulated values not taken from a reference record are r_0, r_p (Pre-charge rules); sizes (Accumulator size list). Enter each as one so its source, edition and licence travel with it.  [record]
  WARNING  p_2: the highest working pressure is 210 bar absolute, above 200 bar, where nitrogen departs from the ideal gas. The volume shown is too small, so enter f_rg (up to 3) from a gas-property source.  [realgas]

    process: not entered, so the default of 'By duty' is used.
