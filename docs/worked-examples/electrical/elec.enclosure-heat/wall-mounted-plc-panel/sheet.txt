1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Total heat load inside a control enclosure, the effective surface that can dissipate it for the mounting condition, the internal temperature rise with natural convection and radiation through a stated overall heat-transfer coefficient, the forced-ventilation airflow for a target rise (density corrected for altitude), the fan duty point against a fan curve and the system resistance, optional solar gain, and the cooling-unit capacity needed when the limit cannot be met.
Heat dissipated inside the enclosure, plus any solar gain: P = Σ P_i n_i d_i (+ α I A_sun). Effective cooling surface, each face weighted by how freely it sheds heat: A_eff = Σ f_face × A_face. Temperature rise with natural cooling, one coefficient over the exposed faces (the IEC TR 60890 tables are not used): ΔT = P/(h A_eff). Air flow a fan must move to hold the target rise: V = P/(ρ c_p ΔT_target), with ρ = p(z)/(287.055 T) and c_p = 1006 J/(kg·K). Fan duty point, where the fan curve meets the system resistance (found by bisection): p_fan(V) = p_sys (V/V_ref)². Cooling unit capacity, when the surfaces alone cannot reject the heat: P_cool = P − h A_eff (T_max − T_amb). Sealed enclosure (sealing temperature T_seal entered, no breather), pressure difference across the walls when the air warms or cools from the sealing temperature at constant volume, in absolute temperatures (a rise when T_int > T_seal, a suction when below): Δp = p_site·|T_int/T_seal − 1|. A pressure-compensation element (breather or relief) limits it to its cracking pressure: dp_seal = min(Δp, dp_relief). Force of that pressure on a cover of area A_cover: F_seal = dp_seal·A_cover.
  Ref: IEC TR 60890 — A method of temperature-rise verification of low-voltage switchgear and controlgear assemblies by calculation
  Ref: Manufacturer enclosure climate-control guides (Rittal, nVent Hoffman) — Effective surface, heat-transfer coefficient and fan-sizing practice (cited, not copied)
  Ref: BS EN 61439-1 10.10 — Verification of temperature rise

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Heat leaves the enclosure through its exposed faces at one overall coefficient h (natural convection plus radiation to surroundings at the ambient temperature); covered faces dissipate nothing. (IEC TR 60890-style effective-surface approach; h is a reference value the user may edit.)
  A2. Forced ventilation removes the full heat load with air entering at ambient and leaving at the target rise; natural dissipation is neglected for the airflow (conservative). (Manufacturer fan-sizing practice.)
  A3. A sealed enclosure (T_seal entered) is a closed, rigid, leak-free volume of dry air at the site pressure when closed: the pressure difference follows from the ideal-gas relation at constant volume, from the sealing temperature to the internal temperature with natural dissipation T_int (the temperature without a cooling unit); a breather, a seal that leaks or a flexing wall reduces it, so it is an upper bound (a pressure-compensation element is modelled only as a cap at its cracking pressure, with no flow resistance); the site pressure is the standard atmosphere at the altitude. (Ideal gas at constant volume (derived).)
  A4. The fan curve is linear between the entered points; the system resistance scales with airflow squared. (Duty-point idealisation.)
  A5. altitude (Site altitude (air density)) is taken as 0 m. (Wall mounted on a floor plinth: rear and bottom covered; indoors.)
