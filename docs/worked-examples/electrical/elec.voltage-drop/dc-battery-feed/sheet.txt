1. Scope and method
Calculates the voltage drop, the conductor power loss and the estimated conductor temperature of a DC, single-phase or three-phase circuit. The drop limit is a user input, because the limits of BS 7671 are not embedded.
Conductor resistance at its operating temperature, for n conductors in parallel: R = ρ_T L/(A n), with ρ_T = ρ_20 [1 + α (T_c − 20)]. Resistance and reactance from per-length figures instead: R = R′ L/n; X = X′ L/n. Voltage drop on a DC circuit: ΔV = 2 I R. Voltage drop on a single-phase AC circuit: ΔV = 2 I (R cos φ + X sin φ). Voltage drop on a three-phase AC circuit: ΔV = √3 I (R cos φ + X sin φ). Voltage drop from a tabulated mV/A/m figure, which already carries the 2 or √3: ΔV = (mV/A/m) I L / 1000. Power lost in the cable: P_loss = 2 I² R (DC and single-phase); 3 I² R (three-phase). Estimated conductor temperature at this current: T_c,est = T_amb + (T_rated − T_amb)(I/I_z)².

3. Assumptions
  A1. The load is balanced across the phases. (Standard engineering approximation.)
  A2. The conductor reactance is the entered X′ per kilometre times the length. (Standard engineering approximation.)
  A3. The voltage drop is the arithmetic sum R cos φ + X sin φ, not the vector difference. (Standard engineering approximation.)
  A4. A mV/A/m figure is used as tabulated, with its own temperature and system convention. (Cable table convention.)
  A5. The estimated conductor temperature scales the rated temperature rise with (I/I_z)². (BS 7671:2018 Appendix 4 approach, formula only.)
  A6. I_z is the tabulated capacity for the installation method, with its correction factors applied. (BS 7671:2018 Appendix 4 approach, formula only.)
  A7. When the estimate is above T_c, the resistance and the drop are computed at the estimate. (Keeps the drop on the safe side.)
  A8. system (System) is taken as DC. (Design convention.)
  A9. data (Conductor data from) is taken as resistivity and area. (Design convention.)
  A10. n_par (Parallel conductors per phase) is taken as 1. (Design convention.)
  A11. X_km (Reactance per km per conductor) is taken as 0 Ω/km. (Design convention.)
  A12. V_n (Nominal voltage (line-to-line for three-phase)) is taken as 48 V. (Design convention.)
  A13. T_c (Conductor operating temperature for the resistance) is taken as 70 °C. (Design convention.)

4. Working
  [9] sin_phi = 0
      = 0
        sin φ
  [15] rho_T = rho_20 * (1 + alpha * (T_c - 20 degC) / (1 K))
      = 0.01724 ohm·mm²/m × (1 + 0.00393 × (70 degC − 20 degC) / 1 K)
      = 0.020628 ohm·mm²/m
        Resistivity at the operating temperature
  [16] R = rho_T * L / (A_c * n_par)
      = 0.020628 ohm·mm²/m × 30 m / (6 mm² × 1)
      = 0.10314 ohm
        Conductor resistance (one conductor, parallel paths combined)
  [17] X = X_km * L / n_par
      = 0 ohm/km × 30 m / 1
      = 0 ohm
        Conductor reactance
  [18] dV = 2 * I * R
      = 2 × 20 A × 0.10314 ohm
      = 4.1255 V
        Voltage drop
  [19] dV_pct = 100 * dV / V_n
      = 100 × 4.1255 V / 48 V
      = 8.5949
        Voltage drop, per cent
  [20] P_loss = 2 * I^2 * R
      = 2 × (20 A)^2 × 0.10314 ohm
      = 0.082511 kW
        Conductor power loss
  [21] u_dv = dV_pct / dv_limit
      = 8.5949 / 5
      = 1.719
        Drop utilisation against the limit
  [22] dV_max = dv_limit / 100 * V_n
      = 5 / 100 × 48 V
      = 2.4 V
        Largest voltage drop the limit allows
