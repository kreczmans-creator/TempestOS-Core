1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Automatic disconnection of supply in a TN system for one circuit: the earth fault loop impedance at the conductor's operating temperature against the device's maximum, the disconnection time, the protective conductor (and line conductor) against the adiabatic minimum, and the prospective three-phase fault at the origin against the device's breaking capacity.
Loop impedance: Z_s = Z_e + (R_1 + R_2)·[1 + α(θ − 20)], R = k_R·ρ_20·L/S (k_R = 1.125 takes the pure-copper ρ_20 to the cable standard's maximum conductor resistance; k enters with the pure ρ_20) Fault current: I_f = C_min·U_0/Z_s, C_min = 0.95 Maximum loop impedance: Z_s,max = C_min·U_0/I_a; I_a = 5, 10 or 20·I_n for MCB types B, C, D, or read from the time–current points at t_req (log-log) Adiabatic conductor check: S_min = √(I²t)/k, k = √[Q_c(β + 20)/ρ_20 · ln(1 + (θ_f − θ_i)/(β + θ_i))] (derived); I²t = I_f²·t_d from 0.1 s; below 0.1 s the device's let-through I²t at the maximum prospective fault, which bounds the protective and the line conductor at the origin where the earth-fault current is largest (reg. 434.5.2, 543.1.3); without it neither is checked at the origin, the sheet fails 'Conductors against let-through at the origin' and the protective conductor check uses I_f²·0.1 s at the end-of-line fault only Protective device check: the device chosen here must be the one carried from the cable step (device_code 1 = MCB B, 2 = C, 3 = D, 4 = fuse or MCCB), else the sheet is blocked Fault level (simplified): Z_T = u_k·U_n²/S_r, I_k3 = c·U_n/(√3·Z_T), U_n = √3·U_0
  Ref: BS 7671:2018 (as amended) reg. 411.3.2.2, 411.3.2.3, 411.4.4 — Automatic disconnection in TN systems: times and the loop impedance condition
  Ref: BS 7671:2018 (as amended) reg. 543.1.3, 434.5.2 — Protective conductor and fault current adiabatic checks
  Ref: BS EN 60204-1:2018 cl. 6.3.3 — Protection by automatic disconnection of supply
  Ref: IEC 60909-0:2016 cl. 5.3 — Voltage factor c

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. TN system, a single earth fault of negligible impedance; no motor contribution, arc fault or IT system. (Scope of the check.)
  A2. Line and protective conductors are the same material at the same temperature; R = ρ₂₀·L/S corrected by α. (Conductor data from a material record.)
  A3. k is derived from the material constants and labelled derived; it is not read from a licensed table. (Derived constants, not read from a table.)
  A4. Fuse and MCCB characteristics are points the user reads off the manufacturer's curve (log-log interpolation; no extrapolation). (No time–current table ships.)
  A5. Optional field-test value 0.8 × Z_s,max is a rule of thumb for measurements at ambient temperature. (Labelled on the sheet.)
  A6. The line conductor check (reg. 434.5.2) needs the device's let-through energy at the maximum prospective fault; the end-of-line earth fault is the minimum fault and does not bound it, so the check is made only when I2t_let is entered or the disconnection time is 0.1 s or more. (Stated limitation: the end-of-line earth fault is the minimum fault, not the maximum.)
  A7. C_min (Voltage factor C_min) is taken as 0.95. (Minimum voltage factor.)
  A8. c_max (Voltage factor c for the maximum fault) is taken as 1.1. (Maximum voltage factor.)
  A9. theta (Conductor temperature for Z_s) is taken as 70 degC. (Thermoplastic insulation at full load.)
  A10. rho_20 (Resistivity at 20 °C (used for k, and for R₁ + R₂ through k_R)) is taken as 0.000017241 ohm*mm. (Annealed copper resistivity.)
  A11. k_R (Conductor resistance factor on ρ₂₀ for R₁ + R₂ (1 = the resistivity itself; the cable standard's maximum conductor resistances are 5 to 12 % above pure copper, so the default is 1.125 and Z_s is not understated; k is not changed by it)) is taken as 1.125. (Cable standard maximum conductor resistance over the pure-copper resistivity (5 to 12 % higher); 1.125 covers the sizes used.)
  A12. alpha (Temperature coefficient of resistance) is taken as 0.00393 1/K. (Copper temperature coefficient.)
  A13. Q_c (Volumetric heat capacity) is taken as 0.00345 J/(K*mm^3). (Copper volumetric heat capacity.)
  A14. beta (Reciprocal temperature coefficient β) is taken as 234.5 K. (Copper reciprocal temperature coefficient.)
  A15. theta_i (Initial temperature for the adiabatic check) is taken as 70 degC. (Thermoplastic operating temperature.)
  A16. theta_f (Final temperature for the adiabatic check) is taken as 160 degC. (Thermoplastic limiting temperature.)
  A17. I2t_let (Device let-through energy I²t at the maximum prospective fault (disconnection below 0.1 s)) is taken as 1500 A^2*s. (Read from the MCB let-through chart at the fault current.)

4. WORKING
  [19] R_12 = rho_20 * k_R * L * (1 / S_line + 1 / S_cpc) * (1 + alpha * (theta - 20 degC))
      = 0.000017241 ohm·mm × 1.125 × 40 m × (1 / 4 mm² + 1 / 2.5 mm²) × (1 + 0.00393 1/K × (70 degC − 20 degC))
      = 0.60339 ohm
        R₁ + R₂ at the conductor temperature
  [20] Z_s = Z_e + R_12    « BS 7671 reg. 411.4.4
      = 0.8 ohm + 0.60339 ohm
      = 1.4034 ohm
        Earth fault loop impedance
  [21] I_f = C_min * U_0 / Z_s
      = 0.95 × 230 V / 1.4034 ohm
      = 155.69 A
        Earth fault current
  [22] I_a = 10 * I_n    « BS EN 60898-1
      = 10 × 16 A
      = 160 A
        Instantaneous tripping current (10 I_n)
  [23] Z_s_max = C_min * U_0 / I_a    « BS 7671 reg. 411.4.4
      = 0.95 × 230 V / 160 A
      = 1.3656 ohm
        Maximum earth fault loop impedance
  [24] u_Zs = Z_s / Z_s_max
      = 1.4034 ohm / 1.3656 ohm
      = 1.0277
        Loop impedance utilisation
  [25] Z_test = 0.8 * Z_s_max
      = 0.8 × 1.3656 ohm
      = 1.0925 ohm
        Field-test maximum at ambient (0.8 × Z_s,max, rule of thumb)

5. RESULTS
  R_12 = 0.60339 ohm    R₁ + R₂
  Z_s = 1.4034 ohm    Loop impedance
  I_f = 155.69 A    Earth fault current
  I_a = 160 A    Disconnection current
  Z_s_max = 1.3656 ohm    Maximum loop impedance
  u_Zs = 1.0277    Loop impedance utilisation
  Z_test = 1.0925 ohm    Field-test maximum (rule of thumb)

6. CHECKS (governing first)
