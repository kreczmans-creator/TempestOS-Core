1. SCOPE AND METHOD
The fill of a conduit, trunking or tray by the cables in it against the space factor you enter, the bend radius of the route against the multiple of the largest cable diameter you enter, and the pull of the cables through a route of straight lengths and bends in both directions: the tension along the route, the sidewall pressure at each bend, and the pull direction that passes every check (the lower maximum tension when both do or neither does). Space factor, bend multiple, friction coefficient and pulling limits are your own records; no cable or conduit factor method is offered.
Cable area: A_cab = Σ n_i·π·d_i²/4 (n_i cables of overall diameter d_i) Containment area: A_int = π·D²/4 (conduit, internal diameter D) or W·H (trunking or tray, internal width × depth) Fill: fill = A_cab/A_int; utilisation u_fill = fill/f_max, f_max the space factor from your record Bend radius: R_min = n_bend·d_max, d_max the largest cable diameter, n_bend from your record; each bend u_b = R_min/R Weight: w = Σ n_i·m_i (kg/m); force per metre w·g·w_c, g = 9.80665 m/s², w_c your weight-correction factor (1 = full weight) Straight length: T₂ = T₁ + w·g·w_c·μ·L Bend: T₂ = T₁·e^(w_c·μ·θ) (capstan equation with the friction scaled by the weight-correction factor, θ in radians) Sidewall pressure at a bend: SWP = T₂/R (N/m) Direction: the route is run in the listed order and in reverse; the direction that passes every tension and sidewall pressure check is recommended, and when both or neither pass, the one of lower maximum tension (equal tension: lower SWP) Cable limit: load shared equally, so each cable carries T/N (N cables); T_cab = k_n·N·min(T_max,i), k_n your derating for more than 3 cables (1 if none); u_T = max_i (T/N)/(k_n·T_max,i) = T/T_cab Grip limit: u_grip = T/T_grip, T_grip the limit of the grip or pulling eye (your record); sidewall pressure u_P = SWP_max/P_lim
  Ref: BS 7671:2018 (as amended) reg. 522.8 — Other mechanical stresses on wiring systems (damage during installation, bending radius)
  Ref: BS EN 61386-1 general — Conduit systems for cable management: general requirements
  Ref: Beer and Johnston, Vector Mechanics for Engineers: Statics Ch. 8 — Friction: belt friction (the capstan equation)

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Cables are circular and counted by overall diameter; there is no packing or cable and conduit factor method (the jam-ratio warning is the only jamming check). The space factor is yours, from your own record. (Fill by geometry; the factor is a user record.)
  A2. Every cable is pulled together as one bundle on one pulling head with the load shared equally, so each cable carries T/N and the weakest cable governs: the cable limit is N times the lowest maximum pulling tension, optionally derated by your factor for more than 3 cables. The grip or pulling-eye limit is a separate limit you enter. (Equal sharing; unequal sharing is not modelled.)
  A3. Straight lengths are horizontal and the cable weight bears on the containment over the full length; bends are taken in the plane of the route. Inclines, vertical runs and pulley or roller reductions are not modelled; fold them into μ. A cradled or triangular weight correction is entered as w_c, which scales the weight on the straight lengths and the friction in the bends (the contact force of a cradled or triangular arrangement is w_c times that of a flat one), so T₂ = T₁ + w·g·w_c·μ·L and T₂ = T₁·e^(w_c·μ·θ). (Equivalent friction w_c·μ in the capstan equation; blank w_c means 1.)
  A4. The coefficient of friction is one constant for the whole route; static and running friction are not distinguished. (A user record with its source.)
  A5. Sidewall pressure is the tension leaving the bend over the bend radius, for the bundle as a whole. Enter the cable maker's per-cable limit unchanged: the bundle T/R against it is conservative for a triangular arrangement and roughly equal for a cradled one. Do not multiply the limit by the number of cables. (SWP = T₂/R.)
  A6. The bend radius uses the individual cable diameter; for a triplexed or bundled assembly, enter the assembly diameter from the cable maker as the overall diameter. (Cable data sheet.)
  A7. The tension at the start of the pull is the same in both directions; a bend at the very start of a pull with no start tension adds nothing, so enter the pulling rope or drum tension when it matters. (Capstan equation.)
