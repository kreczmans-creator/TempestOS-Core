1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Bending of a thin, flat, isotropic plate under a lateral load by Kirchhoff small-deflection theory: circular plates (uniform pressure, central patch, ring load; clamped or simply supported) in closed form; rectangular plates simply supported (uniform pressure or central patch, Navier series) or clamped (Ritz, approximate). Maximum deflection, greatest von Mises stress at the surface, stress and deflection utilisations and the fundamental frequency.
Flexural rigidity: D = E·t³/(12(1 − ν²)); surface stress σ = 6M/t²; von Mises σ_vm = √(σ_x² − σ_x·σ_y + σ_y² + 3τ_xy²) Circular, uniform q, clamped: w = q·a⁴/(64D); σ_edge = 3q·a²/(4t²); simply supported: w = (5 + ν)·q·a⁴/(64(1 + ν)D); σ_centre = 3(3 + ν)·q·a²/(8t²) Circular, central patch (radius c, pressure q) or ring (radius b, force F): w = A + B·r² (+ q·r⁴/64D) inside, A′ + B′·r² + C′·ln(r/a) + k·r²·ln(r/a) outside, k = q·c²/8D or F/8πD; constants from continuity of w, w′, M_r at the load radius and the edge conditions; M_r = −D(w″ + ν·w′/r), M_θ = −D(w′/r + ν·w″) Rectangular, simply supported: w = Σ W_mn·sin(mπx/a)·sin(nπy/b) over odd m, n; W_mn = q_mn/(π⁴D(m²/a² + n²/b²)²); q_mn = 16q/(π²mn) (uniform) or 16q/(π²mn)·sin(mπ/2)·sin(nπ/2)·sin(mπu/2a)·sin(nπv/2b) (patch u × v); M_x = Dπ²ΣW_mn(m²/a² + ν·n²/b²)·sin·sin, M_y likewise, M_xy = −D(1 − ν)π²ΣW_mn(mn/ab)·cos·cos; terms added until w, the centre moments and the corner M_xy each change by < 10⁻⁶ over a doubling (cap 1024 × 1024) Rectangular, clamped: Ritz, w = Σ c_ij·X_i(x/a)·Y_j(y/b), X_k(ξ) = ξ²(1 − ξ)²·P₂ₖ(2ξ − 1); c from the minimum of the total potential energy; order K raised from 4 to 20 until w and the stress change by < 10⁻⁵ Fundamental frequency: simply supported rectangle ω₁₁ = π²(1/a² + 1/b²)·√(D/(ρt)), f = ω/2π; circular ω = (λ²/a²)·√(D/(ρt)), λ² from the Bessel characteristic equation; clamped rectangle by Rayleigh–Ritz with the same basis Checks: σ_vm ≤ f_y/γ_M0; w_max ≤ span/ratio (circular span = diameter, rectangle span = shorter side); WARN w/t > 0.5
  Ref: Timoshenko & Woinowsky-Krieger, Theory of Plates and Shells Ch. 3 — Symmetrical bending of circular plates
  Ref: Timoshenko & Woinowsky-Krieger, Theory of Plates and Shells Ch. 5 — Simply supported rectangular plates (Navier solution)
  Ref: Timoshenko & Woinowsky-Krieger, Theory of Plates and Shells Ch. 6 — Rectangular plates with various edge conditions
  Ref: Leissa, Vibration of Plates (NASA SP-160) Ch. 2, 4 — Natural frequencies of circular and rectangular plates
  Ref: BS EN 1993-1-1:2005+A1:2014 6.1(1) — Partial factor γ_M0

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Thin plate, small-deflection (Kirchhoff) theory: flat, isotropic, linear elastic, constant thickness; the middle surface is unstrained; shear deformation is neglected (span/t of 10 or more). (Scope of S9; a warning below span/t = 10.)
  A2. The deflection stays small against the thickness: w/t above 0.5 means membrane action stiffens the plate and the small-deflection result is not valid (it overstates the deflection and the bending stress and ignores the membrane stress). (The sheet warns at w/t > 0.5.)
  A3. The edge support is the same all round and ideal: a simply supported edge is held at zero deflection and free to rotate; a clamped edge allows no deflection or rotation. A simply supported rectangle has its corners held down (the corner reaction 2·M_xy is reported); if the corners can lift, the deflection and the central moments are larger than computed. (Navier solution; no elastic edges, no rotational springs.)
  A4. The von Mises stress is that of the plane-stress field at the plate surface from the bending and twisting moments (σ = 6M/t²); the through-thickness shear and the local stress under a concentrated load are not included. The stress governing a simply supported rectangle is at the centre or at a corner (twisting); a clamped rectangle is scanned on a 21 × 21 grid over a quarter of the plate. (Surface stresses; no local effects.)
  A5. The rectangular patch and the circular patch are centred; a ring load is concentric. A load smaller than the thickness is a concentrated load whose local stress thin-plate theory does not give. (Warning `patch-thin`; patches below 1 % (circular), 2 % (simply supported rectangle) or 10 % (clamped rectangle) of the plate size are refused.)
  A6. The clamped rectangle is a Ritz approximation: the deflection converges quickly, the moments and stress more slowly (about 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻³ relative at the highest order for aspect ratios up to 5). Compare a critical result with finite elements. (Approximate; the sheet states the order used and the last change.)
  A7. Material properties are inputs taken from the W3 library (E, ν, ρ, f_y), each citing the material record, its revision and the page or table; the generic textbook seeds are indicative. (Record kind material.properties.)
  A8. support (Edge support, all round) is taken as simply supported. (The lid sits on a gasket land and is bolted at close pitch: treated as simply supported, which is the safe side for deflection and the central stress.)
  A9. load_basis (Uniform and patch loads given as) is taken as pressure q. (Load given as a pressure.)
  A10. E (Modulus of elasticity) is taken as 70 GPa. (Indicative value for a 5000-series aluminium alloy; replace with the W3 material record for the alloy on the drawing.)
  A11. nu (Poisson's ratio) is taken as 0.33. (Indicative value for aluminium alloys (W3 generic seed); replace with the material record.)
  A12. rho (Density (for the frequency)) is taken as 2700 kg/m^3. (Indicative value for aluminium alloys (W3 generic seed).)
  A13. f_y (Yield strength) is taken as 150 MPa. (Indicative 0.2 % proof strength for the sheet temper; replace with the material record from the mill certificate.)
