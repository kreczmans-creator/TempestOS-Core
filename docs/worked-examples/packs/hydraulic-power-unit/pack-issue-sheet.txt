1. SCOPE AND METHOD
A double-acting hydraulic cylinder in its circuit: the bore, rod and annulus areas; the force and speed on the extend and the retract stroke with mechanical and volumetric efficiency and an optional back pressure, or with the regenerative extend connection; the swept volume and the cycle time; the pump delivery, shaft power, torque and speed; a hydraulic motor's torque; the velocity in the suction, pressure and return lines against your own limits and the smallest listed hose or pipe bore that meets them; a sharp orifice's flow for flow-control screening; and a screening check of the rod against buckling (Euler above the transition slenderness, Johnson parabola below it). screening only; ISO/TS 13725 method not implemented. Not a cylinder design to a standard: the limits, the hose sizes, the mounting factor and the material properties are your records.
Piston and rod areas: A_b = π·D²/4, A_r = π·d_r²/4, A_a = A_b − A_r, area ratio φ = A_b/A_a Cylinder force: F_ext = (p·A_b − p_back·A_a)·η_m, F_ret = (p·A_a − p_back·A_b)·η_m; regenerative extend F_ext = p·A_r·η_m Speed and time: v_ext = Q·η_vc/A_b (regenerative: A_r), v_ret = Q·η_vc/A_a; t = stroke/v; t_cycle = t_ext + t_ret + t_dwell Required speed (optional): when v_req is given it gives the flow, Q = v_req·A_b/η_vc (regenerative: A_r), and replaces the entered Q; the flow must lie within 0.1 to 2000 L/min Swept volume: ΔV_ext = A_b·stroke (regenerative: A_r·stroke), ΔV_ret = A_a·stroke; ΔV = the larger Pump flow and power: Q_p = V_g·n·η_vp; P = p·Q_p/η_tp; T = P/ω; displacement needed V_g,req = Q/(n·η_vp) Hydraulic motor: T = Δp·V_g/(2π)·η_hm (V_g per revolution) Line velocities: v = Q_line/A_line; suction Q_suc = max(Q, V_g·n) (the geometric delivery), pressure/cap-port line Q_prs = Q_line·A_b/A_a on the retract stroke (regenerative extend: Q_line·A_b/A_r if larger), return Q_rtn = Q_line·A_b/A_a, with Q_line = max(Q, Q_p); smallest bore d = √(4Q/(π·v_max)), recommended = the smallest listed size not below it Orifice flow: Q = C_d·A_o·√(2Δp/ρ); bore for the circuit flow d_o = √(4Q/(π·C_d·√(2Δp/ρ))) Rod buckling (screening): L_eff = K·(stroke + L_add), r = d_r/4, λ = L_eff/r, λ_c = π·√(2E/f_y); Euler F_cr = π²·E·I/L_eff² for λ > λ_c, Johnson F_cr = A_r·f_y·[1 − f_y·λ²/(4π²E)] for λ ≤ λ_c; load F_b = max(F_push, F_stall) with F_stall = p·A_b (the stalled cylinder at the supply pressure: no flow, so no back pressure and no friction loss; in the regenerative extend too, since the standard connection can always be selected); utilisation u = F_b·S_f/F_cr
  Ref: ISO/TS 13725 — Cylinder buckling load: method not implemented, screening only
  Ref: Esposito, Fluid Power with Applications Cylinder and pump chapters — Force, speed, flow and power relations, closed form

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. The supply pressure p is the gauge pressure at the cylinder port and, for the pump, at the pump outlet; line losses are not subtracted (the line losses of a pipe run come from the pipe calculation). The force is the pressure on the area less the back pressure on the other side, times the mechanical efficiency. (F = p·A·η_m, closed form.)
  A2. The speed is the flow into the cylinder times the volumetric efficiency over the area, in steady motion: no acceleration, cushioning or compressibility. The cycle time is the two strokes plus the dwell. (v = Q·η_vc/A.)
  A3. The pump power is the supply pressure times the pump's delivery over the total efficiency: when the pump delivers more than the circuit takes, the excess is taken to go over the relief valve at the supply pressure, which is the conservative case for the drive. The shaft torque is the power over the angular speed. (P = p·Q_p/η_tp.)
  A4. The line flows use the pump delivery when it is larger than the circuit flow: the suction line the geometric delivery V_g·n, the pressure line and the return line Q_line = max(Q, Q_p), which carries no volumetric efficiency while the speeds use Q·η_vc (the flows are the conservative side of the speeds). The line to the cylinder cap port is judged at the larger flow of the two strokes: on the retract stroke it carries the cap-side discharge Q_line·A_b/A_a (the same flow as the return line), and in the regenerative extend Q_line·A_b/A_r if that is larger. The velocity limits are your records; the sheet's defaults are placeholders, not values from a standard. (Continuity.)
  A5. The orifice relation is the sharp-edged turbulent one, Q = C_d·A·√(2Δp/ρ), with your discharge coefficient; it ignores viscosity and cavitation and is a screening of a flow-control orifice, not a valve selection. (Orifice equation, closed form.)
  A6. The rod is treated as a solid round column of its own diameter with the effective-length factor of the mounting; the tube, the piston and the rod-end stiffness are not modelled. The rod is judged at the larger of the push load you enter and the stall thrust p·A_b (a stalled, obstructed or relief-limited cylinder loads the rod with the full pressure force on the bore: no flow, so no back pressure, and the seal friction does not relieve it); no pressure-limiting device is credited, and the regenerative extend is judged at the same stall thrust because the standard connection can always be selected. The mounting factor K_m defaults to 1 (pinned at both ends): a fixed-free mounting is K_m = 2, and the factor and the safety factor S_f are your records. screening only; ISO/TS 13725 method not implemented. (Euler and Johnson column relations, closed form.)
  A7. p_back (Back pressure on the discharge side (standard connection, gauge)) is taken as 0 bar g. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. All gas-law pressures are absolute. A gauge entry is converted with the atmospheric pressure entered here, so the same numbers read as gauge or as absolute give different volumes. (p·Vⁿ = const on a fixed mass of gas.)
  A2. The exponent n is the user's: 1 for a slow discharge with heat exchange (isothermal), towards 1.4 for a fast one (adiabatic). With the same exponent both ways the larger n gives the larger volume for a given ΔV. (Gas-law exponents, closed form.)
  A3. The gas process is stated on the sheet. A held accumulator (an emergency stroke, by default) is charged slowly, so isothermally with charge exponent 1 (V2 = V0·p0/p2), and discharged at the exponent n; sizing it with one exponent from the pre-charge to both pressures understates the volume. The shock and pulsation duty always uses one exponent. (Gas-law sizing of a held accumulator: slow charge, fast discharge.)
  A4. The gas is ideal unless a real-gas factor is entered (at least 1: nitrogen's compressibility factor exceeds 1 at high pressure, so the real gas needs more volume, never less); above 200 bar the sheet warns that nitrogen departs from the ideal-gas law and a factor from a gas-property source should be entered. 1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Friction and fitting losses through series pipe segments at a given flow, the static head, the system curve, the duty point against a pump curve entered as points, the affinity-law shift for a speed or impeller change, and the NPSH available at the pump suction.
Design flow from the heat carried, when a heat load is given in place of a flow: Q = Φ/(ρ c_p ΔT). Mean velocity in the pipe: v = Q/(πD²/4). Reynolds number, which decides laminar or turbulent flow: Re = ρvD/μ. Friction factor for laminar flow (Re < 2300): f = 64/Re. Friction factor for turbulent flow, Colebrook–White by fixed-point iteration (at most 100 steps) from the Swamee–Jain value, which also cross-checks it: 1/√f = −2 log10(ε/3.7D + 2.51/(Re√f)). Head lost in the pipe and its fittings: h_f = f (L + L_eq)/D · v²/2g + K v²/2g. System curve, with friction scaled from the design flow as Q² (a closed circuit has Σdz = 0, the rise in the flow being recovered in the return): H_sys(Q) = Σdz + h_out + Σh_f(Q). Water held in the pipes: V_pipe = Σ π/4·D²·L. Duty point, where the pump and system curves cross (found by bisection): H_pump(Q) = H_sys(Q). Pump affinity laws for a change of speed n or impeller diameter D: Q ∝ n D³; H ∝ n² D². Net positive suction head available, to compare with what the pump requires: NPSH_a = p_atm/ρg + z_s − h_f,suction − p_v/ρg.

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Torque, speed and power a motor needs to move a load through a mechanism on a point-to-point cycle: the load reflected to the motor shaft, the acceleration from the motion profile, peak torque during acceleration, RMS torque over the cycle (thermal duty), peak speed, inertia ratio, and a check of a candidate motor's rated and peak torque and speed with a safety margin. A rotary load that runs continuously (a pump, a fan) is sized instead from its steady torque and speed and a run-up time.
Motor-to-load ratio of a linear mechanism, in metres per motor radian: k = p/(2π i) (screw); D/(2 i) (belt); D_p/(2 i) (rack). Load inertia reflected to the motor through a linear mechanism: J_refl = m k²/η. Load torque at the motor through a linear mechanism: T_load = F k/η, with F = m g (sin θ + μ cos θ) + F_p. Reflected inertia and torque of a rotary load through a gearbox: J_refl = J_L/(i² η); T_load = T_L/(i η). Move profile, trapezoidal 1/3–1/3–1/3 (adjustable): v_pk = s/(t_move − t_acc); a = v_pk/t_acc. Move profile, S-curve (sine acceleration, same peak speed): a_pk = (π/2) a_trap. Torque to accelerate the total inertia, and the peak torque: T_acc = J_total α; T_pk = T_acc + T_load; deceleration T_dec = −T_acc + T_load; the peak check is on the larger magnitude, SF·max(|T_pk|, |T_dec|) ≤ T_peak, and the braking power is P_brake = max(0, −T_dec·ω_pk). Design torque for a coupling or any part downstream that must carry what the drive can deliver, on the basis the owner chooses (T_basis): the load peak T_design = T_pk (default), the motor peak T_design = T_peak, a service factor on the rated torque T_design = SF_c·T_rated, or a protective limit T_design = T_lim (a torque limiter or a drive setting). Holding torque in the dwell of a linear mechanism that is not held by a brake: T_hold = m g sin θ·k (efficiency not credited: it is a torque to stand still); it is part of the RMS torque for the whole dwell. Alternating torque amplitude of a point-to-point cycle about T_load, for a shaft's fatigue: T_alt = (T_pk − T_dec)/2 (0 in continuous duty). Torque behind the ratio on the output shaft of a rotary mechanism: T_out = T·i·η (mean, alternating and peak), n_out = n_pk/i. Peak axial thrust of a ball screw on its support bearings: F_ax_pk = F_load + m a_pk (0 for other mechanisms). RMS (thermal) torque over the whole cycle, dwell included: T_rms = √(Σ T_i² t_i / t_cycle), the dwell at T_hold. RMS mechanical power over the cycle, the thermal-equivalent continuous power a supply sees (regeneration counts as power; the instantaneous peak P_pk is not a continuous demand), trapezoidal profile: P_rms = ω_pk √{[t_acc·(2/3)(T_load² + T_acc²) + T_load² t_const]/t_cycle}; for the S-curve the acceleration term becomes t_acc·[(3/4) T_load² + (5/16) T_acc²]. Continuous rotary duty (a pump or fan), speed at the motor: ω = 2π n_L i/60; n_pk = n_L i. Continuous rotary duty, load torque and RMS torque: T_load = T_L/(i η); T_rms = T_load. Peak torque during the run-up, with full load throughout (conservative): T_pk = J_total ω/t_start + T_load. Continuous power: P_cont = T_load ω; P_rms = P_cont; continuous duty needs the steady load torque T_L (blank or zero is refused: it would size the motor for no load). Rated shaft power of the candidate motor, from its rated torque and its rated speed n_rated (not the running speed), the shaft power its supply must be sized for; without n_rated there is no P_rated and the sheet warns: P_rated = T_rated·2π n_rated/60; check P_cont ≤ P_rated, u_power = P_cont/P_rated.
  Ref: Hughes & Drury, Electric Motors and Drives Ch. on motor and drive selection — Load torque, inertia referred to the motor, duty cycles
  Ref: Manufacturer servo-sizing guides (Kollmorgen, Parker) — Reflected inertia, RMS torque, inertia ratio practice

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Rigid transmission; the mechanism efficiency applies to the driving direction and is also used in the deceleration phase (conservative for peak, slightly pessimistic for RMS). (Servo-sizing practice.)
  A2. The friction force is Coulomb friction on the moved mass; viscous and breakaway friction are not modelled unless included in F_p. (Load model.)
  A3. Continuous duty: the load torque is constant at the stated speed and the motor accelerates the whole inertia linearly to speed in t_start while carrying the full load torque (conservative); the move distance, move time, acceleration fraction, dwell and profile are ignored. (Run-up idealisation for pumps and fans.)
  A4. The S-curve profile has sine-shaped acceleration over the same acceleration time, so its peak acceleration is π/2 times the trapezoidal value. (Jerk-limited profile idealisation.)
  A5. i (Gear ratio i (motor : output, 1 = none)) is taken as 1. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Voltage drop along a circuit in volts and per cent of the nominal voltage, the conductor power loss, and an estimate of the conductor operating temperature, for DC, single-phase and three-phase AC, from resistivity and cross-section (with temperature correction), from tabulated R and X per kilometre, or from a mV/A/m figure; parallel conductors per phase; the drop limit is a user input (the regulatory limits live in BS 7671 Appendix 4 and Regulation 525 and are not embedded).
Conductor resistance at its operating temperature, for n conductors in parallel: R = ρ_T L/(A n), with ρ_T = ρ_20 [1 + α (T_c − 20)]. Resistance and reactance from per-length figures instead: R = R′ L/n; X = X′ L/n. Voltage drop on a DC circuit: ΔV = 2 I R. Voltage drop on a single-phase AC circuit: ΔV = 2 I (R cos φ + X sin φ). Voltage drop on a three-phase AC circuit: ΔV = √3 I (R cos φ + X sin φ). Voltage drop from a tabulated mV/A/m figure, which already carries the 2 or √3: ΔV = (mV/A/m) I L / 1000. Power lost in the cable: P_loss = 2 I² R (DC and single-phase); 3 I² R (three-phase). Estimated conductor temperature at this current: T_c,est = T_amb + (T_rated − T_amb)(I/I_z)².

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
The voltage dip when a three-phase induction motor starts, at the motor terminals and at the point of common coupling, from the supply impedance (a fault level or a transformer), the cable and the motor's locked-rotor impedance for a direct-on-line or star-delta start (a passive motor impedance) or a soft-start or drive start (a regulated current), by the complex potential divider; the dip is checked against a user limit (default 10 %). Also the capacitor kvar to raise the running power factor to a target, the capacitor current and the parallel-resonance order, with a warning when harmonic loads are present. Steady-state phasor model: no motor run-up dynamics, no flicker assessment, no starting-torque adequacy check.

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. The supply is a stiff source behind the stated impedance (voltage E = U/√3 before the start). A direct-on-line or star-delta start, and a soft starter that does not reach its current limit at the dipped voltage (full conduction), draw current through a passive winding: a constant impedance equal to the locked-rotor impedance at rated voltage and the starting power factor, so the current falls with the dipped voltage. A soft starter that reaches its limit and a drive regulate the current: they hold |I| = I_start whatever the voltage, so the dip is deeper than the passive model gives; the current phasor lags the voltage at the starter or drive input by the stated power factor. (Standard steady-state starting-dip screening model; the regulated case is solved in closed form.)
  A2. The dip is the steady-state phasor value at the instant of locked rotor; the current falls as the motor accelerates, so the dip is the worst case. No other load is running on the supply and no run-up dynamics, flicker (Pst) or tap-changer action is assessed. (Stated limitation.)
  A3. A star-delta start draws one third of the direct-on-line current at rated voltage (and one third of the starting torque, which is not checked here); the current spike at the star-to-delta changeover is not modelled. (Star-delta connection ratio.)
  A4. The cable resistance follows E3: from the resistivity, area and temperature, or from R′ per kilometre; X′ per kilometre is a user or record value. No cable table is embedded. (User or record values.)
  A5. The capacitor kvar corrects the running power factor of the motor input power P/η at rated load; the capacitor sits at the point of common coupling, and the resonance order uses the short-circuit apparent power U²/|Z_s| there (|Z_s| stands in for X_s, so h reads 1 to 1.4 % low). Capacitor switching, detuning and self-excitation on disconnection are not assessed. (Standard power-factor-correction relation.)
  A6. Source: E5 'Z_s' and 'I_f' are earth-fault-loop quantities (line to earth through the protective conductor), not the positive-sequence source impedance a motor start sees, so they are not linked; the fault level links E5 I_k3. E5 computes I_k3 with c = 1.1 and U_n = √3·U_0 (398.4 V at 230 V), so the I_k basis defaults c to 1.1 and U should be √3·U_0 for an exact match with E5's Z_T. E6 declares no impedance port, so the transformer rating and impedance are entered. (Linking decision.)
  A7. The source impedance should be taken at the minimum fault level (the deepest dip); a transformer alone is an infinite bus behind it, which understates the dip by a few per cent and overstates the resonance order unless the upstream network fault level S_kQ is entered. (Stated limitation.)
  A8. With a drive, the power-factor correction starts from the drive's displacement factor, not the motor's, the drive is itself a harmonic load, and the 'torque factor' and the voltage at the starter or drive input (not the motor terminals) are reported only for what they are. (Regulated-current starters.)
