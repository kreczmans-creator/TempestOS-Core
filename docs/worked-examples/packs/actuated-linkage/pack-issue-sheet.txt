1. SCOPE AND METHOD
A single-degree-of-freedom planar mechanism driven at constant speed: a four-bar linkage or an offset slider-crank (both circuits, position, velocity and acceleration over a sweep of the input angle, Grashof class, transmission angle against a minimum, toggle and dead-centre positions, loop-closure residual, input torque by virtual work), or a cam with a translating follower. Rigid links, no clearance, no friction.
Four-bar position: a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} = d + c·e^{jθ₄}; ℓ = |A − O₄|, cos γ = (c² + ℓ² − b²)/(2cℓ), θ₄ = φ ∓ γ (φ the direction O₄→A; open: −, crossed: +); θ₃ from B − A Four-bar velocity and acceleration: differentiated loop, 2×2 system with det = b·c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄); ω₃ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₄ − θ₂)/(b·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)), ω₄ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₃ − θ₂)/(c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)); α from the second derivative at constant ω₂ Grashof class and transmission angle: s + l < p + q (crank-rocker, double-crank or double-rocker by the shortest link), = at the change point, > non-Grashof; transmission angle cos μ = (b² + c² − ℓ²)/(2bc) folded to 0 … 90°; toggle where ℓ = b + c or |b − c| Slider-crank position: x = r·cosθ + σ·√(l² − (r·sinθ − e)²) (σ = +1 open, −1 crossed); dx/dθ = −r·sinθ − r·cosθ·(r·sinθ − e)/(x − r·cosθ); dead centres where crank and rod are collinear; μ = 90° − |rod angle to the slide line| Input torque by virtual work: T_in·ω_in = Σ F·v; four-bar T_in = (T_L + J·α₄)·ω₄/ω₂; slider T_in = (m·a + F_L)·dx/dθ; RMS torque and mean power over the sweep by Simpson's rule Mechanism forces: four-bar tangential pin force P = (T_L + J·α₄)/c, coupler (and crank-pin) force P/sin μ; slider slide-direction force F_x = m·a + F_L, rod force F_x/cos θ₃, guide reaction F_x·tan θ₃; cam contact normal F/cos φ Assembly limits: four-bar toggles at cos θ₂ = (d² + a² − ℓ²)/(2ad), ℓ = b + c or |b − c|; slider sin θ = (e ± l)/r. The sweep is cut at them and, driven at constant speed into one, the unbounded peaks are not reported Loop closure: |a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} − c·e^{jθ₄} − d| (slider: |r·e^{jθ} + l·e^{jθ₃} − (x + j·e)|) asserted below 1e-9 mm at every step Cam, per segment with x = (θ − θ₀)/β: rise s = s₀ + h·f(x), return s = s₀ − h·f(x); cycloidal f = x − sin(2πx)/(2π); 3-4-5 f = 10x³ − 15x⁴ + 6x⁵; 4-5-6-7 f = 35x⁴ − 84x⁵ + 70x⁶ − 20x⁷; modified trapezoid from its acceleration pieces (C_v = 2, C_a = 8π/(π + 2), C_j = 32π²/(π + 2)) Cam SVAJ at constant ω: v = ω·s′, a = ω²·s″, j = ω³·s‴ (′ = d/dθ); peak coefficients C_v = max f′, C_a = max |f″|, C_j = max |f‴|, so v_pk = C_v·h·ω/β Pressure angle (roller, offset e, R_p = R_b + R_r): φ = arctan[(s′ − e)/(√(R_p² − e²) + s)], which for e = 0 is arctan[s′/(R_p + s)]; the base circle that holds φ ≤ φ_max has R_p,req = √(s₀,req² + e²) with s₀,req = max(|s′ − e|/tan φ_max − s) Radius of curvature of the pitch curve P(θ) = Rot(−θ)·(e, √(R_p² − e²) + s): ρ = |P′|³/(P′ × P″), convex where positive; undercut where ρ_min ≤ R_r (FAIL), none where the curve is nowhere convex; flat-faced follower: signed profile radius ρ = R_b + s + s″, the minimum of which must stay above zero (FAIL at or below) and the face must reach |s′|; the cam turns counter-clockwise with the follower axis at +x offset e Cam follower force F = m·a + k·s + F_pre + F_ext (the follower leaves the cam where F < 0); input torque by virtual work T = F·s′ (T·ω = F·v)
  Ref: Norton, Design of Machinery Ch. 4, 6, 7 — Position, velocity and acceleration analysis; transmission angle; Grashof condition
  Ref: Uicker, Pennock and Shigley, Theory of Machines and Mechanisms Ch. 4–6 — Loop-closure position analysis; velocity and acceleration of planar linkages

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Rigid links, a planar mechanism with one degree of freedom, no joint clearance and no friction; the input turns at a constant speed. (Scope limits of the model.)
  A2. The position is the closed-form solution of the vector loop; the open and crossed circuits are the two branches (θ₄ = φ − γ and φ + γ for the four-bar, σ = +1 and −1 for the slider) and the sweep stays on the chosen branch; for a change-point linkage (parallelogram, deltoid) the circuit is named at a reference angle that does not depend on the sweep (the middle of the first interval between change points in 0 … 2π, π/2 when there are none), so the label at a crank angle means the same motion on every turn; only the true change points (all four links collinear, θ₂ = 0 or π) flip the branch, and it is followed by continuity through them inside the sweep. A linkage with one change point per turn (a kite) repeats every 720° and a sweep of more than one turn warns. Angles at which the linkage cannot be assembled, or at a toggle where the velocity system is singular, are left out of the table and counted. (Norton, Design of Machinery, position analysis of the four-bar and the offset slider-crank.)
  A3. Input torque is by virtual work from the output load and the inertia of the output element only (four-bar: the output link's moment of inertia about its pivot; slider: the slider mass); the crank and the coupler or connecting rod are massless. (No link-inertia or gravity terms: add the link masses to the output element for a first allowance.)
  A4. The peaks (speeds, accelerations, torque, transmission angle, swing) are those of the continuous motion over the swept range, found by a dense scan refined by golden section; the printed table (steps N, up to 3600) only samples it. (Peaks do not depend on the table's step count.)
  A5. Cam: a translating follower on a cam turning at constant speed; the follower mass, a linear return spring (rate and preload at the base circle, for example linked from M7) and a constant external load make the force the cam must apply, F = m·a + k·s + F_pre + F_ext; contact is lost where it falls below zero. The segment table must add to 360° and close; pressure angle and undercut are checked for the roller follower (R_p = R_b + R_r), the positive profile radius for the flat-faced one. (Norton, Design of Machinery, cam design: the four classical laws, pressure angle and radius of curvature.)
  A6. Follower force (four-bar: the tangential force at the output pin, (T_L + J·α₄)/c; slider: the slide-direction force m·a + F_L; cam: the axial force) is the force the mechanism must apply to the output element. The force in the coupler (F_pin_pk) is the tangential force over sin μ; in the connecting rod it is the slide-direction force over cos θ₃, and the guide reaction is that force times tan θ₃; the roller contact normal force is F/cos φ. Crank, coupler and rod are massless two-force members. (Rigid body, frictionless.)
  A7. When the sweep meets an assembly limit (a toggle of a four-bar, the rod perpendicular to the slide line of a slider-crank) the sweep is cut there and, because the speed, acceleration, torque and forces at constant input speed are unbounded at the limit, they are not reported; the limit angles are listed. A change-point four-bar (parallelogram, deltoid) is continued through its singular position on the same branch by continuity (γ changes sign there), but its speeds and torques next to the singular position are not reported. (Closed-form limit angles: ℓ = b + c or |b − c| (four-bar), |r·sinθ − e| = l (slider).)
  A8. Into F1 and E1: output_motion is a kinematic proxy at constant crank speed, not the cylinder speed of an actuated linkage. For E1 use the peak input_torque for the peak check and T_rms_in with n_in for the thermal sizing; the mean power is P_mean. (Used by the actuated-linkage and mechanism-review packs.)

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
A double-acting hydraulic cylinder in its circuit: the bore, rod and annulus areas; the force and speed on the extend and the retract stroke with mechanical and volumetric efficiency and an optional back pressure, or with the regenerative extend connection; the swept volume and the cycle time; the pump delivery, shaft power, torque and speed; a hydraulic motor's torque; the velocity in the suction, pressure and return lines against your own limits and the smallest listed hose or pipe bore that meets them; a sharp orifice's flow for flow-control screening; and a screening check of the rod against buckling (Euler above the transition slenderness, Johnson parabola below it). screening only; ISO/TS 13725 method not implemented. Not a cylinder design to a standard: the limits, the hose sizes, the mounting factor and the material properties are your records.
Piston and rod areas: A_b = π·D²/4, A_r = π·d_r²/4, A_a = A_b − A_r, area ratio φ = A_b/A_a Cylinder force: F_ext = (p·A_b − p_back·A_a)·η_m, F_ret = (p·A_a − p_back·A_b)·η_m; regenerative extend F_ext = p·A_r·η_m Speed and time: v_ext = Q·η_vc/A_b (regenerative: A_r), v_ret = Q·η_vc/A_a; t = stroke/v; t_cycle = t_ext + t_ret + t_dwell Required speed (optional): when v_req is given it gives the flow, Q = v_req·A_b/η_vc (regenerative: A_r), and replaces the entered Q; the flow must lie within 0.1 to 2000 L/min Swept volume: ΔV_ext = A_b·stroke (regenerative: A_r·stroke), ΔV_ret = A_a·stroke; ΔV = the larger Pump flow and power: Q_p = V_g·n·η_vp; P = p·Q_p/η_tp; T = P/ω; displacement needed V_g,req = Q/(n·η_vp) Hydraulic motor: T = Δp·V_g/(2π)·η_hm (V_g per revolution) Line velocities: v = Q_line/A_line; suction Q_suc = max(Q, V_g·n) (the geometric delivery), pressure/cap-port line Q_prs = Q_line·A_b/A_a on the retract stroke (regenerative extend: Q_line·A_b/A_r if larger), return Q_rtn = Q_line·A_b/A_a, with Q_line = max(Q, Q_p); smallest bore d = √(4Q/(π·v_max)), recommended = the smallest listed size not below it Orifice flow: Q = C_d·A_o·√(2Δp/ρ); bore for the circuit flow d_o = √(4Q/(π·C_d·√(2Δp/ρ))) Rod buckling (screening): L_eff = K·(stroke + L_add), r = d_r/4, λ = L_eff/r, λ_c = π·√(2E/f_y); Euler F_cr = π²·E·I/L_eff² for λ > λ_c, Johnson F_cr = A_r·f_y·[1 − f_y·λ²/(4π²E)] for λ ≤ λ_c; load F_b = max(F_push, F_stall) with F_stall = p·A_b (the stalled cylinder at the supply pressure: no flow, so no back pressure and no friction loss; in the regenerative extend too, since the standard connection can always be selected); utilisation u = F_b·S_f/F_cr

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. The supply pressure p is the gauge pressure at the cylinder port and, for the pump, at the pump outlet; line losses are not subtracted (the line losses of a pipe run come from the pipe calculation). The force is the pressure on the area less the back pressure on the other side, times the mechanical efficiency. (F = p·A·η_m, closed form.)
  A2. The speed is the flow into the cylinder times the volumetric efficiency over the area, in steady motion: no acceleration, cushioning or compressibility. The cycle time is the two strokes plus the dwell. (v = Q·η_vc/A.)
  A3. The pump power is the supply pressure times the pump's delivery over the total efficiency: when the pump delivers more than the circuit takes, the excess is taken to go over the relief valve at the supply pressure, which is the conservative case for the drive. The shaft torque is the power over the angular speed. (P = p·Q_p/η_tp.)
  A4. The line flows use the pump delivery when it is larger than the circuit flow: the suction line the geometric delivery V_g·n, the pressure line and the return line Q_line = max(Q, Q_p), which carries no volumetric efficiency while the speeds use Q·η_vc (the flows are the conservative side of the speeds). The line to the cylinder cap port is judged at the larger flow of the two strokes: on the retract stroke it carries the cap-side discharge Q_line·A_b/A_a (the same flow as the return line), and in the regenerative extend Q_line·A_b/A_r if that is larger. The velocity limits are your records; the sheet's defaults are placeholders, not values from a standard. (Continuity.)
  A5. The orifice relation is the sharp-edged turbulent one, Q = C_d·A·√(2Δp/ρ), with your discharge coefficient; it ignores viscosity and cavitation and is a screening of a flow-control orifice, not a valve selection. (Orifice equation, closed form.)
  A6. The rod is treated as a solid round column of its own diameter with the effective-length factor of the mounting; the tube, the piston and the rod-end stiffness are not modelled. The rod is judged at the larger of the push load you enter and the stall thrust p·A_b (a stalled, obstructed or relief-limited cylinder loads the rod with the full pressure force on the bore: no flow, so no back pressure, and the seal friction does not relieve it); no pressure-limiting device is credited, and the regenerative extend is judged at the same stall thrust because the standard connection can always be selected. The mounting factor K_m defaults to 1 (pinned at both ends): a fixed-free mounting is K_m = 2, and the factor and the safety factor S_f are your records. screening only; ISO/TS 13725 method not implemented. 1. SCOPE AND METHOD
The pre-charge gas volume V0 of a hydraulic accumulator that delivers, or absorbs, a liquid volume ΔV between the lowest working pressure p1 and the highest p2, from p·Vⁿ = const with absolute pressures and an exponent n between 1 (isothermal) and 1.4 (adiabatic), the gas either charged as fast as it is discharged or (a held accumulator, the default for an emergency stroke) charged slowly and isothermally to p2 and then discharged at n; the volume scaled to the lowest operating temperature (T_max/T_min) and the pre-charge pressure at the charging temperature; the next size from your own size list; and the bar·litre product PS·V of the vessel's own maximum allowable pressure and volume against the PSSR Schedule 1 250 bar·L exception. Three modes: energy storage, shock and pulsation (screening only) and emergency stroke (ΔV is the stroke volume, linkable from the cylinder circuit). Not a pressure-vessel code check.
Absolute pressures: p_a = p_gauge + p_atm (or as entered, in Absolute mode) Gas volume, one exponent both ways (charged as fast as it is discharged): V0 = ΔV / [(p0/p1)^(1/n) − (p0/p2)^(1/n)], n = 1 isothermal … 1.4 adiabatic Gas volume, slow charge and discharge at n (a held accumulator): charged isothermally to p2, V2 = V0·p0/p2, then discharged at n to p1, V1 = V2·(p2/p1)^(1/n), ΔV = V1 − V2, so V0 = ΔV·(p2/p0) / [(p2/p1)^(1/n) − 1] Either volume × the real-gas factor f_rg (user input, at least 1) and × (T_max + 273.15)/(T_min + 273.15), because p0 is defined at T_max and the gas delivers less when cold Pre-charge rules (records, applied to absolute pressures): p0 ≤ r_0·p1 at T_max (default 0.9, bladder type) and p2/p0(T_min) ≤ r_p (default 4), p0(T_min) = p0·(T_min + 273.15)/(T_max + 273.15) Pre-charge at the charging temperature: p0(T_c) = p0(T_max)·(T_c + 273.15)/(T_max + 273.15) Next size: the smallest listed size not below V0 PSSR bar-litre product: PS·V, PS = the vessel's maximum allowable pressure (bar gauge, entered, not below p2 gauge), V = the vessel volume entered (default the selected size); compared with 250 bar·L (PSSR Schedule 1, indication only; not available without PS and a volume)
  Ref: Pressure Systems Safety Regulations 2000 (SI 2000/128) Schedule 1 — 250 bar·litre exception, indication only
  Ref: Pressure Equipment Directive 2014/68/EU and UK PER 2016 — Pressure equipment; not checked here
  Ref: Esposito, Fluid Power with Applications Ch. on accumulators — Gas-charged accumulator sizing, p·Vⁿ = const

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. All gas-law pressures are absolute. A gauge entry is converted with the atmospheric pressure entered here, so the same numbers read as gauge or as absolute give different volumes. (p·Vⁿ = const on a fixed mass of gas.)
  A2. The exponent n is the user's: 1 for a slow discharge with heat exchange (isothermal), towards 1.4 for a fast one (adiabatic). With the same exponent both ways the larger n gives the larger volume for a given ΔV. (Gas-law exponents, closed form.)
  A3. The gas process is stated on the sheet. A held accumulator (an emergency stroke, by default) is charged slowly, so isothermally with charge exponent 1 (V2 = V0·p0/p2), and discharged at the exponent n; sizing it with one exponent from the pre-charge to both pressures understates the volume. The one-exponent form (charge exponent = n) is for a gas charged as fast as it is discharged, as in a pulsation or a fast cycle; choose it in the energy-storage duty for that case. The shock and pulsation duty always uses one exponent. (Gas-law sizing of a held accumulator: slow charge, fast discharge.)
  A4. The gas is ideal unless a real-gas factor is entered (at least 1: nitrogen's compressibility factor exceeds 1 at high pressure, so the real gas needs more volume, never less); above 200 bar the sheet warns that nitrogen departs from the ideal-gas law and a factor from a gas-property source should be entered. (Real-gas correction as a user factor.)
  A5. The pre-charge p0 is defined at the maximum operating temperature T_max and V0 is sized there; at the lowest operating temperature T_min the same gas delivers only the fraction T_min/T_max (absolute) of ΔV, so V0 is multiplied by T_max/T_min, and the p2/p0 rule is checked with p0 at T_min. The temperature range is the user's; with T_min = T_max the correction is 1. (Manufacturer sizing practice; the gas law at constant mass.)
  A6. The pre-charge rules (p0 ≤ 0.9·p1 and p2/p0 ≤ 4 by default) are records for the accumulator type and make and are checked as PASS/FAIL on ABSOLUTE pressures, whichever basis the pressures are entered on; the right values, and whether the manufacturer states the ratio on gauge or absolute pressures, come from the manufacturer (the same numbers read as gauge pressures give a different ratio). (Rules are records, not built-in limits.)
  A7. The bar·litre product is PS times V of the vessel itself: PS is the vessel's maximum allowable pressure in bar gauge (the nameplate or relief setting, never below the highest working pressure p2) and V its internal volume (the selected size unless entered). Without PS and a volume the indication is not available. It indicates whether the 250 bar·L exception of PSSR Schedule 1 can apply; it is not a compliance statement, and PED / UK PER, PSSR examination and the vessel's own design code stay with the manufacturer and the competent person. (Regulatory gating: no pressure-vessel code check.)
  A8. process (Gas process (By duty: an emergency stroke is charged slowly to p2 and discharged at n, the other duties use one exponent both ways)) is taken as By duty. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A9. p_basis (Pressures entered as) is taken as Gauge. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A10. p_atm (Atmospheric pressure (gauge to absolute)) is taken as 1.01325 bar a. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A11. f_rg (Real-gas factor on V0 (1 = ideal gas; at least 1, as nitrogen's compressibility factor exceeds 1 at high pressure; from a gas-property source above 200 bar)) is taken as 1. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A12. T_c (Charging temperature) is taken as 20 degC. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A13. r_0 (Pre-charge rule: p0 (at T_max) at most this fraction of p1, on absolute pressures (state whether the manufacturer's ratio is on gauge or absolute pressures)) is taken as 0.9. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A14. r_p (Pre-charge rule: p2/p0 (p0 at T_min) at most this ratio, on absolute pressures) is taken as 4. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A15. Emergency stroke duty: ΔV is the volume the emergency stroke needs (swept volume plus line compression; linkable from the cylinder circuit); p_1 is the lowest pressure at which the stroke still has the force it needs; p_2 is the pressure the accumulator is held at, reached by a slow charge. (Gas-law sizing for a stored-energy stroke.)
