1. Scope and method
A single-degree-of-freedom planar mechanism driven at constant speed: a four-bar linkage or an offset slider-crank (both circuits, position, velocity and acceleration over a sweep of the input angle, Grashof class, transmission angle against a minimum, toggle and dead-centre positions, loop-closure residual, input torque by virtual work), or a cam with a translating follower. Rigid links, no clearance, no friction.
Four-bar position: a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} = d + c·e^{jθ₄}; ℓ = |A − O₄|, cos γ = (c² + ℓ² − b²)/(2cℓ), θ₄ = φ ∓ γ (φ the direction O₄→A; open: −, crossed: +); θ₃ from B − A Four-bar velocity and acceleration: differentiated loop, 2×2 system with det = b·c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄); ω₃ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₄ − θ₂)/(b·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)), ω₄ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₃ − θ₂)/(c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)); α from the second derivative at constant ω₂ Grashof class and transmission angle: s + l < p + q (crank-rocker, double-crank or double-rocker by the shortest link), = at the change point, > non-Grashof; transmission angle cos μ = (b² + c² − ℓ²)/(2bc) folded to 0 … 90°; toggle where ℓ = b + c or |b − c| Slider-crank position: x = r·cosθ + σ·√(l² − (r·sinθ − e)²) (σ = +1 open, −1 crossed); dx/dθ = −r·sinθ − r·cosθ·(r·sinθ − e)/(x − r·cosθ); dead centres where crank and rod are collinear; μ = 90° − |rod angle to the slide line| Input torque by virtual work: T_in·ω_in = Σ F·v; four-bar T_in = (T_L + J·α₄)·ω₄/ω₂; slider T_in = (m·a + F_L)·dx/dθ; RMS torque and mean power over the sweep by Simpson's rule Mechanism forces: four-bar tangential pin force P = (T_L + J·α₄)/c, coupler (and crank-pin) force P/sin μ; slider slide-direction force F_x = m·a + F_L, rod force F_x/cos θ₃, guide reaction F_x·tan θ₃; cam contact normal F/cos φ Assembly limits: four-bar toggles at cos θ₂ = (d² + a² − ℓ²)/(2ad), ℓ = b + c or |b − c|; slider sin θ = (e ± l)/r. The sweep is cut at them and, driven at constant speed into one, the unbounded peaks are not reported Loop closure: |a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} − c·e^{jθ₄} − d| (slider: |r·e^{jθ} + l·e^{jθ₃} − (x + j·e)|) asserted below 1e-9 mm at every step Cam, per segment with x = (θ − θ₀)/β: rise s = s₀ + h·f(x), return s = s₀ − h·f(x); cycloidal f = x − sin(2πx)/(2π); 3-4-5 f = 10x³ − 15x⁴ + 6x⁵; 4-5-6-7 f = 35x⁴ − 84x⁵ + 70x⁶ − 20x⁷; modified trapezoid from its acceleration pieces (C_v = 2, C_a = 8π/(π + 2), C_j = 32π²/(π + 2)) Cam SVAJ at constant ω: v = ω·s′, a = ω²·s″, j = ω³·s‴ (′ = d/dθ); peak coefficients C_v = max f′, C_a = max |f″|, C_j = max |f‴|, so v_pk = C_v·h·ω/β Pressure angle (roller, offset e, R_p = R_b + R_r): φ = arctan[(s′ − e)/(√(R_p² − e²) + s)], which for e = 0 is arctan[s′/(R_p + s)]; the base circle that holds φ ≤ φ_max has R_p,req = √(s₀,req² + e²) with s₀,req = max(|s′ − e|/tan φ_max − s) Radius of curvature of the pitch curve P(θ) = Rot(−θ)·(e, √(R_p² − e²) + s): ρ = |P′|³/(P′ × P″), convex where positive; undercut where ρ_min ≤ R_r (FAIL), none where the curve is nowhere convex; flat-faced follower: signed profile radius ρ = R_b + s + s″, the minimum of which must stay above zero (FAIL at or below) and the face must reach |s′|; the cam turns counter-clockwise with the follower axis at +x offset e Cam follower force F = m·a + k·s + F_pre + F_ext (the follower leaves the cam where F < 0); input torque by virtual work T = F·s′ (T·ω = F·v)

3. Assumptions
  A1. Rigid links, a planar mechanism with one degree of freedom, no joint clearance and no friction; the input turns at a constant speed. (Scope limits of the model.)
  A2. The position is the closed-form solution of the vector loop; the open and crossed circuits are the two branches (θ₄ = φ − γ and φ + γ for the four-bar, σ = +1 and −1 for the slider) and the sweep stays on the chosen branch; for a change-point linkage (parallelogram, deltoid) the circuit is named at a reference angle that does not depend on the sweep (the middle of the first interval between change points in 0 … 2π, π/2 when there are none), so the label at a crank angle means the same motion on every turn; only the true change points (all four links collinear, θ₂ = 0 or π) flip the branch, and it is followed by continuity through them inside the sweep. A linkage with one change point per turn (a kite) repeats every 720° and a sweep of more than one turn warns. Angles at which the linkage cannot be assembled, or at a toggle where the velocity system is singular, are left out of the table and counted. (Norton, Design of Machinery, position analysis of the four-bar and the offset slider-crank.)
  A3. Input torque is by virtual work from the output load and the inertia of the output element only (four-bar: the output link's moment of inertia about its pivot; slider: the slider mass); the crank and the coupler or connecting rod are massless. (No link-inertia or gravity terms: add the link masses to the output element for a first allowance.)
  A4. The peaks (speeds, accelerations, torque, transmission angle, swing) are those of the continuous motion over the swept range, found by a dense scan refined by golden section; the printed table (steps N, up to 3600) only samples it. (Peaks do not depend on the table's step count.)
  A5. Cam: a translating follower on a cam turning at constant speed; the follower mass, a linear return spring (rate and preload at the base circle, for example linked from M7) and a constant external load make the force the cam must apply, F = m·a + k·s + F_pre + F_ext; contact is lost where it falls below zero. The segment table must add to 360° and close; pressure angle and undercut are checked for the roller follower (R_p = R_b + R_r), the positive profile radius for the flat-faced one. (Norton, Design of Machinery, cam design: the four classical laws, pressure angle and radius of curvature.)
  A6. Follower force (four-bar: the tangential force at the output pin, (T_L + J·α₄)/c; slider: the slide-direction force m·a + F_L; cam: the axial force) is the force the mechanism must apply to the output element. The force in the coupler (F_pin_pk) is the tangential force over sin μ; in the connecting rod it is the slide-direction force over cos θ₃, and the guide reaction is that force times tan θ₃; the roller contact normal force is F/cos φ. Crank, coupler and rod are massless two-force members. (Rigid body, frictionless.)
  A7. When the sweep meets an assembly limit (a toggle of a four-bar, the rod perpendicular to the slide line of a slider-crank) the sweep is cut there and, because the speed, acceleration, torque and forces at constant input speed are unbounded at the limit, they are not reported; the limit angles are listed. A change-point four-bar (parallelogram, deltoid) is continued through its singular position on the same branch by continuity (γ changes sign there), but its speeds and torques next to the singular position are not reported. (Closed-form limit angles: ℓ = b + c or |b − c| (four-bar), |r·sinθ − e| = l (slider).)
  A8. Into F1 and E1: output_motion is a kinematic proxy at constant crank speed, not the cylinder speed of an actuated linkage. For E1 use the peak input_torque for the peak check and T_rms_in with n_in for the thermal sizing; the mean power is P_mean. (Used by the actuated-linkage and mechanism-review packs.)
  A9. circuit (Assembly circuit (four-bar, slider-crank): open is θ₄ = φ − γ (slider on the +x side of the crank pin), crossed the mirror branch) is taken as Open. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)

1. Scope and method
A cylindrical helical spring of round wire, cold-coiled, with a linear characteristic: compression (rate, working points, solid length by end type, stress at the working points and at solid, buckling, Goodman fatigue, surge frequency), extension (the same with the initial tension and the hook stresses) or torsion (rate with the legs, curvature-corrected bending stress, wound diameter and body length).
Index and rate: C = D/d; k = G·d⁴/(8·D³·n) (compression, extension); k_φ = E·d⁴/(64·D·n_a), n_a = n + (l₁ + l₂)/(3π·D) (torsion) Spring stress: τ_k = k_c·8·F·D/(π·d³), k_c = (C + 0.5)/(C − 0.75) (Bergsträsser) or (4C − 1)/(4C − 4) + 0.615/C (Wahl); torsion σ = q·32·M/(π·d³) with q = (C + 0.07)/(C − 0.75) (EN 13906-3, the default) or K_i = (4C² − C − 1)/(4C·(C − 1)) (Shigley) Solid length (compression): n_t = n + e_n; EN 13906-1 form L_s = d_max·(n_t + e_s) with (e_n, e_s) = (0, 1) plain, (1, 0) plain and ground, (2, 1.5) squared (closed, unground), (2, 0) squared and ground, and the minimum coil clearance S_a = (0.0015·D²/d + 0.1·d)·n checked at the working length (L₂ ≥ L_s + S_a); Shigley form L_s = d·(n_t + e_s) with e_s = 1 for squared; pitch p = (L₀ − a·d)/(n + b) Extension spring hook stresses: F = F₀ + k·s; hook bending σ_A = F·[K_A·16·D/(π·d³) + 4/(π·d²)], K_A = (4C₁² − C₁ − 1)/(4C₁·(C₁ − 1)), C₁ = 2r₁/d; hook torsion τ_B = K_B·8·F·D/(π·d³), K_B = (4C₂ − 1)/(4C₂ − 4), C₂ = 2r₂/d Buckling (compression): λ_eff = ν·L₀/D; y_cr = L₀·C₁′·[1 − √(1 − C₂′/λ_eff²)], C₁′ = E/(2(E − G)), C₂′ = 2π²(E − G)/(2G + E); stable at any deflection when λ_eff² < C₂′ Fatigue (Goodman, as M4): 1/n_f = a/S_e + m/S_u with a = (x₂ − x₁)/2, m = (x₂ + x₁)/2 of the stresses at the two working points; S_u = f_su·R_m in shear, R_m in bending; R_m typed or R_m = A/d^m from a W3 material (A in MPa, d in mm) Surge frequency: f_n = (d/(2π·n·D²))·√(G/(2ρ)); warning when f_n/f_op < 13 Torsion body: D_w = D·n/(n + φ/2π); L_w = d·(n + 1 + φ/2π)

1. Scope and method
Torque, speed and power a motor needs to move a load through a mechanism on a point-to-point cycle: the load reflected to the motor shaft, the acceleration from the motion profile, peak torque during acceleration, RMS torque over the cycle (thermal duty), peak speed, inertia ratio, and a check of a candidate motor's rated and peak torque and speed with a safety margin. A rotary load that runs continuously (a pump, a fan) is sized instead from its steady torque and speed and a run-up time.
Motor-to-load ratio of a linear mechanism, in metres per motor radian: k = p/(2π i) (screw); D/(2 i) (belt); D_p/(2 i) (rack). Load inertia reflected to the motor through a linear mechanism: J_refl = m k²/η. Load torque at the motor through a linear mechanism: T_load = F k/η, with F = m g (sin θ + μ cos θ) + F_p. Reflected inertia and torque of a rotary load through a gearbox: J_refl = J_L/(i² η); T_load = T_L/(i η). Move profile, trapezoidal 1/3–1/3–1/3 (adjustable): v_pk = s/(t_move − t_acc); a = v_pk/t_acc. Move profile, S-curve (sine acceleration, same peak speed): a_pk = (π/2) a_trap. Torque to accelerate the total inertia, and the peak torque: T_acc = J_total α; T_pk = T_acc + T_load; deceleration T_dec = −T_acc + T_load; the peak check is on the larger magnitude, SF·max(|T_pk|, |T_dec|) ≤ T_peak, and the braking power is P_brake = max(0, −T_dec·ω_pk). Design torque for a coupling or any part downstream that must carry what the drive can deliver, on the basis the owner chooses (T_basis): the load peak T_design = max(|T_pk|, |T_dec|) (default; T_pk alone in continuous duty, where there is no deceleration), the motor peak T_design = T_peak, a service factor on the rated torque T_design = SF_c·T_rated, or a protective limit T_design = T_lim (a torque limiter or a drive setting). Holding torque in the dwell of a linear mechanism that is not held by a brake: T_hold = m g sin θ·k (efficiency not credited: it is a torque to stand still); it is part of the RMS torque for the whole dwell. Alternating torque amplitude of a point-to-point cycle about T_load, for a shaft's fatigue: T_alt = (T_pk − T_dec)/2 (0 in continuous duty). Torque behind the ratio on the output shaft of a rotary mechanism: T_out = T·i·η (mean, alternating and peak), n_out = n_pk/i. Peak axial thrust of a ball screw on its support bearings: F_ax_pk = F_load + m a_pk (0 for other mechanisms). RMS (thermal) torque over the whole cycle, dwell included: T_rms = √(Σ T_i² t_i / t_cycle), the dwell at T_hold. RMS mechanical power over the cycle, the thermal-equivalent continuous power a supply sees (regeneration counts as power; the instantaneous peak P_pk is not a continuous demand), trapezoidal profile: P_rms = ω_pk √{[t_acc·(2/3)(T_load² + T_acc²) + T_load² t_const]/t_cycle}; for the S-curve the acceleration term becomes t_acc·[(3/4) T_load² + (5/16) T_acc²]. Continuous rotary duty (a pump or fan), speed at the motor: ω = 2π n_L i/60; n_pk = n_L i. Continuous rotary duty, load torque and RMS torque: T_load = T_L/(i η); T_rms = T_load. Peak torque during the run-up, with full load throughout (conservative): T_pk = J_total ω/t_start + T_load. Continuous power: P_cont = T_load ω; P_rms = P_cont; continuous duty needs the steady load torque T_L (blank or zero is refused: it would size the motor for no load). Rated shaft power of the candidate motor, from its rated torque and its rated speed n_rated (not the running speed), the shaft power its supply must be sized for; without n_rated there is no P_rated and the sheet warns: P_rated = T_rated·2π n_rated/60; check P_cont ≤ P_rated, u_power = P_cont/P_rated.

3. Assumptions
  A1. Every tabulated value (the capacities, the device ratings, the correction factors) is entered from the user's own licensed copy or a manufacturer datasheet; none ships with the calculator. (No table from a standard or a manufacturer is shipped.)
  A2. The capacities in the table are for the installation method stated; the method key is descriptive and is not interpreted. (Records carry their source and edition.)
  A3. A gG fuse to BS 88 is deemed to meet I₂ ≤ 1.45·I_z when I_n ≤ I_z only under BS 7671 (installation context); an IEC 60269-1 gG fuse has I₂ = 1.6·I_n, so in the machine-wiring context I₂ is entered from the datasheet. (BS 7671 reg. 433.1.201; IEC 60269-1.)
  A4. The ambient, grouping and insulation factors are applied as a product; C_g is not looked up from the number of grouped circuits. (The count is carried to the sheet and warns when C_g = 1 with more than one circuit.)
  A5. The optional derived C_a is an approximation for the temperature difference only and is labelled as such; compare it with the licensed table. (Derived, not tabulated.)
  A6. C_c = 0.725 applies to a semi-enclosed fuse only: 1.45 divided by an assumed fusing factor of 2. (Derived.)
  A7. AWG areas follow the geometry d = 0.127·92^((36 − n)/39) mm; no AWG capacity is supplied. (Derived.)
  A8. DC circuits use I_b = P/U; the power factor and efficiency are not applied. (Method.)
  A9. context (Design context (changes clause citations and the scope note only)) is taken as BS EN 60204-1 machine wiring. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A10. supply (Supply) is taken as three-phase. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A11. theta_a (Ambient temperature) is taken as 30 °C. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A12. N_g (Grouped circuits) is taken as 1. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A13. system (Conductor system (AWG: geometry only, no AWG capacities are supplied)) is taken as metric. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A14. C_a (Ambient correction factor (1 does not apply the ambient: enter the factor for θ_a from your licensed table)) is taken as 1. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A15. C_g (Grouping correction factor (at most 1)) is taken as 1. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A16. C_i (Thermal insulation correction factor (at most 1)) is taken as 1. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A17. derive_Ca (Derive C_a from the temperatures instead of the entered factor) is taken as no. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A18. theta_ref (Reference ambient temperature for the derived C_a) is taken as 30 °C. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A19. Machine wiring context (BS EN 60204-1): the conductor is sized for the protective device rating; the capacities and factors are the user's records for the installation method stated. (The context changes citations, and whether a gG fuse, MCCB or MPCB without I₂ may be deemed to meet I₂ ≤ 1.45·I_z (installation) or must have I₂ entered (machine wiring); no other number differs.)
