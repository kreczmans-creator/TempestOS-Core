1. Scope and method
Calculates the position, velocity, acceleration, input torque and forces of a one-degree-of-freedom planar four-bar, slider-crank or cam mechanism. The input turns at constant speed, and the links are rigid, with no clearance and no friction.
Four-bar position: a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} = d + c·e^{jθ₄}, with ℓ = |A − O₄|, cos γ = (c² + ℓ² − b²)/(2cℓ) and θ₄ = φ ∓ γ (φ the direction O₄→A; open: −, crossed: +); θ₃ from B − A Four-bar velocity and acceleration: differentiated loop, 2×2 system with det = b·c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄); ω₃ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₄ − θ₂)/(b·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)), ω₄ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₃ − θ₂)/(c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)); α from the second derivative at constant ω₂ Grashof class and transmission angle: s + l < p + q (crank-rocker, double-crank or double-rocker by the shortest link), = at the change point, > non-Grashof; transmission angle cos μ = (b² + c² − ℓ²)/(2bc) folded to 0 … 90°; toggle where ℓ = b + c or |b − c| Slider-crank position: x = r·cosθ + σ·√(l² − (r·sinθ − e)²) (σ = +1 open, −1 crossed); dx/dθ = −r·sinθ − r·cosθ·(r·sinθ − e)/(x − r·cosθ); dead centres where crank and rod are collinear; μ = 90° − |rod angle to the slide line| Input torque and forces: T_in·ω_in = Σ F·v by virtual work; four-bar T_in = (T_L + J·α₄)·ω₄/ω₂, tangential pin force P = (T_L + J·α₄)/c and coupler force P/sin μ; slider T_in = (m·a + F_L)·dx/dθ, slide-direction force F_x = m·a + F_L, rod force F_x/cos θ₃ and guide reaction F_x·tan θ₃; RMS torque and mean power over the sweep by Simpson's rule Assembly limits and loop closure: four-bar toggles at cos θ₂ = (d² + a² − ℓ²)/(2ad), ℓ = b + c or |b − c|; slider sin θ = (e ± l)/r; the sweep is cut at them and the unbounded peaks are not reported; closure |a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} − c·e^{jθ₄} − d| (slider: |r·e^{jθ} + l·e^{jθ₃} − (x + j·e)|) below 1e-9 mm at every step Cam motion: per segment with x = (θ − θ₀)/β, rise s = s₀ + h·f(x), return s = s₀ − h·f(x); cycloidal f = x − sin(2πx)/(2π); 3-4-5 f = 10x³ − 15x⁴ + 6x⁵; 4-5-6-7 f = 35x⁴ − 84x⁵ + 70x⁶ − 20x⁷; modified trapezoid from its acceleration pieces (C_v = 2, C_a = 8π/(π + 2), C_j = 32π²/(π + 2)); SVAJ at constant ω: v = ω·s′, a = ω²·s″, j = ω³·s‴ (′ = d/dθ), so v_pk = C_v·h·ω/β Cam contact: roller pressure angle φ = arctan[(s′ − e)/(√(R_p² − e²) + s)] with R_p = R_b + R_r, base circle R_p,req = √(s₀,req² + e²) with s₀,req = max(|s′ − e|/tan φ_max − s); pitch-curve radius ρ = |P′|³/(P′ × P″), undercut where ρ_min ≤ R_r; flat-faced profile radius ρ = R_b + s + s″ above zero; follower force F = m·a + k·s + F_pre + F_ext (the follower leaves the cam where F < 0), input torque T = F·s′, roller normal force F/cos φ

3. Assumptions
  A1. The mechanism is rigid and planar, has one degree of freedom, and has no joint clearance or friction. (Scope limits of the model.)
  A2. The input turns at a constant speed. (Scope limits of the model.)
  A3. The position is the closed-form solution of the vector loop, on the chosen circuit. (Open and crossed are the two branches: θ₄ = φ − γ and φ + γ for the four-bar, σ = +1 and −1 for the slider.)
  A4. The sweep stays on the chosen circuit and follows it by continuity through a change point. (Only a change point, with all four links collinear (θ₂ = 0 or π), flips the branch.)
  A5. A linkage with one change point per turn repeats every 720°, and a sweep of more than one turn warns. (A kite, for example 40/40/100/100, is such a linkage.)
  A6. The table leaves out angles where the linkage cannot be assembled or sits at a toggle. (It counts them, and at a toggle the velocity system is singular.)
  A7. The input torque comes from the output load and the inertia of the output element only. (The crank and the coupler or connecting rod are massless: add their mass to the output element for a first allowance.)
  A8. The crank, the coupler and the connecting rod are massless two-force members. (Rigid body, frictionless.)
  A9. The peaks are those of the continuous motion over the swept range, not of the printed table. (A dense scan refined by golden section finds them, so they do not depend on the step count of the table (up to 3600).)
  A10. At an assembly limit the sweep is cut, and the unbounded peaks, torque and forces are not reported. (The limit angles are listed: ℓ = b + c or |b − c| (four-bar), |r·sinθ − e| = l (slider).)
  A11. A change-point four-bar is continued through its singular position on the same branch. (Its speeds and torques next to the singular position are not reported.)
  A12. The cam drives a translating follower at constant speed. (Norton, Design of Machinery, cam design: the four classical laws, pressure angle and radius of curvature.)
  A13. The follower force is m·a + k·s + F_pre + F_ext, and contact is lost below zero. (The spring rate and the preload are those at the base circle, and the segment table must add to 360° and close.)
  A14. Pressure angle and undercut are checked for the roller follower, and the profile radius for the flat-faced one. (The roller has the prime radius R_p = R_b + R_r.)
  A15. The output speed is a kinematic proxy at constant input speed, not the speed of an actuated cylinder. (For motor sizing, use the peak input_torque for the peak check and T_rms_in with n_in for the thermal sizing. The mean power is P_mean.)
  A16. circuit (Assembly circuit (four-bar, slider-crank): open is θ₄ = φ − γ (slider on the +x side of the crank pin), crossed the mirror branch) is taken as Open. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A17. L1 (Ground link d, O₂ to O₄ (four-bar)) is taken as 100 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A18. L2 (Crank length a (four-bar) or r (slider-crank)) is taken as 30 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A19. L3 (Coupler length b (four-bar) or connecting-rod length l (slider-crank)) is taken as 90 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A20. L4 (Rocker length c (four-bar)) is taken as 70 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A21. theta_start (Input angle at the first row of the table) is taken as 0 °. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A22. theta_range (Swept range of the input angle (360° for a full turn)) is taken as 360 °. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A23. load_dir (External load: constant direction, or always opposing the output motion) is taken as Opposes motion. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A24. T_load (External load torque on the output link (four-bar). 1. Scope and method
Calculates the rate, working points, stresses, buckling, fatigue and surge of a cold-coiled round-wire helical spring: compression, extension or torsion. The characteristic is linear, with hook stresses for extension and legs for torsion springs.
Index and rate: C = D/d; k = G·d⁴/(8·D³·n_active) (compression, extension); k_φ = E·d⁴/(64·D·n_a), n_a = n_active + (l_leg_1 + l_leg_2)/(3π·D) (torsion) Spring stress: τ_k = k_c·8·F·D/(π·d³), k_c = (C + 0.5)/(C − 0.75) (Bergsträsser) or (4C − 1)/(4C − 4) + 0.615/C (Wahl); torsion σ = q·32·M/(π·d³) with q = (C + 0.07)/(C − 0.75) (EN 13906-3, the default) or K_i = (4C² − C − 1)/(4C·(C − 1)) (Shigley) Solid length (compression): n_t = n_active + e_n; EN 13906-1 form L_s = d_max·(n_t + e_s) with (e_n, e_s) = (0, 1) plain, (1, 0) plain and ground, (2, 1.5) squared (closed, unground), (2, 0) squared and ground, and the minimum coil clearance S_a = (0.0015·D²/d + 0.1·d)·n_active checked at the working length (L₂ ≥ L_s + S_a); Shigley form L_s = d·(n_t + e_s) with e_s = 1 for squared; pitch p = (L₀ − a·d)/(n_active + b) Extension spring hook stresses: F = F₀ + k·s; hook bending σ_A = F·[K_A·16·D/(π·d³) + 4/(π·d²)], K_A = (4C₁² − C₁ − 1)/(4C₁·(C₁ − 1)), C₁ = 2r₁/d; hook torsion τ_B = K_B·8·F·D/(π·d³), K_B = (4C₂ − 1)/(4C₂ − 4), C₂ = 2r₂/d Buckling (compression): λ_eff = ν·L₀/D; y_cr = L₀·C₁′·[1 − √(1 − C₂′/λ_eff²)], C₁′ = E/(2(E − G)), C₂′ = 2π²(E − G)/(2G + E); stable at any deflection when λ_eff² < C₂′ Fatigue (Goodman, as M4): 1/n_f = a/S_e + m/S_u with a = (x₂ − x₁)/2, m = (x₂ + x₁)/2 of the stresses at the two working points; S_u = f_su·R_m in shear, R_m in bending; the extension hook has its own rows, bending σ_A with S_e_b and R_m, side bend τ_B with S_e and f_su·R_m; R_m typed or R_m = A/d^m from a material record (A in MPa, d in mm) Surge frequency: f_n = (d/(2π·n_active·D²))·√(G/(2ρ)); warning when f_n/f_op < 13 Torsion body: D_w = D·n_active/(n_active + φ/2π); L_w = d·(n_active + 1 + φ/2π)

3. Assumptions
  A1. The rate is constant between the working points, with no progressive or conical spring and no variable pitch. (Cold-coiled round wire up to 20 mm.)
  A2. The active coils are as entered, and end coils count only in the solid length and the pitch. (Shigley 10th ed. Ch. 10, geometry of the ends.)
  A3. Hook flexibility is not in the rate, and leg flexibility is in the rate only for torsion springs. (Shigley 10th ed. Ch. 10, geometry of the ends.)
  A4. The EN 13906-1 solid length uses the maximum wire diameter d_max and adds 1.5 d for an unground squared end. (EN 13906-1 relations written in own notation, to be checked against the owner's copy, and Shigley 10th ed. Table 10-1.)
  A5. The EN 13906-1 form checks the minimum coil clearance S_a at the working length L₂. (EN 13906-1 relation written in own notation, to be checked against the owner's copy.)
  A6. Plain ends have no EN row and keep the geometric form, and the Shigley form (nominal d) is selectable. (Shigley 10th ed. Table 10-1.)
  A7. The default bending stress factor of a torsion spring is the EN 13906-3 q = (C + 0.07)/(C − 0.75). (EN 13906-3 relation written in own notation, to be checked against the owner's copy.)
  A8. Shigley's K_i = (4C² − C − 1)/(4C(C − 1)) is selectable and 1 % lower at C = 8. (Shigley 10th ed. Ch. 10, torsion springs.)
  A9. R_m(d) and the endurance strength are user inputs from the wire standard or certificate, with no default. (No wire-strength table or permissible-stress diagram is shipped.)

1. Scope and method
Calculates the bending moment, slope, deflection, fatigue and yield safety factors, critical speed and keys of a stepped shaft on two bearings. It takes radial loads in two planes and a steady torque with an optional alternating part.
Shaft bending moment: the shaft as an S2 beam (pin at A, roller at B) in each plane; M = √(M_y² + M_z²) Stress at a section: σ_a = K_f·32M_a·d/(π(d⁴ − d_i⁴)), τ_m = K_fs·16T_m·d/(π(d⁴ − d_i⁴)), τ_a = K_fs·16T_a·d/(π(d⁴ − d_i⁴)); K_f = 1 + q(K_t − 1) Endurance limit: S_e = k_a·k_b·k_c·k_d·k_e·k_f·S_e1, S_e1 (the rotating-beam limit S_e′) = 0.5·S_ut (S_ut ≤ 1400 MPa, else 700 MPa); k_a = a·S_ut^b, k_b = 1.24d^−0.107 or 1.51d^−0.157, k_e = 1 − 0.08·z_R Fatigue (DE-Goodman, bending fully reversed, torque T_m ± T_a): 1/n_f = √(σ_a² + 3τ_a²)/S_e + √3·τ_m/S_ut; yield n_y = S_y/√((σ_a)² + 3τ_pk²), τ_pk from the larger of T_m + T_a and the peak torque T_pk when given Critical speed: Rayleigh ω = √(g·Σm·y/Σm·y²), y signed (an overhung mass lifts the span, which lowers the quotient); Dunkerley 1/ω² = Σ m_i·δ_ii (lower bound, used for the ratio to the operating speed) Weight loads (with w_masses): F_y,i = −m_i·g at each node, m_i the carried mass plus the shaft's own mass split to the node; without it the loads table is the whole load Parallel key, on the peak torque T_k = the larger of T_m + T_a and T_pk: τ = 2T_k/(d·w·L), σ = 4T_k/(d·h·L); required length L = max(2T_k·n_target/(d·w·0.577·S_y,key), 4T_k·n_target/(d·h·S_y,min)), S_y,min the lowest of the key, the shaft and (when entered) the hub; a shoulder is checked on both sides and the lower n_f governs

3. Assumptions
  A1. The shaft is linear elastic on two simple bearing supports, a pin at A and a roller at B. (Shigley 10th ed. §7-4.)
  A2. Bending is fully reversed by rotation. (Shigley 10th ed. §7-4.)
  A3. The torque is a steady part T_m plus an alternating part T_a, acting between the torque-in and torque-out positions. (The torque is fully alternating when T_m = 0.)
  A4. The critical speed is the first lateral mode, by Rayleigh with Dunkerley as a lower bound. (Shigley 10th ed. §7-6.)
  A5. K_t and K_ts come from user records or published fit equations. (No stress-concentration chart is shipped, so the factors are entered or derived.)
  A6. At a shoulder both sides are checked, each on its own diameter and bore, and the lower factor governs. (Shigley 10th ed. §6-9, §7-4.)
  A7. The load factor k_c is 1, because the stresses are combined by von Mises. (Shigley 10th ed. §6-9, §7-4.)
  A8. The loads table is the whole load unless w_masses is on, which adds the weights. (With w_masses on, the sheet adds m·g at the nodes in the vertical plane.)

1. Scope and method
Calculates the basic and modified rating life of a rolling bearing under a steady load or a duty cycle, with the static safety factor, against required values. It takes ratings, load factors and a_ISO from reference records.
Equivalent load: F_dyn = X_1·F_r + Y_1·F_a when F_a/F_r ≤ e_lim (X_1 = 1, Y_1 = 0 for a single-row radial ball bearing), else F_dyn = X·F_r + Y·F_a (Y = 0 is refused when an axial load acts above e_lim) Duty cycle: F_dyn = (Σ F_dyn,i^p·U_i / Σ U_i)^(1/p) Basic rating life: L_10 = (C/F_dyn)^p million revolutions, p = 3 ball, 10/3 roller Life in hours: L_10h = 10⁶·L_10 / (60·n) Reliability factor (ISO 281:2007, derived): a_1 = 0.95·[ln(100/R_rel)/ln(100/90)]^(2/3) + 0.05 Modified life: L_nm = a_1·a_ISO·L_10 Static safety: P_0 = max(X_0·F_r + Y_0·F_a, F_r), s_0 = C_0/P_0

1. Scope and method
Calculates the torque, speed and power a motor needs to move a load through a mechanism, and checks a candidate motor against them. A continuously running rotary load is sized from its steady torque, speed and run-up time.
Linear mechanism: k = p/(2π i) (screw), D/(2 i) (belt), D_p/(2 i) (rack), in metres per motor radian; J_refl = m k²/η; T_load = F k/η, with F = m g (sin θ + μ cos θ) + F_p Rotary mechanism through a gearbox: J_refl = J_L/(i² η); T_load = T_L/(i η) Move profile: trapezoidal v_pk = s/(t_move − t_acc), a = v_pk/t_acc; S-curve (sine acceleration, same peak speed) a_pk = (π/2) a_trap Peak torque: T_acc = J_total α; T_pk = T_acc + T_load; T_dec = −T_acc + T_load; the check is SF·max(|T_pk|, |T_dec|) ≤ T_peak; braking power P_brake = max(0, −T_dec·ω_pk) Thermal duty: T_rms = √(Σ T_i² t_i / t_cycle) over the whole cycle, the dwell at T_hold = m g sin θ·k for a linear mechanism not held by a brake; P_rms = ω_pk √{[t_acc·(2/3)(T_load² + T_acc²) + T_load² t_const]/t_cycle}, and for the S-curve the acceleration term is t_acc·[(3/4) T_load² + (5/16) T_acc²] Output side and design torque: T_alt = (T_pk − T_dec)/2 (0 in continuous duty); T_out = T·i·η and n_out = n_pk/i for a rotary mechanism; F_ax_pk = F_load + m a_pk for a ball screw; T_design on the basis T_basis (load peak max(|T_pk|, |T_dec|), T_peak, SF_c·T_rated or T_lim) Continuous rotary duty: ω = 2π n_L i/60, n_pk = n_L i; T_load = T_L/(i η), T_rms = T_load; T_pk = J_total ω/t_start + T_load; P_cont = T_load ω, P_rms = P_cont Rated power of the candidate motor: P_rated = T_rated·2π n_rated/60 (without n_rated there is no P_rated); the check is P_cont ≤ P_rated, u_power = P_cont/P_rated
