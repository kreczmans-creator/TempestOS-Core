Title: Pipe expansion, loops and anchor forces (screening)
Author: unknown    Authored: 2026-10-27
Checker: J. Reviewer    Checked: 2026-10-27
Issued: 2026-10-27    Issue: PIP-SUP-001    Client: Client Co

1. Scope and method
Calculates the thermal growth of a pipe run between anchors, the leg or loop that absorbs it, and the force and moment on the anchors. It is a screening calculation, not a piping stress analysis.
Pipe section: d_i = OD − 2·t; A_m = π/4·(OD² − d_i²); I = π/64·(OD⁴ − d_i⁴); Z = I/(OD/2) Temperature change and growth: ΔT = T_operate − T_install, or as entered; L_tot = L, or L + L2 for a Z-bend; ΔL = α·L_tot·|ΔT|, its sign giving the direction Fully restrained pipe: σ_r = E·α·|ΔT| (E at the installation temperature); F_r = E_r·α·|ΔT|·A_m (E_r = E_hot, else E) Guided cantilever, required leg: Δ = ΔL for an offset leg or Z-bend and ΔL/2 for a U-loop; L_req = √(3·E·OD·Δ/Sa); PASS when L_req ≤ the available leg H Force and moment of the available leg: F_leg = 12·E_r·I·Δ/H³ (along the run); M_leg = 6·E_r·I·Δ/H² (= F_leg·H/2); σ_leg = 3·E·OD·Δ/H² = Sa·(L_req/H)² Runs of an offset leg or Z-bend: the runs bend for the leg's growth Δ_leg = α·|ΔT|·H under one force F_run = 12·E_r·I·Δ_leg/(L³ + L2³) along the leg (L2 = 0 for an L-bend); run i takes L_i³/(L³ + L2³) of Δ_leg, with σ_i = 3·E·OD·Δ_i/L_i² and M_i = F_run·L_i/2, and passes when √(σ_i/Sa) ≤ 1 Force on the anchors: √(F_leg² + F_run²) from the two perpendicular components; the moment reported is the largest root moment of the legs and runs Screening and bellows: OD·Y/(L_dev − U)² ≤ K1 with Y = α·|ΔT|·U (offset leg or Z-bend L_dev = L_tot + H, U = √(L_tot² + H²); U-loop L_dev = L + 2·H, U = L); axial bellows anchor force = P·A_eff + k·ΔL, with no leg check and no screening

3. Assumptions
  A1. The result is a screen, not a piping stress analysis. (Stated scope.)
  A2. The result tells whether a simple leg or loop is plausible and what it puts on its anchors. (Stated scope.)
  A3. It does not replace a flexibility analysis of the real routing, including elbow flexibility, intensification, torsion, branches, supports and loads. (Stated scope.)
  A4. A guided cantilever is straight, uniform, fixed against rotation at both ends and loaded only by the movement it absorbs. (Beam theory.)
  A5. Its stiffness is 12·E·I/H³ and its end moment is F·H/2. (Beam theory.)
  A6. Elbows are taken as rigid and the leg as slender. (Beam theory.)
  A7. The beam model is not valid for legs shorter than about ten diameters. (Beam theory.)
  A8. No stress-intensification factor and no elbow flexibility are applied. (Stated limit.)
  A9. The stress at the elbow root of a real bend is higher than the beam stress M/Z. (Stated limit.)
  A10. A leg that passes here can still exceed Sa at the elbow, which is why the result is a screen. (Stated limit.)
  A11. A U-loop is two identical legs in series, each a guided cantilever taking half the growth. (Loop model.)
  A12. The cross leg of a U-loop is a rigid guide. (Loop model.)
  A13. The force along the line is the same in both legs of a U-loop. (Loop model.)
  A14. The moment M is the moment at the leg roots, the corners of the loop. (Loop model.)
  A15. It reaches an anchor only where the leg ends at the anchor, as in an offset leg. (Loop model.)
  A16. The growth of the loop's own legs, which the cross leg has to absorb, is not checked. (Loop model.)
  A17. The run of an offset leg bends for the leg's growth as a second guided cantilever, checked against Sa. (Offset-leg and Z-bend model.)
  A18. In a Z-bend the two runs are in series under one force. (Offset-leg and Z-bend model.)
  A19. Each run takes its share of the leg's growth in proportion to L_i³ and is checked as its own cantilever. (Offset-leg and Z-bend model.)
  A20. The leg of a Z-bend takes the growth of both runs. (Offset-leg and Z-bend model.)
  A21. The force on the anchors is the resultant of the two perpendicular forces along the run and along the leg. (Offset-leg and Z-bend model.)
  A22. anchor_moment is the largest root moment of the legs and runs, M = F·L/2 with each end fixed against rotation. (Stated limit.)
  A23. It is not the moment on a frame anchor, which depends on the whole frame. (Stated limit.)
  A24. A formal analysis is needed before an anchor is sized for moment. (Stated limit.)
  A25. α is the mean coefficient of expansion between the install and operating temperatures. (Material library.)
  A26. E is the modulus at the installation temperature, used for the stress check as the piping codes do. (The ASME process piping code, cl. 319.3.2 and 319.5 (numbers to be checked against the owner's copy).)
  A27. E_hot, when given, is used only for the reactions on the anchors. (Code convention to be checked.)
  A28. The material properties are taken from a library material applied at the right temperature. (Material library.)
  A29. Sa and K1 are the owner's records from the owner's own copy of the piping code or the design basis. (No table from a standard is shipped.)
  A30. No allowable stress, K1 or code constant ships. (No table from a standard is shipped.)
  A31. The sheet applies the relation to the numbers entered, whatever code or load case they come from. (No table from a standard is shipped.)
  A32. No weight, spring-hanger or cold-spring effect is included in the leg force. (Stated limit.)
  A33. The anchor force adds support friction μ·w·L only when μ and the weight per metre are entered. (Stated limit; a warning says so when they are not.)
  A34. The bellows anchor force is the pressure thrust P·A_eff plus the spring force k·ΔL at the governing pressure. (Stated limit.)
  A35. The governing pressure is the design or test pressure, whichever governs, because compression and extension ratings can differ. (Stated limit.)
  A36. Guide friction and any other bellows load come from the manufacturer's data. (Stated limit.)
  A37. Layout: the offset leg of 9.5 m takes the whole movement of 100.8 mm as a guided cantilever fixed against rotation at both ends, and the run of 60 m bends for the leg's growth of 15.96 mm. Screening only — not a piping stress analysis. (Guided-cantilever model.)

1. Scope and method
Calculates reactions, member forces and deflections of a linear elastic plane frame or continuous beam by the direct stiffness method. Global x is right and y up; bending is about the section y-y axis, anticlockwise positive.
Member stiffness: three freedoms per node, Euler–Bernoulli members with axial EA/L and bending EI/L³ terms. End releases: pins are taken out by static condensation; supports remove freedoms or add spring stiffness. Span loads: they become equivalent nodal loads, the exact fixed-end forces. Nodal displacements: K u = F, solved by banded LDLᵀ after a reverse Cuthill–McKee ordering; a non-positive pivot is reported as a mechanism. Internal forces: they follow from the member end actions and the Macaulay functions of the span loads. Member extremes: found at stations along each member, plus the zero-shear and zero-slope points found by bisection.

3. Assumptions
  A1. The analysis is linear elastic and first order, with small displacements. (Euler–Bernoulli direct stiffness.)
  A2. Members have no shear deformation, and there are no P–Δ effects or buckling. (Euler–Bernoulli direct stiffness.)
  A3. Members are prismatic between nodes and connect rigidly unless a release is stated. (Static condensation of the released rotation.)
  A4. A released member end transmits no moment. (Static condensation of the released rotation.)
  A5. Distributed loads act perpendicular to the member unless marked as gravity loads. (Load table convention.)
  A6. Gravity loads act in global −y per metre of member length. (Load table convention.)
  A7. n_div (Stations per member) is taken as 20. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)

1. Scope and method
Calculates the bearing, bolt tension, T-stub and shear utilisations of a base plate under an I/H, RHS or CHS column on grout, and the plate thickness that satisfies them. The anchorage is designed outside this sheet.
Bearing (cl. 6.2.5): f_cd = α_cc·f_ck/γ_c; f_jd = β_j·α·f_cd, β_j = 2/3; c = t_p·√[f_y/(3·f_jd·γ_M0)] Axial (cl. 6.2.8.2): N_j,Rd = f_jd·A_eff, A_eff the bearing strips of width c round the column footprint (full rectangle when they overlap); u_N = N_c,Ed/N_j,Rd Moment (cl. 6.2.8.3, Table 6.7): z_C from the section ((h − t_f)/2, (h − t)/2, half-annulus centroid) or entered, x_b the bolt row from the axis, z = z_C + x_b; C_Ed = (M_Ed + N_Ed·x_b)/z and T_Ed = C_Ed − N_Ed when both are ≥ 0; with N_Ed > 0 and T_Ed < 0 both sides are in compression, C = N_Ed/2 ± M_Ed/(2z_C); with N_Ed < 0 and C_Ed < 0 both rows are in tension, T = −N_Ed/2 ± M_Ed/(2x_b); F_t,Ed = T_Ed/n_t at the more loaded row Plate T-stub (cl. 6.2.6.11, 6.2.4, Table 6.2): M_pl,Rd = 0.25·l_eff·t_p²·f_y/γ_M0; without prying min(2·M_pl,Rd/m, n_t·F_t,Rd); with prying the modes 4·M_pl,Rd/m, (2·M_pl,Rd + n·ΣF_t,Rd)/(m + n) with n = min(e_min, 1.25m), and n_t·F_t,Rd; l_eff is the total for the n_t bolts Compression T-stub (cl. 6.2.6.9, 6.2.6.10): C_Rd = f_jd·A_flange, checked when M_Ed > 0 and the resultant is in compression Shear (cl. 6.2.2): F_2,vb,Rd = α_bc·f_ub·A_s/γ_M2, α_bc = 0.44 − 0.0003·f_yb; F_vb,Rd = min(F_1,vb,Rd, F_2,vb,Rd); V_Rd = C_f,d·N_c,Ed + n_b·F_vb,Rd; V_b,Ed = max(V_Ed − C_f,d·N_c,Ed, 0)/n_b Required thickness: the smallest t_p in 10–100 mm with u_N, u_C and u_T all ≤ 1, z_C, C_Ed and T_Ed re-evaluated at every trial t_p

3. Assumptions
  A1. The axial force is positive in compression, the opposite of the member check sheet. (Sign convention.)
  A2. A link from the frame sheet takes the reaction R1_y, never the member force N_e1. (R1_y is up positive, so compression into the base.)
  A3. Loading is static, with a major-axis moment only. (Scope of this sheet.)
  A4. The plate is unstiffened and rectangular, on grout adequate for β_j = 2/3. (The grout is checked when its strength and thickness are entered.)
  A5. The simplified T-stub model has one compression resultant and one tension resultant per bolt row. (BS EN 1993-1-8:2005 cl. 6.2.8.3.)
  A6. The compression resultant acts at the flange centre, or at the half-annulus centroid of a CHS. (BS EN 1993-1-8:2005 cl. 6.2.8.3.)
  A7. The force regime follows Table 6.7, and the web is not credited in the moment check. (BS EN 1993-1-8:2005 cl. 6.2.8.3.)
  A8. The effective length l_eff is the total for the n_t bolts of a row. (Read from the cl. 6.2.6.5 patterns.)
  A9. One l_eff serves modes 1 and 2, which is conservative for mode 2. (l_eff,1 is not more than l_eff,2.)
  A10. The concrete, pull-out, breakout, edge and interaction failures of the anchors are designed in Hilti PROFIS. (From F_t,Ed and V_b,Ed per anchor; the report is filed as Evidence and referenced.)
  A11. With prying switched on, the prying force Q is not in F_t,Ed and PROFIS must add it. (External anchorage design.)
  A12. The friction resistance counts only the compression, C_f,d·max(N_Ed, 0). (BS EN 1993-1-8:2005 cl. 6.2.2.)
  A13. A column in net tension has no friction resistance. (BS EN 1993-1-8:2005 cl. 6.2.2.)
  A14. Anchor bolts share the shear equally. (BS EN 1993-1-8:2005 cl. 6.2.2.)
  A15. Anchor bolt holes are no larger than the standard clearance. (Oversized or slotted holes are outside this sheet.)
  A16. The bearing outstand c is cut back where it would pass the plate edge. (Conservative against an uncut c.)
  A17. The web and the column interior keep the full outstand c. (Conservative against an uncut c.)
  A18. The CHS compression side is half the annular bearing area, with its centroid as lever arm. (An approximation.)
  A19. The compression T-stub is checked against the flange area alone when M_Ed is above 0. (Conservative limitation.)
  A20. The whole footprint resists at M_Ed = 0, so the compression check jumps just above 0, conservatively. (Limitation.)
  A21. Partial factors and the friction coefficient come from the records in use. (γ_c and α_cc: EN 1992-1-1 NA; γ_M2: EN 1993-1-8 NA; γ_M0: EN 1993-1-1 NA; C_f,d: the grout used.)
  A22. The defaults are the EN recommended and UK values. (A small set of factors only; no table from a standard is shipped.)
  A23. prying (Prying forces develop (short bolts, L_b ≤ L_b*); off is conservative for the plate) is taken as no. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A24. n_t (Holding-down bolts in each bolt row (one row each side)) is taken as 2. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A25. n_b (Holding-down bolts in total (both sides)) is taken as 4. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A26. t_wall (Wall thickness (RHS and CHS)) is taken as 10 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A27. D (Outside diameter (CHS)) is taken as 220 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A28. f_y (Plate yield strength) is taken as 275 MPa. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A29. f_ck (Concrete characteristic cylinder strength) is taken as 25 MPa. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A30. alpha_cc (Coefficient for long-term effects on the concrete strength) is taken as 0.85. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A31. gamma_c (Partial factor for concrete) is taken as 1.5. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A32. gamma_M0 (Partial factor for the plate) is taken as 1. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A33. gamma_M2 (Partial factor for the anchor bolts) is taken as 1.25. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A34. C_fd (Friction coefficient, plate to grout) is taken as 0.2. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A35. The outstand c is cut back to the plate edge where it would pass it, so no area outside the plate is counted. (Conservative against an uncut c.)
  A36. β_j = 2/3 assumes the grout is at least 0.2·f_ck and no thicker than 0.2 × the smaller plate dimension; enter the grout strength and thickness to check it. (EN 1993-1-8 cl. 6.2.5.)
