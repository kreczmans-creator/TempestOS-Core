1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Screening only — not a piping stress analysis. Thermal growth of a straight run between anchors, the fully restrained stress and force, the leg an offset leg or a U-loop (two guided cantilevers) needs to absorb the growth within the owner's allowable displacement-stress range Sa and whether the available leg is long enough (an offset leg or Z-bend also loads its run or runs with the leg's own growth), the force on the anchors and the largest root moment of the guided-cantilever model, the screening ratio D·Y/(L − U)² against the owner's K1 (PASS, or the line needs a formal stress analysis), and, for an axial bellows, the anchor force P·A_eff plus its spring force. No allowable stress, K1 or code table ships.
Pipe section: d_i = OD − 2·t; A_m = π/4·(OD² − d_i²); I = π/64·(OD⁴ − d_i⁴); Z = I/(OD/2) Temperature change: ΔT = T_operate − T_install, or as entered; the movement uses |ΔT| and its sign gives the direction Run that grows: L_tot = L, or L + L2 for a Z-bend; thermal growth ΔL = α·L_tot·|ΔT| Fully restrained pipe: σ_r = E·α·|ΔT| (E at the installation temperature); F_r = E_r·α·|ΔT|·A_m (E_r = E_hot, else E) Movement one leg must absorb: Δ = ΔL for an offset leg or Z-bend; Δ = ΔL/2 for a U-loop (two identical legs in series, the cross leg a rigid guide) Guided cantilever, required leg: L_req = √(3·E·OD·Δ/Sa), the leg at which the bending stress M/Z reaches Sa; PASS when L_req ≤ the available leg H Force and moment of the available leg: F_leg = 12·E_r·I·Δ/H³ (acts along the run); M_leg = 6·E_r·I·Δ/H² (= F_leg·H/2); σ_leg = 3·E·OD·Δ/H² = Sa·(L_req/H)² Offset leg or Z-bend, the runs bend for the leg's growth Δ_leg = α·|ΔT|·H: the runs act in series under one force F_run = 12·E_r·I·Δ_leg/(L³ + L2³) (L2 = 0 for an L-bend) acting along the leg; run i takes the share L_i³/(L³ + L2³) of Δ_leg, with root stress σ_i = 3·E·OD·Δ_i/L_i² and moment M_i = F_run·L_i/2; each run is checked as its own cantilever, PASS when √(σ_i/Sa) ≤ 1 (for an L-bend the run needed is L_req,run = √(3·E·OD·Δ_leg/Sa)) Force on the anchors: the two components are perpendicular, F_leg along the run and F_run along the leg, so the force is √(F_leg² + F_run²); the moment reported is the largest root moment of the legs and runs in the guided-cantilever model Flexibility screening: OD·Y/(L_dev − U)² ≤ K1 with Y = α·|ΔT|·U, the free growth between the anchors; offset leg or Z-bend L_dev = L_tot + H, U = √(L_tot² + H²); U-loop L_dev = L + 2·H, U = L (so Y = ΔL); PASS, or the line needs a formal stress analysis Axial bellows (no leg or loop, no leg check, no screening): anchor force = P·A_eff + k·ΔL; the movement is checked against the bellows' rated axial movement when given
  Ref: ASME B31.3 para. 319.4.1 (screening relation; number to be checked against the owner's copy) — Process piping — flexibility analysis, exemption from formal analysis
  Ref: ASME B31.3 para. 319.3.2 and 319.5 (reference and hot moduli; numbers to be checked against the owner's copy) — Process piping — displacement stress range and reactions
  Ref: EN 13480-3 cl. 12 (number to be checked against the owner's copy) — Metallic industrial piping — flexibility analysis

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Screening only — not a piping stress analysis. The result tells whether a simple leg or loop is plausible and what it puts on its anchors; it does not replace a flexibility analysis of the real routing (elbow flexibility, intensification, torsion, branches, restraint friction, supports, sustained and occasional loads). (Stated scope.)
  A2. A guided cantilever: the available leg is straight, uniform, fixed against rotation at both ends and loaded only by the movement it absorbs (stiffness 12·E·I/H³, end moment F·H/2). Elbows are taken as rigid and the leg as slender: the beam model is not valid for legs shorter than about ten diameters. (Beam theory.)
  A3. No stress-intensification factor and no elbow flexibility are applied: the stress at the elbow root of a real bend is higher than the beam stress M/Z, by the intensification factor of the code, so a leg that passes here can still exceed Sa at the elbow. This is the main reason the result is a screen and not a stress analysis. (Stated limit.)
  A4. A U-loop is two identical legs in series, each a guided cantilever taking half the growth, the cross leg a rigid guide; the force along the line is the same F in both legs. The moment M is the moment at the leg roots (the corners of the loop); it reaches an anchor only where the leg ends at the anchor (an offset leg). The growth of the loop's own legs, which the cross leg has to absorb, is not checked. (Loop model.)
  A5. An offset leg is anchored at both ends: the run L bends for the growth of the leg (α·|ΔT|·H) as a second guided cantilever and is checked against the same Sa. A Z-bend (second run L2) has the two runs in series under one force, each taking its share of the leg's growth in proportion to its flexibility, L_i³, and each checked as its own cantilever; the leg takes the growth of both runs. The force on the anchors is the resultant of the force along the run (the leg's) and the force along the leg (the runs'), which are perpendicular. (Offset-leg and Z-bend model.)
  A6. anchor_moment is the largest root moment of the legs and runs in the guided-cantilever simplification (M = F·L/2 with each end fixed against rotation). It is not the moment on a frame anchor, which depends on the whole frame (the stiffness of the other legs, the elbow rotations, the anchor's own fixity); a formal analysis is needed before an anchor is sized for moment. (Stated limit.)
  A7. The material properties are single values: α the mean coefficient between the install and operating temperatures; E the modulus at the installation (reference) temperature, used for the stress check, as the piping codes do (ASME B31.3 para. 319.3.2 and 319.5, numbers to be checked against the owner's copy); E_hot, when given, is used only for the reactions on the anchors. Take them from a W3 material applied at the right temperature. (Material library; code convention to be checked.)
  A8. Sa and K1 are the owner's records from the owner's own copy of the piping code (or the design basis), with their provenance; no allowable stress, K1 or code constant ships. The sheet applies the relation to the numbers entered and does not know which code, material or load case they belong to. (No table from a standard is shipped.)
  A9. No friction, weight, spring-hanger or cold-spring effect is included in the leg force; the anchor force adds the friction of the sliding supports, μ·w·L, only when μ and the weight per metre are entered (a warning says so when they are not). The axial bellows anchor force is the pressure thrust P·A_eff plus the spring force k·ΔL at the governing pressure entered (the design or the test pressure, whichever governs; a bellows' compression and extension ratings can differ): guide friction and any other bellows load come from the manufacturer's data. (Stated limit.)
  A10. Layout: the offset leg of 9.5 m takes the whole movement of 100.8 mm as a guided cantilever fixed against rotation at both ends, and the run of 60 m bends for the leg's growth of 15.96 mm. Screening only — not a piping stress analysis. (Guided-cantilever model.)

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Linear elastic analysis of a plane frame or continuous beam by the direct stiffness method: reactions, member end forces, axial force, shear, bending moment and deflection along every member with their extremes and positions, for each load combination and as an envelope. Global x to the right, y up; moments and couples anticlockwise positive; sagging moments positive along a member from node i to node j.
Member stiffness, three freedoms per node, Euler–Bernoulli members: axial EA/L and bending EI/L³ terms. End releases (pins) are taken out by static condensation; supports remove freedoms or add spring stiffness. Span loads become equivalent nodal loads, the exact fixed-end forces. Displacements, by banded LDLᵀ after a reverse Cuthill–McKee ordering (a non-positive pivot is reported as a mechanism): K u = F. Internal forces and deflections follow from the member end actions and Macaulay functions of the span loads. Extremes are found at stations along each member, plus the zero-shear and zero-slope points found by bisection.
  Ref: Hibbeler, Structural Analysis Ch. 16 — Plane frame analysis using the stiffness method
  Ref: Roark's Formulas for Stress and Strain Ch. 8, Table 8.1 — Shear, moment, slope and deflection formulas for elastic straight beams
  Ref: BS EN 1990 6.4.3 — Combinations of actions (user-entered factors)

  A3. Distributed loads act perpendicular to the member unless marked as gravity loads, which act in global −y per metre of member length. (Load table convention.)
  A4. n_div (Stations per member) is taken as 20. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
An unstiffened rectangular base plate under an I/H, RHS or CHS column on grouted concrete: bearing resistance, the force regime and holding-down bolt tension for a major-axis moment, the tension T-stub of the plate and the compression T-stub, shear by friction and anchor bolts, and the plate thickness that satisfies them together. The bolt tension and shear per anchor go to Hilti PROFIS for the anchorage, which is done outside this sheet.
Bearing (cl. 6.2.5): f_cd = α_cc·f_ck/γ_c; f_jd = β_j·α·f_cd, β_j = 2/3; c = t_p·√[f_y/(3·f_jd·γ_M0)] Axial (cl. 6.2.8.2): N_j,Rd = f_jd·A_eff, A_eff the bearing strips of width c round the column footprint (full rectangle when they overlap); u_N = N_c,Ed/N_j,Rd Moment (cl. 6.2.8.3, Table 6.7): z_C from the section ((h − t_f)/2, (h − t)/2, half-annulus centroid) or entered, x_b the bolt row from the axis, z = z_C + x_b; C_Ed = (M_Ed + N_Ed·x_b)/z and T_Ed = C_Ed − N_Ed when both are ≥ 0; with N_Ed > 0 and T_Ed < 0 both sides are in compression, C = N_Ed/2 ± M_Ed/(2z_C); with N_Ed < 0 and C_Ed < 0 both rows are in tension, T = −N_Ed/2 ± M_Ed/(2x_b); F_t,Ed = T_Ed/n_t at the more loaded row Plate T-stub (cl. 6.2.6.11, 6.2.4, Table 6.2): M_pl,Rd = 0.25·l_eff·t_p²·f_y/γ_M0; without prying min(2·M_pl,Rd/m, n_t·F_t,Rd); with prying the modes 4·M_pl,Rd/m, (2·M_pl,Rd + n·ΣF_t,Rd)/(m + n) with n = min(e_min, 1.25m), and n_t·F_t,Rd; l_eff is the total for the n_t bolts Compression T-stub (cl. 6.2.6.9, 6.2.6.10): C_Rd = f_jd·A_flange, checked when M_Ed > 0 and the resultant is in compression Shear (cl. 6.2.2): F_2,vb,Rd = α_bc·f_ub·A_s/γ_M2, α_bc = 0.44 − 0.0003·f_yb; F_vb,Rd = min(F_1,vb,Rd, F_2,vb,Rd); V_Rd = C_f,d·N_c,Ed + n_b·F_vb,Rd; V_b,Ed = max(V_Ed − C_f,d·N_c,Ed, 0)/n_b Required thickness: the smallest t_p in 10–100 mm with u_N, u_C and u_T all ≤ 1, z_C, C_Ed and T_Ed re-evaluated at every trial t_p

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. N_Ed is positive in compression here, the opposite of S3 (tension positive): a link from S2 must take the reaction R1_y (up positive, compression into the base), never the member force N_e1. (Sign convention.)
  A2. Static loading; a major-axis moment only (uniaxial); an unstiffened rectangular plate on grout of adequate strength and thickness for β_j = 2/3 (checked when the grout strength and thickness are entered). (Scope of S6.)
  A3. The simplified T-stub model: one compression resultant at the centre of the compression T-stub (flange centre, or the half-annulus centroid of a CHS) and one tension resultant per bolt row; the force regime follows EN 1993-1-8 Table 6.7. The web is not given credit in the moment check. (EN 1993-1-8 cl. 6.2.8.3.)
  A4. The effective length l_eff is the total for the n_t bolts of a row, read from the cl. 6.2.6.5 patterns; it is used for modes 1 and 2 alike (l_eff,1 ≤ l_eff,2, so this is conservative for mode 2). (One input.)
  A5. Concrete, anchor tension, pull-out, breakout, edge and the tension–shear interaction of the anchors are designed in Hilti PROFIS from F_t,Ed and V_b,Ed per anchor; the report is filed as Evidence and referenced. With prying switched on the prying force Q is not in F_t,Ed and PROFIS must add it. (External anchorage design.)
  A6. The friction resistance counts only the compression, C_f,d·max(N_Ed, 0); a column in net tension has none. Anchor bolts share shear equally, with their holes assumed no larger than the standard clearance (oversized or slotted holes are outside this sheet). (EN 1993-1-8 cl. 6.2.2.)
  A7. The bearing outstand c is cut back where it would pass the plate edge; the web and the column interior keep the full c. The CHS compression side is half of the annular bearing area, with its centroid as lever arm (an approximation). (Conservative against an uncut c.)
  A8. The compression T-stub is checked against the flange area alone as soon as M_Ed > 0, while at M_Ed = 0 the whole footprint (web included) resists, so the compression check jumps at M_Ed = 0⁺ (conservative). (Limitation.)
  A9. Partial factors and the friction coefficient come from the records in use (γ_c, α_cc: EN 1992-1-1 NA; γ_M2: EN 1993-1-8 NA; γ_M0: EN 1993-1-1 NA; C_f,d: the grout used); the values shown default to the EN recommended and UK values.
