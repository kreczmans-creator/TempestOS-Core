1. Scope and method
Calculates the state of moist air from dry-bulb and one other property at a stated pressure or altitude. It then applies one air-handling process with its heat and moisture balance: sensible heating or cooling, dehumidification, humidification or mixing.
Saturation and humidity: p_ws(T) by Hyland–Wexler over water at or above 0 °C and over ice below; W = 0.621945 p_w/(p − p_w); φ = p_w/p_ws Enthalpy and volume per kilogram of dry air: h = 1.006 T + W(2501 + 1.86 T) kJ/kg; v = 0.287042 (T + 273.15)(1 + 1.607858 W)/p Dew point and wet-bulb temperatures, by bisection: p_ws(T_dp) = p_w; the wet-bulb from the adiabatic-saturation relation Sensible heating or cooling at constant moisture content: Phi = ṁ (h₂ − h₁) Cooling with dehumidification, less the heat leaving in the condensate: Q_total = ṁ(h₁ − h₂) − ṁ_w h_w, with ṁ_w = ṁ(W₁ − W₂) Sensible and latent split of the coil load, along T₁ → T₂ at W₁ then W₁ → W₂ at T₂: Q_sensible = ṁ(h₁ − h(T₂, W₁)); Q_latent = ṁ(h(T₂, W₁) − h₂); SHR = Q_sensible/(Q_sensible + Q_latent); with RH_2 blank and T₂ above the entering dew point the coil is dry, W₂ = W₁ Steam and adiabatic humidification: h₂ = h₁ + (W₂ − W₁) h_steam for steam; h₂ = h₁ for adiabatic (spray) humidification Mixing two airstreams, weighted by dry-air mass flow: W_mix = Σṁ W/Σṁ; h_mix = Σṁ h/Σṁ

3. Assumptions
  A1. Moist air is an ideal mixture of ideal gases. (ASHRAE Fundamentals Ch. 1 ideal-gas relations.)
  A2. The enhancement factor is taken as 1. (ASHRAE Fundamentals Ch. 1 ideal-gas relations.)
  A3. Condensate leaves at the coil leaving dry-bulb. (Process conventions.)
  A4. Steam adds the enthalpy h_steam at negligible mass of dry air. (Process conventions.)
  A5. Adiabatic humidification follows a line of constant enthalpy, which is close to constant wet-bulb. (Evaporative process idealisation.)
  A6. m_da (Dry-air mass flow) is taken as 1 kg/s. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A7. h_steam (Steam enthalpy (steam humidification)) is taken as 2676. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)

4. Working
  [2] p_atm = 101.325 (1 − 2.25577e-5 z)^5.2559    « ASHRAE Ch. 1 eq. 3
      = z = 0 m
      = 101.32 kPa
        Barometric pressure (standard atmosphere)
  [6] p_ws_1 = p_ws(t) Hyland–Wexler (entering)
      = t = 28 °C
      = 3.7822 kPa
        Saturation pressure, state 1
  [7] p_w_1 = p W/(0.621945 + W)
      = W = 0.014249
      = 2.2693 kPa
        Vapour pressure, state 1
  [8] T_1_out = dry-bulb (entering)
      = t = 28 °C
      = 28 degC
        Dry-bulb, state 1
  [9] W_1 = 0.621945 p_w/(p − p_w)
      = 2.2693 / (101.32 − 2.2693)
      = 14.249
        Humidity ratio, state 1 (g/kg dry air)
  [10] RH_1 = p_w / p_ws
      = 2.2693 / 3.7822
      = 60
        Relative humidity, state 1 (%)
  [11] phi_1 = p_w / p_ws
      = 2.2693 / 3.7822
      = 60 %
        Relative humidity, state 1, as a percentage quantity (the same value as RH_1; a plain-number output cannot be linked to a percentage input)
  [12] T_dp_1 = p_ws(t_dp) = p_w, bisection
      = p_w = 2.2693 kPa
      = 19.514 degC
        Dew point, state 1
  [13] T_wb_1 = adiabatic-saturation relation, bisection
      = t = 28, W = 0.014249
      = 22.074 degC
        Wet-bulb, state 1
  [14] h_1 = 1.006 t + W (2501 + 1.86 t)
      = 1.006 × 28 + 0.014249 × (2501 + 1.86 × 28)
      = 64.546
        Enthalpy, state 1 (kJ/kg dry air)
  [15] v_1 = 0.287042 (t + 273.15)(1 + 1.607858 W)/p
      = p = 101.32 kPa
      = 0.87267 m³/kg
        Specific volume, state 1 (per kg dry air)
  [16] rho_1 = (1 + W)/v
      = (1 + 0.014249) / 0.87267
      = 1.1622 kg/m³
        Moist-air density, state 1
