1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Friction and fitting losses through series pipe segments at a given flow, the static head, the system curve, the duty point against a pump curve entered as points, the affinity-law shift for a speed or impeller change, and the NPSH available at the pump suction.
Design flow from the heat carried, when a heat load is given in place of a flow: Q = Φ/(ρ c_p ΔT). Mean velocity in the pipe: v = Q/(πD²/4). Reynolds number, which decides laminar or turbulent flow: Re = ρvD/μ. Friction factor for laminar flow (Re < 2300): f = 64/Re. Friction factor for turbulent flow, Colebrook–White by fixed-point iteration (at most 100 steps) from the Swamee–Jain value, which also cross-checks it: 1/√f = −2 log10(ε/3.7D + 2.51/(Re√f)). Head lost in the pipe and its fittings: h_f = f (L + L_eq)/D · v²/2g + K v²/2g. System curve, with the friction of a turbulent or transitional segment and every fitting loss scaled from the design flow as Q², and the friction of a laminar segment (Re < 2300, h_f ∝ Q) as Q (a closed circuit has Σdz = 0, the rise in the flow being recovered in the return): H_sys(Q) = Σdz + h_out + Σh_f,lam·(Q/Q₀) + Σh_f,other·(Q/Q₀)². Water held in the pipes: V_pipe = Σ π/4·D²·L. Duty point, where the pump and system curves cross (every crossing is found by a scan and refined by bisection; the stable one, where the pump curve falls through the system curve, nearest the design flow is reported): H_pump(Q) = H_sys(Q). Pump affinity laws for a change of speed n: Q ∝ n; H ∝ n². For a change of impeller diameter D, by the choice: a trimmed impeller of the same casing, Q ∝ D and H ∝ D² (the default, the usual practice for a trim); or a geometrically similar pump scaled in all dimensions, Q ∝ D³ and H ∝ D². Speed and diameter combine as Q ∝ n·D^a, H ∝ n²·D², a = 1 or 3. Net positive suction head available, to compare with what the pump requires: NPSH_a = p_atm/ρg + z_s − h_f,suction − p_v/ρg, with p_v from the water correlation or, for a custom fluid, from the vapour pressure entered. The friction h_f,suction is that of the segments marked 'suction' only, at the larger of the design flow and the duty flow the pump runs at. Shaft torque for the motor: T = P/ω with P the larger of the design-flow shaft power and the duty-point shaft power, at the speed n_pump; when a speed or diameter ratio shifts the duty, the shifted duty power at the shifted speed n_pump·n₂/n₁.
  Ref: Crane Technical Paper 410 — Flow of fluids through valves, fittings and pipe
  Ref: Çengel & Cimbala, Fluid Mechanics Ch. 8 — Internal flow: friction factor, minor losses, pump duty
  Ref: CIBSE Guide C Section 4 — Flow of fluids in pipes and ducts (principles)
  Ref: Kell, J. Chem. Eng. Data 20(1), 1975 — Density of liquid water

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
The state of moist air (humidity ratio, relative humidity, dew point, wet-bulb, enthalpy, specific volume, density) from dry-bulb and one other property at a stated pressure or altitude, and one air-handling process from that state with its heat and moisture balance: sensible heating or cooling, cooling with dehumidification, steam humidification, adiabatic humidification, or mixing with a second stream.
Saturation vapour pressure at the dry-bulb temperature, over water at or above 0 °C and over ice below: p_ws(t) by Hyland–Wexler. Moisture content (humidity ratio) of the air: W = 0.621945 p_w/(p − p_w). Relative humidity: φ = p_w/p_ws. Specific enthalpy per kilogram of dry air: h = 1.006 t + W(2501 + 1.86 t) kJ/kg_da. Specific volume: v = 0.287042 (t + 273.15)(1 + 1.607858 W)/p. Dew point and wet-bulb temperatures, by bisection (wet-bulb from the adiabatic-saturation relation): p_ws(t_dp) = p_w. Sensible heating or cooling at constant moisture content: Q = ṁ (h₂ − h₁). Cooling with dehumidification, less the heat leaving in the condensate: Q_total = ṁ(h₁ − h₂) − ṁ_w h_w, with ṁ_w = ṁ(W₁ − W₂). Sensible and latent split of the coil load, along the path t₁ → t₂ at W₁ then W₁ → W₂ at t₂: Q_sensible = ṁ(h₁ − h(t₂, W₁)); Q_latent = ṁ(h(t₂, W₁) − h₂); SHR = Q_sensible/(Q_sensible + Q_latent). With RH_2 blank and t₂ above the entering dew point the coil is dry: W₂ = W₁, no condensate, sensible only. Steam humidification: h₂ = h₁ + (W₂ − W₁) h_steam. Adiabatic (spray) humidification, at constant enthalpy: h₂ = h₁. Mixing two airstreams, weighted by dry-air mass flow: W_mix = Σṁ W/Σṁ; h_mix = Σṁ h/Σṁ.

  Ref: Hyland & Wexler, ASHRAE Trans. 89(2A), 1983 — Saturation pressure of water and ice
  Ref: CIBSE Guide C Section 1 — Properties of humid air (principles)

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
The insulation thickness on a pipe (cylinder) or a flat surface that meets a heat-flow limit, a limit on the surface temperature of a hot surface and an anti-condensation requirement for a cold one, each solved separately and the governing one reported; and for a chosen thickness the heat flow, the surface temperature and PASS/FAIL per criterion. Steady state, one layer, no thickness table of any standard.
Pipe resistance per metre: R = ln(D_e/D_i)/(2π·λ) + 1/(h_se·π·D_e), D_e = D_i + 2t; with the inner film and pipe wall R_oth = 1/(h_i·π·D_p) + ln(D_i/D_p)/(2π·λ_w) added in series Flat resistance per square metre: R = t/λ + 1/h_se (+ 1/h_i + t_w/λ_w) Heat flow: q = (θ_i − θ_a)/R, positive from the process to the ambient Surface temperature: θ_se = θ_a + q/(h_se·π·D_e) (pipe), θ_a + q/h_se (flat) Surface coefficient: h_se as given, or h_se = h_c + h_r with h_r = ε·σ·(T_s² + T_a²)(T_s + T_a); h_c from free convection to air (laminar or turbulent, the larger), forced convection (cross-flow cylinder or flat plate) and their cube-law blend; solved with θ_se, which it depends on Heat-loss limit: the thickness beyond which |q| ≤ q_max; surface-temperature limit (hot): θ_se ≤ θ_se,max; anti-condensation (cold): θ_se ≥ θ_dew + margin, θ_dew entered (for example from B2) or from the Magnus relation at θ_a and RH Thickness solution: each criterion's thickness by scanning t from 0 to 1 m for the last broken point and bisecting the bracket after it; governing t = max over the selected criteria Critical diameter: D_crit = 2λ/h_se; below it, insulation raises the heat flow until D_e passes D_crit
  Ref: ISO 12241 formula route (clause numbers to be checked) — Thermal insulation for building equipment and industrial installations: calculation rules
  Ref: Incropera, Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer 7th ed., §7.2, §7.4, §9.6, §9.9 (to be checked) — External forced, free and mixed convection correlations

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Steady one-dimensional heat flow through a single homogeneous layer of constant conductivity; no thermal bridges at supports, no joints, no moisture in the insulation. λ is taken at the value entered: use the value at the mean insulation temperature shown on the sheet (optionally noted in 'Temperature the conductivity applies at'). (Formula route of the standard.)
  A2. With the correlation, h_se = h_c + h_r. Radiation: h_r = ε·σ·(T_s² + T_a²)(T_s + T_a) to surroundings at the ambient air temperature. Free convection to air: Holman's simplified relations at atmospheric pressure, the larger of the laminar and turbulent forms (horizontal cylinder 1.32(ΔT/D)^0.25 and 1.24ΔT^(1/3); vertical surface 1.42(ΔT/L)^0.25 and 1.31ΔT^(1/3)). Forced convection: Churchill–Bernstein for a cylinder in cross-flow, the laminar/mixed flat-plate relations along a plate; air properties from fits at the film temperature, Pr = 0.71; blended with free convection as (h_nat³ + h_forced³)^(1/3). Indicative, not the standard's own correlation. (Incropera 7th ed. §7.2, §7.4, §9.6 free convection and §9.9 mixed convection (section numbers to be checked against the owner's copy); the free-convection constants are Holman's, Heat Transfer; the standard's own coefficients are not reproduced.)
  A3. The dew point is entered (for example from B2) or is the Magnus relation at θ_a and RH, the same closed form B7 uses. Below 0 °C the relation is over ice, so RH is then taken relative to ice, whereas meteorological RH is usually relative to water: enter the dew point for sub-zero air. A margin above it is the user's. (Derived; B7 shares the function.)
  A4. Pipes are treated as horizontal for free convection and flat surfaces as vertical; air properties are fits (conductivity within about 3 %, viscosity within about 4 % over 250–400 K), Pr = 0.71. (Stated.)
  A5. The required thickness is the least thickness beyond which the criterion holds for every thicker layer up to 1 m, which is what matters when thin insulation on a small pipe raises the heat flow (critical diameter). (Stated.)
  A6. No thickness table is used or shipped: not BS 5422, not the tables of any standard. The conductivity is the user's input, from a W3 material or the maker's data. (No standard's table is reproduced.)
  A7. eps (Emissivity of the outer surface (for the correlation; with h_se as given it only judges that value against condensation on a cold surface)) is taken as 0.9. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A8. v_wind (Air speed over the surface (correlation; 0 = still air)) is taken as 0 m/s. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A9. L_surf (Height or flow length of the flat surface (correlation, flat)) is taken as 1 m. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
