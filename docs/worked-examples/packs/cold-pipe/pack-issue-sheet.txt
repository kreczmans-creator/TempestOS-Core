Title: Pipe pressure drop and pump duty
Author: unknown    Authored: 2026-10-27

1. Scope and method
Calculates the friction and fitting losses of series pipe segments at a given flow, the system curve and the duty point against a pump curve. It also gives the affinity-law shift for a speed or impeller change, and the NPSH.
Design flow and velocity: Q = Φ/(ρ c_p ΔT) when a heat load is given in place of a flow; v = Q/(πD²/4) Reynolds number and friction factor: Re = ρvD/μ; f = 64/Re for laminar flow (Re < 2300); Colebrook–White 1/√f = −2 log10(ε/3.7D + 2.51/(Re√f)) for turbulent flow, by fixed-point iteration (at most 100 steps) from the Swamee–Jain value, which also cross-checks it Head lost in a segment: h_f = f (L + L_eq)/D · v²/2g + K v²/2g System curve: H_sys(Q) = Σdz + h_out + Σh_f,lam·(Q/Q₀) + Σh_f,other·(Q/Q₀)², with a laminar segment's friction scaled as Q and every other friction and fitting loss as Q² (a closed circuit has Σdz = 0) Water held in the pipes: V_pipe = Σ π/4·D²·L Duty point: H_pump(Q) = H_sys(Q), every crossing found by a scan and refined by bisection, the stable one nearest the design flow reported; pump affinity laws Q ∝ n·D^a, H ∝ n²·D², with a = 1 for a trimmed impeller (the default) and a = 3 for a geometrically similar pump Net positive suction head available: NPSH_a = p_atm/ρg + z_s − h_f,suction − p_v/ρg, with h_f,suction that of the 'suction' segments at the larger of the design flow and the duty flow Shaft torque for the motor: T = P/ω, with P the larger of the design-flow and duty-point shaft power at the speed n_pump, or the shifted duty power at the shifted speed n_pump·n₂/n₁

3. Assumptions
  A1. Flow is steady, incompressible and fully developed, and all segments carry the full design flow in series. (Darcy–Weisbach.)
  A2. Fitting losses scale with velocity head (K method) or add pipe length (equivalent-length method). (Crane TP-410 practice.)
  A3. Both fitting methods may be used on one segment. (Crane TP-410 practice.)
  A4. The system curve scales turbulent and transitional friction and all fitting losses with Q² from the design flow. (H_sys = H_static + k₁ Q + k₂ Q².)
  A5. The friction factor is held at its design-flow value on the system curve. (H_sys = H_static + k₁ Q + k₂ Q².)
  A6. The friction of a laminar segment scales with Q, and the static part is independent of flow. (H_sys = H_static + k₁ Q + k₂ Q².)
  A7. Water properties are from published correlations at atmospheric pressure, 0 to 100 °C. (Kell 1975; Vogel fit; Hyland & Wexler 1983.)
  A8. Any other fluid is entered with its own density and viscosity and their provenance. (Stated.)
  A9. h_out (Pressure head required at the outlet) is taken as 0 m. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A10. p_atm (Atmospheric pressure (NPSH)) is taken as 101.325 kPa. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)

1. Scope and method
Calculates the state of moist air from dry-bulb and one other property at a stated pressure or altitude. It then applies one air-handling process with its heat and moisture balance: sensible heating or cooling, dehumidification, humidification or mixing.
Saturation and humidity: p_ws(T) by Hyland–Wexler over water at or above 0 °C and over ice below; W = 0.621945 p_w/(p − p_w); φ = p_w/p_ws Enthalpy and volume per kilogram of dry air: h = 1.006 T + W(2501 + 1.86 T) kJ/kg; v = 0.287042 (T + 273.15)(1 + 1.607858 W)/p Dew point and wet-bulb temperatures, by bisection: p_ws(T_dp) = p_w; the wet-bulb from the adiabatic-saturation relation Sensible heating or cooling at constant moisture content: Phi = ṁ (h₂ − h₁) Cooling with dehumidification, less the heat leaving in the condensate: Q_total = ṁ(h₁ − h₂) − ṁ_w h_w, with ṁ_w = ṁ(W₁ − W₂) Sensible and latent split of the coil load, along T₁ → T₂ at W₁ then W₁ → W₂ at T₂: Q_sensible = ṁ(h₁ − h(T₂, W₁)); Q_latent = ṁ(h(T₂, W₁) − h₂); SHR = Q_sensible/(Q_sensible + Q_latent); with RH_2 blank and T₂ above the entering dew point the coil is dry, W₂ = W₁ Steam and adiabatic humidification: h₂ = h₁ + (W₂ − W₁) h_steam for steam; h₂ = h₁ for adiabatic (spray) humidification Mixing two airstreams, weighted by dry-air mass flow: W_mix = Σṁ W/Σṁ; h_mix = Σṁ h/Σṁ

1. Scope and method
Calculates the insulation thickness of a pipe or flat surface that meets a heat-loss limit, a hot-surface limit or an anti-condensation requirement. For a chosen thickness it gives the heat flow, surface temperature and PASS or FAIL.
Pipe resistance per metre: R = ln(D_e/D_i)/(2π·λ) + 1/(h_se·π·D_e), D_e = D_i + 2t; with the inner film and pipe wall R_oth = 1/(h_i·π·D_p) + ln(D_i/D_p)/(2π·λ_w) added in series Flat resistance per square metre: R = t/λ + 1/h_se (+ 1/h_i + t_w/λ_w) Heat flow: q = (θ_i − θ_a)/R, positive from the process to the ambient Surface temperature: θ_se = θ_a + q/(h_se·π·D_e) (pipe), θ_a + q/h_se (flat) Surface coefficient: h_se as given, or h_se = h_c + h_r with h_r = ε·σ·(T_s² + T_a²)(T_s + T_a); h_c from free convection to air (laminar or turbulent, the larger), forced convection (cross-flow cylinder or flat plate) and their cube-law blend; solved with θ_se, which it depends on Heat-loss limit: the thickness beyond which |q| ≤ q_max; surface-temperature limit (hot): θ_se ≤ θ_se,max; anti-condensation (cold): θ_se ≥ θ_dew + allowance, θ_dew entered (for example from the psychrometrics calculation) or from the Magnus relation at θ_a and RH Thickness solution: each criterion's thickness by scanning t from 0 to 1 m for the last broken point and bisecting the bracket after it; governing t = max over the selected criteria Critical diameter: D_crit = 2λ/h_se; below it, insulation raises the heat flow until D_e passes D_crit

  A1. Heat flows in steady state, one-dimensionally, through a single homogeneous layer of constant conductivity. (Formula route of the standard.)
  A2. The insulation has no thermal bridges at supports, no joints and no moisture. (Formula route of the standard.)
  A3. The conductivity λ is the value entered, which should be the value at the mean insulation temperature on the sheet. (Stated.)
  A4. With the correlation, the outer surface coefficient is h_se = h_c + h_r. (Incropera, §7.2, §7.4, §9.6 and §9.9 (section numbers to be checked against the owner's copy).)
  A5. Radiation is h_r = ε·σ·(T_s² + T_a²)(T_s + T_a) to surroundings at the ambient air temperature. (Incropera, §1.2.)
  A6. Free convection to air uses Holman's simplified relations at atmospheric pressure, the larger of the laminar and turbulent forms. (Holman's constants: horizontal cylinder 1.32(ΔT/D)^0.25 and 1.24ΔT^(1/3), vertical surface 1.42(ΔT/L)^0.25 and 1.31ΔT^(1/3).)
  A7. Forced convection uses Churchill–Bernstein for a cylinder in cross-flow and the laminar or mixed flat-plate relations along a plate. (Incropera, §7.2 and §7.4.)
  A8. Air properties are fits at the film temperature, with Pr = 0.71. (Stated.)
  A9. Free and forced convection blend as (h_nat³ + h_forced³)^(1/3). (Stated.)
  A10. The correlation is indicative, not the standard's own. (The standard's own coefficients are not reproduced.)
  A11. The dew point is entered, or is the Magnus relation at the ambient temperature and humidity. (Derived; the Glaser condensation calculation shares the function.)
  A12. Below 0 °C the Magnus relation is over ice, so the humidity is then taken relative to ice. (Stated; enter the dew point for sub-zero air, because weather humidity is usually relative to water.)
  A13. An allowance above the dew point is the user's choice. (Stated.)
  A14. Pipes are treated as horizontal and flat surfaces as vertical for free convection. (Stated.)
  A15. Air conductivity is a fit within about 3 % over 250 to 400 K. (Stated.)
  A16. Air viscosity is a fit within about 4 % over 250 to 400 K. (Stated.)
  A17. The required thickness is the least thickness beyond which the criterion holds for every thicker layer up to 1 m. (Stated; this matters when thin insulation on a small pipe raises the heat flow (critical diameter).)
  A18. No thickness table of any standard, including BS 5422, is used or shipped. (No standard's table is reproduced.)
  A19. The conductivity is the user's input, from a library material or the maker's data. (No standard's table is reproduced.)
  A20. L_surf (Height or flow length of the flat surface (correlation, flat)) is taken as 1 m. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A21. v_wind (Air speed over the surface (correlation; 0 = still air)) is taken as 0 m/s. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A22. eps (Emissivity of the outer surface (for the correlation; with h_se as given it only judges that value against condensation on a cold surface)) is taken as 0.9. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
