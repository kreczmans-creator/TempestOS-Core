1. SCOPE AND METHOD
U-values of built-up elements from their layers and surface resistances, and the steady-state design heat loss of one room: fabric loss through each element to its adjacent temperature, a thermal-bridge allowance, ventilation loss (through the outside temperature or an MVHR supply temperature), the room load for sizing emitters, the heat loss coefficient and the loss per floor area; optionally checked against target U-values.
Element U-value: U = 1/(R_si + Σ d/λ + R_g + R_se), or the user's U override for ground floors and glazing Element fabric loss: Φ_i = A·U·f·(θ_int − θ_adj), θ_adj = θ_e where the element faces outside Thermal bridges: Φ_tb = y_tb·Σ(A·f)_ext·(θ_int − θ_e) on the elements facing outside Fabric total: Φ_T = ΣΦ_i + Φ_tb Ventilation flow: q_v = n·V Ventilation loss: Φ_V = 0.34·q_v·(θ_int − θ_e), or (θ_int − θ_supply) through an MVHR supply, plus 0.34·n_inf·V·(θ_int − θ_e) infiltration with MVHR Room load: Φ_HL = Φ_T + Φ_V (CIBSE DHDG method: × (1 + additional allowance)) Heat loss coefficient: HLC = Φ_HL/(θ_int − θ_e); loss per floor area Φ_HL/A_floor U-value check: U against the target U of each element (the global target applies to built-up elements facing outside only; an element's own target applies wherever it faces)
  Ref: BS EN ISO 6946 homogeneous layers and surface resistances (clause numbers to be checked against the owner's copy) — Building components — thermal resistance and thermal transmittance
  Ref: BS EN 12831-1 cl. 6.3.2 (clause number to be checked against the owner's copy) — Heating systems in buildings — design heat load: transmission heat loss
  Ref: BS EN 12831-1 cl. 6.3.3 (clause number to be checked against the owner's copy) — Heating systems in buildings — design heat load: ventilation heat loss
  Ref: CIBSE Domestic Heating Design Guide 2026 ed., reduced method (name and edition unverified; to be checked against the owner's copy) — Room heat loss and the additional allowance

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Steady state: every temperature is constant, with no solar or internal gains, no thermal mass and no dynamic simulation. (Design heat loss, not an annual energy estimate.)
  A2. Layers are homogeneous and in series (one-dimensional flow); no bridged layers (timber studs, mortar joints, fixings) are corrected for: a thermal-bridge allowance y_tb is added instead. (BS EN ISO 6946 homogeneous-layer calculation.)
  A3. A blank Rsi is the conventional BS EN ISO 6946 value for the element's heat-flow direction (0.13 m²K/W horizontal, 0.10 upward, 0.17 downward) and a blank Rse is 0.04 m²K/W for an element facing outside (no adjacent temperature, or one at or below θ_e), or the same as Rsi where it faces another space. (Small conventional BS EN ISO 6946 set (three values), not a table of design data.)
  A4. The global target U applies to built-up elements facing outside only: an internal partition or floor has no outside target, so it is checked only against a target entered on its own row. (Scope of the target check.)
  A5. Ground-floor U-values and glazing U-values are entered by the user (override); the calculator does not model ground heat transfer. (Stated limit.)
  A6. The air constant 0.34 Wh/(m³·K) is the conventional rounded ρ·c_p of air (about 1.2 kg/m³ × 1005 J/(kg·K) ÷ 3600); it is derived, not read from a table. (Derived.)
  A7. Design temperatures, conductivities, surface resistances and the thermal-bridge allowance are user records from the user's own licensed copies or manufacturers' data; none is embedded. (No table from a standard or a manufacturer is shipped.)
  A8. method (Calculation method (changes the citations; the DHDG option adds the allowance below)) is taken as BS EN 12831-1 elemental. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A9. y_tb (Thermal-bridge allowance on the area facing outside) is taken as 0 W/(m^2*K). (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A10. f_add (Additional allowance on the room load (CIBSE DHDG method only)) is taken as 0 %. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A11. Conventional BS EN ISO 6946 surface resistances (R_si 0.13 horizontal, 0.10 upward, 0.17 downward; R_se 0.04 outside, or the same as R_si where the element faces another space; m²K/W) are used where blank, for: External wall. (Small conventional set; enter your own where the construction needs other values.)

4. WORKING
  [8] R_1 = R_si + Σ d/λ + R_g + R_se    « BS EN ISO 6946
      = External wall: 0.13 + (0.05 + 2 + 0.26875 + 0.016667) + 0 + 0.04
      = 2.5054 m²·K/W
        External wall: total thermal resistance
  [9] U_1 = 1/R    « BS EN ISO 6946
      = 1 / 2.5054
      = 0.39914 W/(m²·K)
        External wall: U-value
  [10] Phi_1 = A·U·f·(θ_int − θ_adj)    « BS EN 12831-1 cl. 6.3.2
      = 18 × 0.39914 × 1 × (21 − -3) °C

        External wall: fabric loss to θ_e = -3 °C
  [11] U_2 = U override (user-entered)

        Window: U-value as entered (ground floor or glazing)
  [12] Phi_2 = A·U·f·(θ_int − θ_adj)    « BS EN 12831-1 cl. 6.3.2
      = 3 × 1.4 × 1 × (21 − -3) °C
      = 100.8 W
        Window: fabric loss to θ_e = -3 °C
  [13] A_fab = Σ A
      = 21 m²
      = 21 m²
        Total fabric area
  [14] A_ext = Σ A facing outside
      = 21 m²
      = 21 m²

  [16] U_m = Σ A·U·f / Σ A
      = 11.384 / 21

        Area-weighted mean U-value of the fabric

      = 21 degC − (-3 degC)

        Design temperature difference, inside to outside
  [18] Phi_el = Σ Φ_i    « BS EN 12831-1 cl. 6.3.2
      = 273.23 W

  [19] Phi_tb = y_tb * A_tb * dT    « BS EN 12831-1 cl. 6.3.2
      = 0 W/(m²·K) × 21 m² × 24 K
      = 0 W
        Thermal-bridge allowance
  [20] Phi_T = Phi_el + Phi_tb    « BS EN 12831-1 cl. 6.3.2
      = 273.23 W + 0 W
      = 273.23 W
        Fabric heat loss
  [21] q_v = n * V
      = 0.5 1/h × 48 m³
      = 24 m³/h
        Ventilation air flow
  [22] dT_v = dT
      = 24 K
      = 24 K
        Ventilation temperature difference, inside to outside
  [23] Phi_V = 0.34 Wh/(m^3*K) * q_v * dT_v    « BS EN 12831-1 cl. 6.3.3
      = 0.34 Wh/(m^3*K) × 24 m³/h × 24 K
      = 195.84 W
        Ventilation heat loss (0.34 Wh/m³K = conventional ρ·c_p of air, derived)
  [25] Phi_HL = Phi_T + Phi_V    « BS EN 12831-1 cl. 6.3.2
      = 273.23 W + 195.84 W
      = 469.07 W
        Room design heat loss
  [26] HLC = Phi_HL / dT
      = 469.07 W / 24 K
      = 19.544 W/K
        Heat loss coefficient
  [28] Phi_A = Phi_HL / A_floor
      = 469.07 W / 16 m²
      = 29.317 W/m²
        Room design heat loss per floor area
  [29] u_1 = U / U_target
      = 0.39914 / 0.45
      = 0.88697
        External wall: utilisation of the target U-value
