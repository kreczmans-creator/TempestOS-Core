1. Scope and method
U-values of built-up elements from their layers and surface resistances, and the steady-state design heat loss of one room: fabric loss through each element to its adjacent temperature, a thermal-bridge allowance, ventilation loss (through the outside temperature or an MVHR supply temperature), the room load for sizing emitters, the heat loss coefficient and the loss per floor area; optionally checked against target U-values.
Element U-value: U = 1/(R_si + Σ d/λ + R_g + R_se), or the user's U override for ground floors and glazing Element fabric loss: Φ_i = A·U·f·(θ_int − θ_adj), θ_adj = θ_e where the element faces outside Thermal bridges: Φ_tb = y_tb·Σ(A·f)_ext·(θ_int − θ_e) on the elements facing outside Fabric total: Φ_T = ΣΦ_i + Φ_tb Ventilation flow: q_v = n·V Ventilation loss: Φ_V = 0.34·q_v·(θ_int − θ_e), or (θ_int − θ_supply) through an MVHR supply, plus 0.34·n_inf·V·(θ_int − θ_e) infiltration with MVHR Room load: Φ_HL = Φ_T + Φ_V (CIBSE DHDG method: × (1 + additional allowance)) Heat loss coefficient: HLC = Φ_HL/(θ_int − θ_e); loss per floor area Φ_HL/A_floor U-value check: U against the target U of each element (the global target applies to built-up elements facing outside only; an element's own target applies wherever it faces)

3. Assumptions
  A1. Steady state: every temperature is constant, with no solar or internal gains, no thermal mass and no dynamic simulation. (Design heat loss, not an annual energy estimate.)
  A2. Layers are homogeneous and in series (one-dimensional flow); no bridged layers (timber studs, mortar joints, fixings) are corrected for: a thermal-bridge allowance y_tb is added instead. (BS EN ISO 6946 homogeneous-layer calculation.)
  A3. A blank Rsi is the conventional BS EN ISO 6946 value for the element's heat-flow direction (0.13 m²K/W horizontal, 0.10 upward, 0.17 downward) and a blank Rse is 0.04 m²K/W for an element facing outside (no adjacent temperature, or one at or below θ_e), or the same as Rsi where it faces another space. (Small conventional BS EN ISO 6946 set (three values), not a table of design data.)
  A4. The global target U applies to built-up elements facing outside only: an internal partition or floor has no outside target, so it is checked only against a target entered on its own row. (Scope of the target check.)
  A5. Ground-floor U-values and glazing U-values are entered by the user (override); the calculator does not model ground heat transfer. (Stated limit.)
  A6. The air constant 0.34 Wh/(m³·K) is the conventional rounded ρ·c_p of air (about 1.2 kg/m³ × 1005 J/(kg·K) ÷ 3600); it is derived, not read from a table. (Derived.)
  A7. Design temperatures, conductivities, surface resistances and the thermal-bridge allowance are user records from the user's own licensed copies or manufacturers' data; none is embedded. (No table from a standard or a manufacturer is shipped.)
  A8. method (Calculation method (changes the citations; the DHDG option adds the allowance below)) is taken as BS EN 12831-1 elemental. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A9. y_tb (Thermal-bridge allowance on the area facing outside) is taken as 0 W/(m²·K). (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A10. f_add (Additional allowance on the room load (CIBSE DHDG method only)) is taken as 0 %. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)

1. Scope and method
The state of moist air (humidity ratio, relative humidity, dew point, wet-bulb, enthalpy, specific volume, density) from dry-bulb and one other property at a stated pressure or altitude, and one air-handling process from that state with its heat and moisture balance: sensible heating or cooling, cooling with dehumidification, steam humidification, adiabatic humidification, or mixing with a second stream.
Saturation vapour pressure at the dry-bulb temperature, over water at or above 0 °C and over ice below: p_ws(t) by Hyland–Wexler. Moisture content (humidity ratio) of the air: W = 0.621945 p_w/(p − p_w). Relative humidity: φ = p_w/p_ws. Specific enthalpy per kilogram of dry air: h = 1.006 t + W(2501 + 1.86 t) kJ/kg_da. Specific volume: v = 0.287042 (t + 273.15)(1 + 1.607858 W)/p. Dew point and wet-bulb temperatures, by bisection (wet-bulb from the adiabatic-saturation relation): p_ws(t_dp) = p_w. Sensible heating or cooling at constant moisture content: Q = ṁ (h₂ − h₁). Cooling with dehumidification, less the heat leaving in the condensate: Q_total = ṁ(h₁ − h₂) − ṁ_w h_w, with ṁ_w = ṁ(W₁ − W₂). Sensible and latent split of the coil load, along the path t₁ → t₂ at W₁ then W₁ → W₂ at t₂: Q_sensible = ṁ(h₁ − h(t₂, W₁)); Q_latent = ṁ(h(t₂, W₁) − h₂); SHR = Q_sensible/(Q_sensible + Q_latent). With RH_2 blank and t₂ above the entering dew point the coil is dry: W₂ = W₁, no condensate, sensible only. Steam humidification: h₂ = h₁ + (W₂ − W₁) h_steam. Adiabatic (spray) humidification, at constant enthalpy: h₂ = h₁. Mixing two airstreams, weighted by dry-air mass flow: W_mix = Σṁ W/Σṁ; h_mix = Σṁ h/Σṁ.

1. Scope and method
Interstitial condensation in a layered wall by the Glaser method over twelve monthly climate steps: the temperature and saturation pressure at every layer interface, the vapour-pressure line with its tangent construction at the condensation planes, the condensate on each interface month by month carried forward from the first month of condensation (drying when the weather turns), the annual balance, the peak accumulation against the owner's limit, and the surface temperature factor f_Rsi against its minimum at the critical surface humidity.
Layer resistance and diffusion thickness: R_j = d_j/λ_j; s_d,j = μ_j·d_j Total resistance and interface temperature: R_T = R_si + Σ R_j + R_se; θ_k = θ_i − (θ_i − θ_e)·(R_si + Σ_(j≤k) R_j)/R_T Saturation pressure (Magnus): p_sat = 610.5·exp(17.269·θ/(237.3 + θ)) over water (θ ≥ 0 °C); p_sat = 610.5·exp(21.875·θ/(265.5 + θ)) over ice (θ < 0 °C) Outside vapour pressure: p_e = RH_e·p_sat(θ_e) Inside vapour pressure: p_i = RH_i·p_sat(θ_i), or p_e + f_Δp·Δv(θ_e)·R_v·(T_i + T_e)/2 with the moisture excess Δv linear between two user points and constant beyond them (R_v = 461.5 J/(kg·K)) and the safety factor f_Δp on the pressure difference (a record, default 1.10; clause wording to be checked against the owner's copy of ISO 13788) Vapour flux through a layer: g = δ0·Δp/Δs_d with δ0 = 2×10⁻¹⁰ kg/(m·s·Pa) Tangent construction: the line from p_i to p_e is bent onto the saturation line at the interfaces it would rise above (and at wet interfaces), by the smallest-slope rule from each contact point Condensation at an interface: g_c = g_in − g_out; Mc accumulates g_c·t over 12 months from the first month of condensation (t = the row's days, else the calendar days of the month, January to December order), a wet interface (held at saturation) drying at g_c < 0 until nothing is left Refinement check: the same year is run again with every layer split into 20 equal sub-layers of the same λ and μ (inside the model, not counted against the 30-layer limit), because the saturation line is curved inside a layer and the interface-only model can miss the condensate that forms between two interfaces; the annual balance passes only when it passes both ways, and the sheet warns when the two runs differ (a verdict flips, or Mc_end or the wall peak differ by more than 10 %) Annual balance and peak: PASS when no condensate remains after the 12 months (as entered and in 20 sub-layers per layer); Mc_max = the most held on any interface, checked against the user's limit Wall U-value and its cross-check: U = 1/R_T; when the heat-loss calculation's U-value U_ref is given, the sheet warns if |U − U_ref|/U_ref exceeds 2 % (the two calculations then describe different constructions) Surface factor: f_Rsi = 1 − R_si,f/R_T,f with R_si,f = 0.25 m²K/W by default for an opaque element (conventional, a record; 0.13 for glazing); f_Rsi,min = the largest monthly (θ_si,min − θ_e)/(θ_i − θ_e) with p_sat(θ_si,min) = p_i/φ_crit; PASS when f_Rsi ≥ f_Rsi,min

3. Assumptions
  A1. Steady state in each month: one-dimensional diffusion of water vapour through homogeneous layers in series, with the monthly mean climate; no air movement, no liquid transport, no moisture storage, no solar or rain gain. (The Glaser method.)
  A2. Material properties (λ, μ) are constants of the layer, not functions of its moisture or temperature; condensate does not change them. (The Glaser method.)
  A3. Condensation is checked at the interfaces between layers; the saturation line is joined straight between the interface values, which can miss condensate that forms inside a layer. The annual balance is therefore also run with every layer split into 20 sub-layers and passes only when both pass; the limit on the condensate per interface is checked on the interfaces as entered. (Stated limit; refinement check (bs.glaser-01). Whether ISO 13788 requires sub-division is to be checked against the owner's copy.)
  A4. The Magnus saturation pressure is used over water at and above 0 °C and over ice below, and the water-vapour permeability of air is δ0 = 2×10⁻¹⁰ kg/(m·s·Pa); both are closed-form physics, not data of a standard. (Derived.)
  A5. Where a month's length is blank it is the calendar days of the month, taking the climate rows as January to December (February 28 days); enter the days where the record starts in another month. A wet interface that dries within a month is taken as dry from the next month; the month's rates are those of the wet state at its start. (Monthly steps.)
  A6. The monthly climate, the humidity-class points, the surface resistances, the critical humidity and the limit on the condensate are the user's records from the user's own licensed copies; no climate data, humidity-class table, material table or limit of any standard is shipped. (No table from a standard is shipped.)
  A7. dp_f (Safety factor on the vapour-pressure difference Δp = Δv·R_v·(T_i + T_e)/2 in the moisture-excess mode (design margin, 1.10 by convention; a record, wording to be checked against the owner's copy of ISO 13788)) is taken as 1.1. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A8. breathable (The build-up is timber or breathable (hygroscopic layers, capillary transport)) is taken as no. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A9. Month length: 12 row(s) of the climate gave no days, so the calendar days of the month are used with the rows taken as January to December (February 28 days). If the record starts in another month, enter the days of each row. (Calendar months.)
