1. Scope and method
Calculates the U-values of built-up elements and the steady-state design heat loss of one room. It gives the fabric and ventilation loss, the room load for sizing emitters, the heat loss coefficient and an optional check against target U-values.
Element U-value: U = 1/(R_si + Σ d/λ + R_g + R_se), or the user's U override for ground floors and glazing, or the ground floor record U_g for a floor without layers or an override Element fabric loss: Φ_i = A·U·f·(T_int − θ_adj), with θ_adj = θ_e where the element faces outside Thermal bridges: Φ_tb = y_tb·Σ(A·f)_ext·(T_int − θ_e) on the elements facing outside Fabric total: Φ_T = ΣΦ_i + Φ_tb Ventilation loss: q_v = n·V; Φ_V = 0.34·q_v·(T_int − θ_e), or (T_int − T_supply) through an MVHR supply, plus 0.34·n_inf·V·(T_int − θ_e) infiltration with MVHR Room load: Φ_HL = Φ_T + Φ_V (CIBSE DHDG method: × (1 + additional allowance)) Heat loss coefficient: HLC = Φ_HL/(T_int − θ_e); loss per floor area Φ_HL/A_floor U-value check: U against the target U of each element (the global target applies to built-up elements facing outside only; an element's own target applies wherever it faces)

3. Assumptions
  A1. Steady state: every temperature is constant, with no solar or internal gains, no thermal mass and no dynamic simulation. (Design heat loss, not an annual energy estimate.)
  A2. Layers are homogeneous and in series, with one-dimensional heat flow. (BS EN ISO 6946:2017 homogeneous-layer calculation.)
  A3. Bridged layers such as timber studs, mortar joints and fixings are not corrected for. (BS EN ISO 6946:2017 homogeneous-layer calculation.)
  A4. A thermal-bridge allowance y_tb is added instead of correcting bridged layers. (Stated.)
  A5. A blank Rsi is the conventional BS EN ISO 6946 value for the element's heat-flow direction. (Small conventional BS EN ISO 6946 set (three values), not a table of design data.)
  A6. The conventional Rsi is 0.13 m²K/W horizontal, 0.10 upward and 0.17 downward. (Small conventional BS EN ISO 6946 set (three values), not a table of design data.)
  A7. A blank Rse is 0.04 m²K/W for an element facing outside. (Small conventional BS EN ISO 6946 set, not a table of design data.)
  A8. Where an element faces another space, a blank Rse is the same as Rsi. (Small conventional BS EN ISO 6946 set, not a table of design data.)
  A9. The global target U applies to built-up elements facing outside only. (Scope of the target check: an internal partition or floor is checked only against a target entered on its own row.)
  A10. Ground-floor and glazing U-values are entered by the user as an override. (Stated limit.)
  A11. A ground floor U-value record fills U_g for a floor element without layers or an override. (Stated limit: the record's value is the user's, from its table.)
  A12. The calculator does not model ground heat transfer. (Stated limit.)
  A13. The air constant 0.34 Wh/(m³·K) is the conventional rounded ρ·c_p of air. (Derived: about 1.2 kg/m³ × 1005 J/(kg·K) ÷ 3600, not read from a table.)
  A14. Design temperatures, conductivities, surface resistances and the thermal-bridge allowance are the user's own records. (No table from a standard or a manufacturer is shipped.)

1. Scope and method
Calculates the state of moist air from dry-bulb and one other property at a stated pressure or altitude. It then applies one air-handling process with its heat and moisture balance: sensible heating or cooling, dehumidification, humidification or mixing.
Saturation and humidity: p_ws(T) by Hyland–Wexler over water at or above 0 °C and over ice below; W = 0.621945 p_w/(p − p_w); φ = p_w/p_ws Enthalpy and volume per kilogram of dry air: h = 1.006 T + W(2501 + 1.86 T) kJ/kg; v = 0.287042 (T + 273.15)(1 + 1.607858 W)/p Dew point and wet-bulb temperatures, by bisection: p_ws(T_dp) = p_w; the wet-bulb from the adiabatic-saturation relation Sensible heating or cooling at constant moisture content: Phi = ṁ (h₂ − h₁) Cooling with dehumidification, less the heat leaving in the condensate: Q_total = ṁ(h₁ − h₂) − ṁ_w h_w, with ṁ_w = ṁ(W₁ − W₂) Sensible and latent split of the coil load, along T₁ → T₂ at W₁ then W₁ → W₂ at T₂: Q_sensible = ṁ(h₁ − h(T₂, W₁)); Q_latent = ṁ(h(T₂, W₁) − h₂); SHR = Q_sensible/(Q_sensible + Q_latent); with RH_2 blank and T₂ above the entering dew point the coil is dry, W₂ = W₁ Steam and adiabatic humidification: h₂ = h₁ + (W₂ − W₁) h_steam for steam; h₂ = h₁ for adiabatic (spray) humidification Mixing two airstreams, weighted by dry-air mass flow: W_mix = Σṁ W/Σṁ; h_mix = Σṁ h/Σṁ

1. Scope and method
Calculates interstitial condensation in a layered wall by the Glaser method over twelve monthly climate steps. It gives the condensate on each interface, the annual balance, the peak against the limit and the surface temperature factor.
Layer resistance and diffusion thickness: R_j = d_j/λ_j; s_d,j = μ_j·d_j Total resistance, U-value and interface temperature: R_T = R_si + Σ R_j + R_se; U = 1/R_T; T_k = T_int − (T_int − T_e)·(R_si + Σ_(j≤k) R_j)/R_T Saturation pressure (Magnus): p_sat = 610.5·exp(17.269·T/(237.3 + T)) over water (T ≥ 0 °C); p_sat = 610.5·exp(21.875·T/(265.5 + T)) over ice (T < 0 °C) Vapour pressures: p_e = RH_e·p_sat(T_e); p_i = RH_i·p_sat(T_int), or p_e + f_Δp·Δv(T_e)·R_v·(T_i + T_e)/2 in the moisture-excess mode with R_v = 461.5 J/(kg·K) Vapour flux and condensate: g = δ0·Δp/Δs_d with δ0 = 2×10⁻¹⁰ kg/(m·s·Pa); g_c = g_in − g_out on the tangent-construction profile; Mc accumulates g_c·t over 12 months, a wet interface drying at g_c < 0 Refinement check: the same year with every layer split into 20 equal sub-layers of the same λ and μ; the annual balance passes only when it passes both ways Annual balance and peak: PASS when no condensate remains after 12 months; Mc_max = the most held on any interface, checked against Mc_limit Surface factor: f_Rsi = 1 − R_si,f/R_T,f; f_Rsi,min = the largest monthly (T_si,min − T_e)/(T_int − T_e) with p_sat(T_si,min) = p_i/φ_crit; PASS when f_Rsi ≥ f_Rsi,min

3. Assumptions
  A1. Vapour diffuses one-dimensionally through homogeneous layers in series, in steady state each month. (The Glaser method.)
  A2. Each month uses its mean climate, with no air movement, liquid transport, moisture storage, or solar or rain gain. (The Glaser method.)
  A3. Material properties λ and μ are constants of each layer. (The Glaser method.)
  A4. Condensate does not change the properties of a layer. (The Glaser method.)
  A5. Condensation is checked at the interfaces between layers. (The Glaser method.)
  A6. The saturation line is straight between interface values, which can miss condensate inside a layer. (Stated limit; whether ISO 13788 requires sub-division is to be checked against the owner's copy.)
  A7. The annual balance is also run with every layer split into 20 sub-layers and passes only when both runs pass. (Refinement check.)
  A8. The limit on the condensate per interface is checked on the interfaces as entered. (Stated limit.)
  A9. The Magnus saturation pressure is used over water at and above 0 °C and over ice below. (Derived.)
  A10. The water-vapour permeability of air is δ0 = 2×10⁻¹⁰ kg/(m·s·Pa). (Derived; closed-form physics, not data of a standard.)
  A11. A blank month length is the calendar days of the month, with the climate rows taken as January to December. (Calendar months, February 28 days; enter the days where the record starts in another month.)
  A12. A wet interface that dries within a month is dry from the next month. (Monthly steps.)
  A13. The rates of a month are those of the wet state at its start. (Monthly steps.)
  A14. The climate, humidity-class points, surface resistances, critical humidity and condensate limit are the user's own records. (No table from a standard is shipped.)
  A15. breathable (The build-up is timber or breathable (hygroscopic layers, capillary transport)) is taken as no. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A16. dp_f (Safety factor on the vapour-pressure difference Δp = Δv·R_v·(T_i + T_e)/2 in the moisture-excess mode (1.10 by convention; a record, wording to be checked against the owner's copy of ISO 13788)) is taken as 1.1. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A17. Month length: 12 row(s) of the climate gave no days, so the calendar days of the month are used with the rows taken as January to December (February 28 days). If the record starts in another month, enter the days of each row. (Calendar months.)
