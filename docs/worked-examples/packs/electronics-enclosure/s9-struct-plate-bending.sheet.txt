1. Scope and method
Bending of a thin, flat, isotropic plate under a lateral load by Kirchhoff small-deflection theory: circular plates (uniform pressure, central patch, ring load; clamped or simply supported) in closed form; rectangular plates simply supported (uniform pressure or central patch, Navier series) or clamped (Ritz, approximate). Maximum deflection, greatest von Mises stress at the surface, stress and deflection utilisations and the fundamental frequency.
Flexural rigidity: D = E·t³/(12(1 − ν²)); surface stress σ = 6M/t²; von Mises σ_vm = √(σ_x² − σ_x·σ_y + σ_y² + 3τ_xy²) Circular, uniform q, clamped: w = q·a⁴/(64D); σ_edge = 3q·a²/(4t²); simply supported: w = (5 + ν)·q·a⁴/(64(1 + ν)D); σ_centre = 3(3 + ν)·q·a²/(8t²) Circular, central patch (radius c, pressure q) or ring (radius b, force F): w = A + B·r² (+ q·r⁴/64D) inside, A′ + B′·r² + C′·ln(r/a) + k·r²·ln(r/a) outside, k = q·c²/8D or F/8πD; constants from continuity of w, w′, M_r at the load radius and the edge conditions; M_r = −D(w″ + ν·w′/r), M_θ = −D(w′/r + ν·w″) Rectangular, simply supported: w = Σ W_mn·sin(mπx/a)·sin(nπy/b) over odd m, n; W_mn = q_mn/(π⁴D(m²/a² + n²/b²)²); q_mn = 16q/(π²mn) (uniform) or 16q/(π²mn)·sin(mπ/2)·sin(nπ/2)·sin(mπu/2a)·sin(nπv/2b) (patch u × v); M_x = Dπ²ΣW_mn(m²/a² + ν·n²/b²)·sin·sin, M_y likewise, M_xy = −D(1 − ν)π²ΣW_mn(mn/ab)·cos·cos; terms added until w, the centre moments and the corner M_xy each change by < 10⁻⁶ over a doubling (cap 1024 × 1024; the corner sum of a uniform load, which converges like 1/N², is Richardson-extrapolated before it is compared) Rectangular, clamped: Ritz, w = Σ c_ij·X_i(x/a)·Y_j(y/b), X_k(ξ) = ξ²(1 − ξ)²·P₂ₖ(2ξ − 1); c from the minimum of the total potential energy; order K taken as 8, 12, 16, 20, 25, 30 until w and the stress change by < 10⁻⁵ (or the change stops falling) Fundamental frequency: simply supported rectangle ω₁₁ = π²(1/a² + 1/b²)·√(D/(ρt)), f = ω/2π; circular ω = (λ²/a²)·√(D/(ρt)), λ² from the Bessel characteristic equation; clamped rectangle by Rayleigh–Ritz with the same basis Plate checks: σ_vm ≤ f_y/γ_M0; w_max ≤ span/ratio (circular span = diameter, rectangle span = shorter side); WARN w/t > 0.5

3. Assumptions
  A1. Thin plate, small-deflection (Kirchhoff) theory: flat, isotropic, linear elastic, constant thickness; the middle surface is unstrained; shear deformation is neglected (span/t of 10 or more). (Scope of S9; a warning below span/t = 10.)
  A2. The deflection stays small against the thickness: w/t above 0.5 means membrane action stiffens the plate and the small-deflection result is not valid (it overstates the deflection and the bending stress and ignores the membrane stress). (The sheet warns at w/t > 0.5.)
  A3. The edge support is the same all round and ideal: a simply supported edge is held at zero deflection and free to rotate; a clamped edge allows no deflection or rotation. A simply supported rectangle has its corners held down (the corner reaction 2·M_xy is reported); if the corners can lift, the deflection and the central moments are larger than computed. (Navier solution; no elastic edges, no rotational springs.)
  A4. The von Mises stress is that of the plane-stress field at the plate surface from the bending and twisting moments (σ = 6M/t²); the through-thickness shear and the local stress under a concentrated load are not included. The stress governing a simply supported rectangle is at the centre or at a corner (twisting); a clamped rectangle is scanned on a 21 × 21 grid over a quarter of the plate. (Surface stresses; no local effects.)
  A5. The rectangular patch and the circular patch are centred; a ring load is concentric. A load smaller than the thickness is a concentrated load whose local stress thin-plate theory does not give. (Warning `patch-thin`; patches below 1 % (circular), 2 % (simply supported rectangle) or 25 % (clamped rectangle) of the plate size are refused.)
  A6. The clamped rectangle is a Ritz approximation: the deflection converges quickly, the moments and stress more slowly (the sheet reports the order used and the last change; long plates and small patches converge slowest, and the last change understates the error). Compare a critical result with finite elements. (Approximate; the sheet states the order used and the last change.)
  A7. Material properties are inputs taken from the W3 library (E, ν, ρ, f_y), each citing the material record, its revision and the page or table; the generic textbook seeds are indicative. (Record kind material.properties.)
  A8. P (Total force P (force basis: uniform, patch and ring loads; a ring load always uses P)) is taken as 5 kN. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A9. r0 (Patch radius (circular plate, central patch)) is taken as 50 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A10. u (Patch size in x (rectangular plate, central patch)) is taken as 100 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A11. v (Patch size in y (rectangular plate, central patch)) is taken as 100 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A12. r_ring (Ring radius (circular plate, ring load)) is taken as 150 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A13. defl_ratio (Deflection limit: span/ratio (span = diameter of a circular plate, shorter side of a rectangle)) is taken as 250. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
