Title: PCB trace width (IPC-2221)
Author: unknown    Authored: 2026-10-26
Checker: J. Reviewer    Checked: 2026-10-26
Issued: 2026-10-26    Issue: ELX-ENC-001    Client: Client Co

1. Scope and method
The minimum width of a printed-circuit trace for a current and an allowed temperature rise, from the legacy IPC-2221 curve-fit, and the resistance, voltage drop and I²R loss of the trace as drawn: legacy IPC-2221 curve-fit, conservative for multilayer boards. External or internal layer; copper weight in oz/ft²; resistance at the trace's operating temperature (ambient plus rise). The IPC-2152 method (charts, plane and board-thickness factors) is not implemented.
Trace cross-section: I = k·ΔT^0.44·A^0.725, so A = [I/(k·ΔT^0.44)]^(1/0.725) with A in mil², k = 0.048 external, 0.024 internal Copper thickness: t = w_Cu·t_oz, t_oz = 1.378 mil per oz/ft² (35 µm, derived) Minimum width: w_min = A/t; utilisation u_w = w_min/w_act Resistivity at the operating temperature: ρ_T = ρ_20·[1 + α·(T_amb + ΔT − 20)] Trace resistance: R = ρ_T·L/(w_act·t); voltage drop ΔV = I·R; loss P = I²·R

3. Assumptions
  A1. The curve-fit is the legacy IPC-2221 relation, drawn for a trace on an outer or inner layer with no plane or convective help, 1 to 3 oz copper, up to 35 A, rises of 10 to 100 K and widths to 10 mm; it is conservative for multilayer boards and is a screening of width, not a thermal analysis of the board. (IPC-2221 curve-fit (legacy); IPC-2152 is not implemented.)
  A2. Copper thickness is the nominal 1.378 mil (35 µm) per oz/ft² of finished copper; plating build-up on outer layers and etch-back are not modelled. (Derived: 35 µm / 25.4 µm per mil.)
  A3. Resistance uses the trace as drawn at the temperature ambient + rise, from the resistivity at 20 °C and a linear temperature coefficient; vias, pads, bends and skin effect are ignored. (Derived constants for annealed copper.)
  A4. Steady direct current (or RMS current at low frequency); the rise is the allowed rise in the worst case. (Scope of the curve-fit.)
  A5. k_ext (Curve-fit constant k, external layer) is taken as 0.048. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A6. k_int (Curve-fit constant k, internal layer) is taken as 0.024. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A7. n_dT (Exponent of the temperature rise) is taken as 0.44. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A8. n_A (Exponent of the cross-section) is taken as 0.725. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A9. t_oz (Copper thickness per oz/ft² (35 µm = 1.378 mil, derived)) is taken as 1.378 mil. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A10. rho_20 (Copper resistivity at 20 °C (annealed copper, 1.724e-8 Ω·m)) is taken as 0.01724 Ω·mm²/m. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A11. alpha (Temperature coefficient of resistance of copper) is taken as 0.00393 1/K. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)

1. Scope and method
The demand of a set of loads per supply (three-phase AC, single-phase AC, DC) and the minimum DC power supply, transformer and battery for it, each with a margin; the heat the loads dissipate for the enclosure calculation.
Demand per load: P_i·d_i·k_i; apparent power P_i·d_i·k_i/cosφ_i (arithmetic sum, conservative); this duty-weighted demand sizes the transformer and the enclosure heat Running load of each load while it is on: P_i·k_i, apparent P_i·k_i/cosφ_i (the duty d is the share of time on, so it is left out: a cable, a bus and a power supply carry the load while it is on) Bus currents: I_3φ = S_3φ,run/(√3·U), I_1φ = S_1φ,run/U, I_DC = P_DC,run/U_DC, from the running loads; when a bus is chosen for the DC power supply its input P_DC,run/η_PSU (power factor 1) is added to that bus Heat dissipated inside the enclosure: Φ = Σ h_i·P_i·d_i·k_i + P_DC·(1/η_PSU − 1), h_i = the share of the input power released inside (blank: the loss 1 − η_i; electronics 1; a machine outside the enclosure 0); the second term is the power supply's own loss, present when an AC bus is chosen for it Power supply sizing: P_PSU ≥ margin·P_DC,run/f_derate (the running DC load, duty not applied; f_derate from the manufacturer's curve at θ and altitude) Transformer sizing: S_T ≥ margin·S for the running load; starting peak S_peak = S + max(m_i·P_i/cosφ_i − S_i) ≤ k_T·S_T (the starting load undiversified), so S_T ≥ S_peak/k_T for the start; the rating to buy is the larger of the two Battery sizing: C = I_b·t/(DoD·η_b·k_temp·k_age), I_b = Σ backed P_i·d_i·k_i/(U_b·η_conv); Peukert: I_b·H·(t/H)^(1/k_p) in place of I_b·t, never below I_b·t (the exponent is not valid beyond the rated hour rate H)

Title: Enclosure heat dissipation and fan sizing
Author: unknown    Authored: 2026-10-26
Checker: J. Reviewer    Checked: 2026-10-26
Issued: 2026-10-26    Issue: ELX-ENC-001    Client: Client Co

1. Scope and method
Total heat load inside a control enclosure, the effective surface that can dissipate it for the mounting condition, the internal temperature rise with natural convection and radiation through a stated overall heat-transfer coefficient, the forced-ventilation airflow for a target rise (density corrected for altitude), the fan duty point against a fan curve and the system resistance, optional solar gain, and the cooling-unit capacity needed when the limit cannot be met.
Heat dissipated inside the enclosure, plus any solar gain: P = Σ P_i n_i d_i (+ α I A_sun). Effective cooling surface, each face weighted by how freely it sheds heat: A_eff = Σ f_face × A_face. Temperature rise with natural cooling, one coefficient over the exposed faces (the IEC TR 60890 tables are not used): ΔT = P/(h A_eff). Air flow a fan must move to hold the target rise: V = P/(ρ c_p ΔT_target), with ρ = p(z)/(287.055 T) and c_p = 1006 J/(kg·K). Fan duty point, where the fan curve meets the system resistance (found by bisection): p_fan(V) = p_sys (V/V_ref)². Cooling unit capacity, when the surfaces alone cannot reject the heat: P_cool = P − h A_eff (T_max − T_amb); with T_max at or below T_amb the term is a gain, P_cool = P + h A_eff (T_amb − T_max), and no ventilation airflow or fan duty is given. Sealed enclosure (sealing temperature T_seal entered, no breather), pressure difference across the walls when the air warms or cools from the sealing temperature at constant volume, in absolute temperatures (a rise when T_int > T_seal, a suction when below): Δp = p_site·|T_int/T_seal − 1|. A pressure-compensation element (breather or relief) limits it to its cracking pressure: dp_seal = min(Δp, dp_relief). Force of that pressure on a cover of area A_cover: F_seal = dp_seal·A_cover.

1. Scope and method
A plastic snap-fit joint of one of five forms: a cantilever lug of constant section, of tapered thickness or of tapered width, an annular (closed ring) snap, or a torsion bar. The strain at the critical section against the permissible strain of the plastic (one-time or repeated joining), the deflection force with the secant modulus, the assembly force (inclined entry face) and the separation force (return face) with Coulomb friction, and whether the joint is self-locking.
Cantilever geometry (Euler–Bernoulli): y = K_y·ε·l²/h with K_y = 2/3 (constant section; the design guides print the rounded 0.67); a linear taper of the thickness to r_h = h_tip/h gives K_y = 2·J/φ_max with J = [2r_h − r_h²/2 − ln r_h − 3/2]/(1 − r_h)³ and φ_max = 1 for r_h ≥ 1/2 (otherwise the strain peaks inside the beam at t = (2a − 1)/a, a = 1 − r_h, with φ_max = (1 − t)/(1 − a·t)²); a linear taper of the width to r_b gives K_y = 2·J with J = [1/2 − 2r_b + 3r_b²/2 − r_b²·ln r_b]/(1 − r_b)³ Strain and force of the cantilever: ε = y·h/(K_y·l²); P = b·h²·Es·ε/(6·l·φ_max) = (b·h²/6)·(Es·ε/l) at the root of a constant section; Es is the secant modulus at ε (typed, or interpolated from the W3 points Es(ε)) Annular (derived, free thin ring): nominal hoop strain ε_nom = ΔD/D (the mean diameter D widened by the diametral interference ΔD); the strain checked is the larger of ε = K_ann·ε_nom (K_ann the user's strain factor) and the inner-surface strain ΔD/(D − h); the radial contact force summed round the ring is P = K_F·2π·Es·ε_nom·h·b (hoop force Es·ε_nom·h·b over the radius, K_F the user's optional force factor, 1 if none), b being the effective length of a free ring Torsion bar of radius r with a lever of length a deflected y: φ = y/a; the bar is held at one end (free length l_eff = l) or at both ends with the lever in the middle (l_eff = l/2, two halves in parallel); γ = φ·r/l_eff, equivalent tensile strain ε = γ/(1 + ν), γ_perm = ε_perm·(1 + ν); G = Es/(2(1 + ν)), T = n·G·(π r⁴/2)·φ/l_eff with n = 1 or 2, P = T/a (both ends: P = 4·G·J·φ/(l·a)) Joining forces: W = P·(μ + tan α)/(1 − μ·tan α) on the entry face (angle α to the joining direction) and W′ = P·(μ + tan α′)/(1 − μ·tan α′) on the return face (α′); the joint is self-locking, with an infinite separation force, when α′ ≥ arctan(1/μ); assembly jams when μ·tan α ≥ 1 Utilisation u = ε/ε_perm (FAIL above 1); permissible deflection y_perm = K_y·ε_perm·l²/h (annular ΔD_perm = ε_perm·min(D/K_ann, D − h); torsion y_perm = γ_perm·l_eff·a/r)

3. Assumptions
  A1. Beam theory (Euler–Bernoulli) for the cantilever: a rigid root, a linear taper, a constant secant modulus Es at the design strain over the whole beam, small deflection. The strain is the nominal surface strain at the critical section, with no local concentration from the root fillet; the geometry factors are derived by integration (the 0.67 of the design guides is the constant-section 2/3 rounded). (Euler–Bernoulli beam theory (derived); the guide's closed forms for the constant section are to be checked against the owner's copy.)
  A2. Es and the permissible strain are properties of the plastic at the temperature of use, with the loading (one-time or repeated) and the moisture state of the part. No values are supplied: they come from the plastic maker's data or a W3 material, and the temperature entered records what they were taken at. (No plastics data are shipped.)
  A3. Annular snap: a free, thin, closed ring of effective axial length b. The nominal hoop strain is the diametral interference over the mean diameter, ΔD/D; the strain checked is the larger of that times the user's strain factor K_ann (record snapfit.annular-factor, no default) and the inner-surface strain. The contact force is the hoop force Es·ε_nom·h·b taken round the ring, from the nominal strain only, times the user's optional force factor K_F (record snapfit.annular-force-factor; blank is 1); a tube behind the ring stiffens the joint and raises the force, which only K_F can represent. The strain on the inner (contact) surface is ΔD/(D − h), slightly above the mean-diameter value, so the check takes the larger of the two; a wall above a tenth of the diameter warns that the thin-ring model has run out. (Derived from ring geometry (not a relation of the design guide); compare with the owner's copy of the guide.)
  A4. Torsion bar: a solid round bar of radius r loaded at the end of a lever, the angle small (φ = y/a), held at one end or at both ends with the lever in the middle (twice the strain and four times the stiffness of one end for the same total length). The permissible shear strain is ε_perm·(1 + ν) and the equivalent tensile strain γ/(1 + ν); G = Es/(2(1 + ν)) with the secant modulus at that strain. (γ = φ·r/l and γ_perm ≈ ε_perm·(1 + ν) are the brief's design-guide relations, to be checked against the owner's copy; the torque, the force and the two-ended case are derived.)
  A5. Assembly and separation: a rigid mating part, Coulomb friction μ on the inclined face (α for assembly, α′ for separation), the deflection force P taken at the design deflection; no creep or stress relaxation of the joint in service, no impact (speed) effect. (Friction on an inclined plane; the friction coefficient is the user's record.)

1. Scope and method
Bending of a thin, flat, isotropic plate under a lateral load by Kirchhoff small-deflection theory: circular plates (uniform pressure, central patch, ring load; clamped or simply supported) in closed form; rectangular plates simply supported (uniform pressure or central patch, Navier series) or clamped (Ritz, approximate). Maximum deflection, greatest von Mises stress at the surface, stress and deflection utilisations and the fundamental frequency.
Flexural rigidity: D = E·t³/(12(1 − ν²)); surface stress σ = 6M/t²; von Mises σ_vm = √(σ_x² − σ_x·σ_y + σ_y² + 3τ_xy²) Circular, uniform q, clamped: w = q·a⁴/(64D); σ_edge = 3q·a²/(4t²); simply supported: w = (5 + ν)·q·a⁴/(64(1 + ν)D); σ_centre = 3(3 + ν)·q·a²/(8t²) Circular, central patch (radius c, pressure q) or ring (radius b, force F): w = A + B·r² (+ q·r⁴/64D) inside, A′ + B′·r² + C′·ln(r/a) + k·r²·ln(r/a) outside, k = q·c²/8D or F/8πD; constants from continuity of w, w′, M_r at the load radius and the edge conditions; M_r = −D(w″ + ν·w′/r), M_θ = −D(w′/r + ν·w″) Rectangular, simply supported: w = Σ W_mn·sin(mπx/a)·sin(nπy/b) over odd m, n; W_mn = q_mn/(π⁴D(m²/a² + n²/b²)²); q_mn = 16q/(π²mn) (uniform) or 16q/(π²mn)·sin(mπ/2)·sin(nπ/2)·sin(mπu/2a)·sin(nπv/2b) (patch u × v); M_x = Dπ²ΣW_mn(m²/a² + ν·n²/b²)·sin·sin, M_y likewise, M_xy = −D(1 − ν)π²ΣW_mn(mn/ab)·cos·cos; terms added until w, the centre moments and the corner M_xy each change by < 10⁻⁶ over a doubling (cap 1024 × 1024; the corner sum of a uniform load, which converges like 1/N², is Richardson-extrapolated before it is compared) Rectangular, clamped: Ritz, w = Σ c_ij·X_i(x/a)·Y_j(y/b), X_k(ξ) = ξ²(1 − ξ)²·P₂ₖ(2ξ − 1); c from the minimum of the total potential energy; order K taken as 8, 12, 16, 20, 25, 30 until w and the stress change by < 10⁻⁵ (or the change stops falling) Fundamental frequency: simply supported rectangle ω₁₁ = π²(1/a² + 1/b²)·√(D/(ρt)), f = ω/2π; circular ω = (λ²/a²)·√(D/(ρt)), λ² from the Bessel characteristic equation; clamped rectangle by Rayleigh–Ritz with the same basis Plate checks: σ_vm ≤ f_y/γ_M0; w_max ≤ span/ratio (circular span = diameter, rectangle span = shorter side); WARN w/t > 0.5

3. Assumptions
  A1. Thin plate, small-deflection (Kirchhoff) theory: flat, isotropic, linear elastic, constant thickness; the middle surface is unstrained; shear deformation is neglected (span/t of 10 or more). (Scope of S9; a warning below span/t = 10.)
  A2. The deflection stays small against the thickness: w/t above 0.5 means membrane action stiffens the plate and the small-deflection result is not valid (it overstates the deflection and the bending stress and ignores the membrane stress). (The sheet warns at w/t > 0.5.)
  A3. The edge support is the same all round and ideal: a simply supported edge is held at zero deflection and free to rotate; a clamped edge allows no deflection or rotation. A simply supported rectangle has its corners held down (the corner reaction 2·M_xy is reported); if the corners can lift, the deflection and the central moments are larger than computed. (Navier solution; no elastic edges, no rotational springs.)
  A4. The von Mises stress is that of the plane-stress field at the plate surface from the bending and twisting moments (σ = 6M/t²); the through-thickness shear and the local stress under a concentrated load are not included. The stress governing a simply supported rectangle is at the centre or at a corner (twisting); a clamped rectangle is scanned on a 21 × 21 grid over a quarter of the plate. (Surface stresses; no local effects.)
  A5. The rectangular patch and the circular patch are centred; a ring load is concentric. A load smaller than the thickness is a concentrated load whose local stress thin-plate theory does not give. (Warning `patch-thin`; patches below 1 % (circular), 2 % (simply supported rectangle) or 25 % (clamped rectangle) of the plate size are refused.)
  A6. The clamped rectangle is a Ritz approximation: the deflection converges quickly, the moments and stress more slowly (the sheet reports the order used and the last change; long plates and small patches converge slowest, and the last change understates the error). Compare a critical result with finite elements. (Approximate; the sheet states the order used and the last change.)
  A7. Material properties are inputs taken from the W3 library (E, ν, ρ, f_y), each citing the material record, its revision and the page or table; the generic textbook seeds are indicative. (Record kind material.properties.)
  A8. P (Total force P (force basis: uniform, patch and ring loads; a ring load always uses P)) is taken as 5 kN. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A9. r0 (Patch radius (circular plate, central patch)) is taken as 50 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A10. u (Patch size in x (rectangular plate, central patch)) is taken as 100 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A11. v (Patch size in y (rectangular plate, central patch)) is taken as 100 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A12. r_ring (Ring radius (circular plate, ring load)) is taken as 150 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
