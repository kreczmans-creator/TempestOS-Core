Title: PCB trace width (IPC-2221)
Author: unknown    Authored: 2026-10-27
Checker: J. Reviewer    Checked: 2026-10-27
Issued: 2026-10-27    Issue: ELX-ENC-001    Client: Client Co

1. Scope and method
Calculates the minimum width of a printed-circuit trace for a current and an allowed temperature rise by the legacy IPC-2221 curve-fit, conservative for multilayer boards. It also gives the resistance, voltage drop and I²R loss of the trace as drawn.
Trace cross-section: I = k·ΔT^0.44·A^0.725, so A = [I/(k·ΔT^0.44)]^(1/0.725) with A in mil², k = 0.048 external, 0.024 internal Copper thickness: t = w_Cu·t_oz, t_oz = 1.378 mil per oz/ft² (35 µm, derived) Minimum width: w_min = A/t; utilisation u_w = w_min/w_act Resistivity at the operating temperature: ρ_T = ρ_20·[1 + α·(T_amb + ΔT − 20)] Trace resistance: R = ρ_T·L/(w_act·t); voltage drop ΔV = I·R; loss P = I²·R

3. Assumptions
  A1. The curve-fit is the legacy IPC-2221 relation for a trace with no plane or convective help. (IPC-2221 curve-fit (legacy); IPC-2152 is not implemented.)
  A2. The curve-fit was drawn for 1 to 3 oz copper and currents up to 35 A. (IPC-2221 curve-fit (legacy).)
  A3. The curve-fit was drawn for rises of 10 to 100 K and widths up to 10 mm. (IPC-2221 curve-fit (legacy).)
  A4. The result screens the trace width and is not a thermal analysis of the board. (Scope of the curve-fit.)
  A5. The curve-fit is conservative for multilayer boards. (IPC-2221 curve-fit (legacy).)
  A6. Copper thickness is the nominal 1.378 mil (35 µm) per oz/ft² of finished copper. (Derived: 35 µm / 25.4 µm per mil.)
  A7. Plating build-up on outer layers and etch-back are not modelled. (Scope of the sheet.)
  A8. The resistance uses the trace as drawn at the temperature ambient plus rise. (Derived constants for annealed copper.)
  A9. The resistivity at 20 °C is corrected with a linear temperature coefficient. (Derived constants for annealed copper.)
  A10. Vias, pads, bends and skin effect are ignored. (Scope of the sheet.)
  A11. The current is steady direct current, or RMS current at low frequency. (Scope of the curve-fit.)
  A12. The rise is the allowed rise in the worst case. (Scope of the curve-fit.)
  A13. rho_20 (Copper resistivity at 20 °C (annealed copper, 1.724e-8 Ω·m)) is taken as 0.01724 Ω·mm²/m. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A14. alpha (Temperature coefficient of resistance of copper) is taken as 0.00393 1/K. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A15. k_ext (Curve-fit constant k, external layer) is taken as 0.048. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A16. k_int (Curve-fit constant k, internal layer) is taken as 0.024. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A17. n_dT (Exponent of the temperature rise) is taken as 0.44. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A18. n_A (Exponent of the cross-section) is taken as 0.725. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A19. t_oz (Copper thickness per oz/ft² (35 µm = 1.378 mil, derived)) is taken as 1.378 mil. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)

1. Scope and method
Calculates the demand of a set of loads per supply and the minimum DC power supply, transformer and battery for it. It also gives the heat the loads dissipate for the enclosure calculation.
Demand per load: P_i·d_i·k_i; apparent power P_i·d_i·k_i/cosφ_i (arithmetic sum, conservative); this duty-weighted demand sizes the transformer and the enclosure heat Running load of each load while it is on: P_i·k_i, apparent P_i·k_i/cosφ_i (the duty d is the share of time on, so it is left out: a cable, a bus and a power supply carry the load while it is on) Bus currents: I_3φ = S_3φ,run/(√3·U), I_1φ = S_1φ,run/U, I_DC = P_DC,run/U_DC, from the running loads; when a bus is chosen for the DC power supply its input P_DC,run/η_PSU (power factor 1) is added to that bus Heat dissipated inside the enclosure: Φ = Σ h_i·P_i·d_i·k_i + P_DC·(1/η_PSU − 1), h_i = the share of the input power released inside (blank: the loss 1 − η_i; electronics 1; a machine outside the enclosure 0); the second term is the power supply's own loss, present when an AC bus is chosen for it Power supply sizing: P_PSU ≥ SF·P_DC,run/f_derate (the running DC load, duty not applied; f_derate from the manufacturer's curve at T_amb and altitude) Transformer sizing: S_T ≥ SF·S for the running load; starting peak S_peak = S + max(m_i·P_i/cosφ_i − S_i) ≤ k_T·S_T (the starting load undiversified), so S_T ≥ S_peak/k_T for the start; the rating to buy is the larger of the two Battery sizing: C = I_b·t/(DoD·η_b·k_temp·k_age), I_b = Σ backed P_i·d_i·k_i/(U_b·η_conv); Peukert: I_b·H·(t/H)^(1/k_p) in place of I_b·t, never below I_b·t (the exponent is not valid beyond the rated hour rate H)

Title: Enclosure heat dissipation and fan sizing
Author: unknown    Authored: 2026-10-27
Checker: J. Reviewer    Checked: 2026-10-27
Issued: 2026-10-27    Issue: ELX-ENC-001    Client: Client Co

1. Scope and method
Calculates the heat load of a control enclosure, its natural temperature rise and the airflow for a target rise. It also gives the fan duty point and the cooling-unit capacity needed when the limit cannot be met.
Heat dissipated inside the enclosure, plus any solar gain: P = Σ P_i n_i d_i (+ α I A_sun). Effective cooling surface, each face weighted by how freely it sheds heat: A_eff = Σ f_face × A_face. Temperature rise with natural cooling, one coefficient over the exposed faces (the IEC TR 60890 tables are not used): ΔT = P/(h_conv·A_eff). Air flow a fan must move to hold the target rise: V = P/(ρ c_p ΔT_target), with ρ = p(z)/(287.055 T) and c_p = 1006 J/(kg·K). Fan duty point, where the fan curve meets the system resistance (found by bisection): p_fan(V) = p_sys (V/V_ref)². Cooling unit capacity, when the surfaces alone cannot reject the heat: P_cool = P − h_conv·A_eff·(T_max − T_amb); with T_max at or below T_amb the term is a gain, P_cool = P + h_conv·A_eff·(T_amb − T_max), and no ventilation airflow or fan duty is given. Sealed enclosure (sealing temperature T_seal entered, no breather): Δp = p_site·|T_int/T_seal − 1|, the pressure difference across the walls when the air warms or cools from the sealing temperature at constant volume, in absolute temperatures (a rise when T_int > T_seal, a suction when below); a breather or relief element limits it, dp_seal = min(Δp, dp_relief); the force on a cover of area A_cover is F_seal = dp_seal·A_cover

1. Scope and method
Calculates the strain, deflection force and joining forces of a plastic snap-fit: a cantilever lug, an annular ring or a torsion bar. It checks the strain against the permissible strain and whether the joint is self-locking.
Cantilever geometry (Euler–Bernoulli): y = K_y·ε·l²/h with K_y = 2/3 (constant section; the design guides print the rounded 0.67); a linear taper of the thickness to r_h = h_tip/h gives K_y = 2·J/φ_max with J = [2r_h − r_h²/2 − ln r_h − 3/2]/(1 − r_h)³ and φ_max = 1 for r_h ≥ 1/2 (otherwise the strain peaks inside the beam at t = (2a − 1)/a, a = 1 − r_h, with φ_max = (1 − t)/(1 − a·t)²); a linear taper of the width to r_b gives K_y = 2·J with J = [1/2 − 2r_b + 3r_b²/2 − r_b²·ln r_b]/(1 − r_b)³ Strain and force of the cantilever: ε = y·h/(K_y·l²); F_defl = b·h²·Es·ε/(6·l·φ_max) = (b·h²/6)·(Es·ε/l) at the root of a constant section; Es is the secant modulus at ε (typed, or interpolated from the W3 points Es(ε)) Annular (derived, free thin ring): nominal hoop strain ε_nom = ΔD/D (the mean diameter D widened by the diametral interference ΔD); the strain checked is the larger of ε = K_ann·ε_nom (K_ann the user's strain factor) and the inner-surface strain ΔD/(D − h); the radial contact force summed round the ring is F_defl = K_F·2π·Es·ε_nom·h·b (hoop force Es·ε_nom·h·b over the radius, K_F the user's optional force factor, 1 if none), b being the effective length of a free ring Torsion bar of radius r with a lever of length a deflected y: φ = y/a; the bar is held at one end (free length l_eff = l) or at both ends with the lever in the middle (l_eff = l/2, two halves in parallel); γ = φ·r/l_eff, equivalent tensile strain ε = γ/(1 + ν), γ_perm = ε_perm·(1 + ν); G = Es/(2(1 + ν)), T = n·G·(π r⁴/2)·φ/l_eff with n = 1 or 2, F_defl = T/a (both ends: F_defl = 4·G·J·φ/(l·a)) Joining forces: W = F_defl·(μ + tan α)/(1 − μ·tan α) on the entry face (angle α to the joining direction) and W′ = F_defl·(μ + tan α′)/(1 − μ·tan α′) on the return face (α′); the joint is self-locking, with an infinite separation force, when α′ ≥ arctan(1/μ); assembly jams when μ·tan α ≥ 1 Utilisation and permissible deflection: u = ε/ε_perm (FAIL above 1); y_perm = K_y·ε_perm·l²/h (annular ΔD_perm = ε_perm·min(D/K_ann, D − h); torsion y_perm = γ_perm·l_eff·a/r)

3. Assumptions
  A1. The cantilever follows Euler–Bernoulli beam theory, with a rigid root, a linear taper and small deflection. (Euler–Bernoulli beam theory (derived); the guide's closed forms for the constant section are to be checked against the owner's copy.)
  A2. The secant modulus is constant over the whole beam, at the design strain. (One modulus stands for the stress–strain curve.)
  A3. The strain is the nominal surface strain at the critical section, with no local concentration from the root fillet. (The geometry factors are derived by integration, and the 0.67 of the design guides is the constant-section 2/3 rounded.)
  A4. Es and the permissible strain are the plastic's values for the temperature, loading and moisture of use. (No plastics data are shipped, so they come from the plastic maker's data or a material record.)
  A5. The annular snap is a free, thin, closed ring of effective axial length b. (Derived from ring geometry, not a relation of the design guide, so compare it with the owner's copy of the guide.)
  A6. The nominal hoop strain is the diametral interference over the mean diameter, ΔD/D. (Derived from ring geometry.)
  A7. The strain checked is the larger of K_ann times the nominal strain and the inner-surface strain ΔD/(D − h). (K_ann is the user's strain factor (record snapfit.annular-factor, no default), and the inner-surface strain is slightly above the mean-diameter value.)
  A8. The contact force is the hoop force Es·ε_nom·h·b taken round the ring, times the optional force factor K_F. (K_F is the user's record (blank is 1), and a tube behind the ring stiffens the joint, which only K_F can represent.)
  A9. The torsion bar is solid and round, loaded at a lever end, with a small angle φ = y/a. (γ = φ·r/l and γ_perm ≈ ε_perm·(1 + ν) are the brief's design-guide relations, to be checked against the owner's copy.)
  A10. The bar is held at one end, or at both ends with the lever in the middle. (Held at both ends it has twice the strain and four times the stiffness of one end for the same total length, and the two-ended case is derived.)
  A11. The permissible shear strain is ε_perm·(1 + ν), and the equivalent tensile strain is γ/(1 + ν). (G = Es/(2(1 + ν)) uses the secant modulus at that strain.)
  A12. The mating part is rigid, with Coulomb friction μ on the inclined face (α for assembly, α′ for separation). (Friction on an inclined plane, with the friction coefficient the user's record.)
  A13. The deflection force is taken at the design deflection, with no creep, stress relaxation or impact effect in service. (A static joint model.)

1. Scope and method
Calculates the deflection, von Mises stress and fundamental frequency of a thin, flat, isotropic plate under a lateral load, by Kirchhoff small-deflection theory. It covers circular and rectangular plates, simply supported or clamped, under uniform, patch or ring loads.
Flexural rigidity: D = D_flex = E·t_p³/(12(1 − ν²)); surface stress σ = 6M/t_p²; von Mises σ_vm = √(σ_x² − σ_x·σ_y + σ_y² + 3τ_xy²) Circular, uniform q, clamped: w = q·a⁴/(64D); σ_edge = 3q·a²/(4t_p²); simply supported: w = (5 + ν)·q·a⁴/(64(1 + ν)D); σ_centre = 3(3 + ν)·q·a²/(8t_p²) Circular, central patch (radius c, pressure q) or ring (radius b, force F): w = A + B·r² (+ q·r⁴/64D) inside, A′ + B′·r² + C′·ln(r/a) + k·r²·ln(r/a) outside, k = q·c²/8D or F/8πD; constants from continuity of w, w′, M_r at the load radius and the edge conditions; M_r = −D(w″ + ν·w′/r), M_θ = −D(w′/r + ν·w″) Rectangular, simply supported: w = Σ W_mn·sin(mπx/a)·sin(nπy/b) over odd m, n; W_mn = q_mn/(π⁴D(m²/a² + n²/b²)²); q_mn = 16q/(π²mn) (uniform) or 16q/(π²mn)·sin(mπ/2)·sin(nπ/2)·sin(mπu/2a)·sin(nπv/2b) (patch u × v); M_x = Dπ²ΣW_mn(m²/a² + ν·n²/b²)·sin·sin, M_y likewise, M_xy = −D(1 − ν)π²ΣW_mn(mn/ab)·cos·cos; terms added until w, the centre moments and the corner M_xy each change by < 10⁻⁶ over a doubling (cap 1024 × 1024; the corner sum of a uniform load, which converges like 1/N², is Richardson-extrapolated before it is compared) Rectangular, clamped: Ritz, w = Σ c_ij·X_i(x/a)·Y_j(y/b), X_k(ξ) = ξ²(1 − ξ)²·P₂ₖ(2ξ − 1); c from the minimum of the total potential energy; order K taken as 8, 12, 16, 20, 25, 30 until w and the stress change by < 10⁻⁵ (or the change stops falling) Fundamental frequency: simply supported rectangle ω₁₁ = π²(1/a² + 1/b²)·√(D/(ρ·t_p)), f = ω/2π; circular ω = (λ²/a²)·√(D/(ρ·t_p)), λ² from the Bessel characteristic equation; clamped rectangle by Rayleigh–Ritz with the same basis Plate checks: σ_vm ≤ f_y/γ_M0; w_max ≤ span/ratio (circular span = diameter, rectangle span = shorter side); WARN w/t_p > 0.5

3. Assumptions
  A1. The plate is thin, flat, isotropic, linear elastic and of constant thickness, so Kirchhoff small-deflection theory applies. (The middle surface is unstrained, and shear deformation is neglected, so the sheet is blocked below span/t of 10.)
  A2. The deflection stays small against the thickness, with w/t at most 0.5. (Above 0.5, membrane action stiffens the plate, and the small-deflection result overstates the deflection and the bending stress. The sheet warns.)
  A3. The edge support is the same all round and ideal. (A simply supported edge is held at zero deflection and is free to rotate, and a clamped edge allows no deflection or rotation. There are no elastic edges.)
  A4. A simply supported rectangle has its corners held down. (The sheet reports the corner reaction 2·M_xy. If the corners can lift, the deflection and the central moments are larger than computed.)
  A5. The von Mises stress is that of the plane-stress field at the plate surface, from the bending and twisting moments. (The stress is 6M/t². The through-thickness shear and the local stress under a concentrated load are not included.)
  A6. The stress of a clamped rectangle is scanned on a 21 × 21 grid over a quarter of the plate. (A simply supported rectangle is governed at the centre or at a corner, where the twisting moment acts.)
  A7. The patches are centred and a ring load is concentric. (A load smaller than the thickness is a concentrated load, and the sheet warns (rule `patch-thin`). Patches below 1 % (circular), 2 % (simply supported rectangle) or 25 % (clamped rectangle) of the plate size are refused.)
  A8. The clamped rectangle is a Ritz approximation, so its moments and stress converge more slowly than its deflection. (The sheet reports the order used and the last change, which understates the error for long plates and small patches. Compare a critical result with finite elements.)
  A9. The material properties E, ν, ρ and f_y are inputs, each citing its source when taken from the material library. (The generic textbook seeds in the library are indicative.)
  A10. F (Total force (force basis: uniform, patch and ring loads; a ring load always uses the force)) is taken as 5 kN. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A11. r0 (Patch radius (circular plate, central patch)) is taken as 50 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A12. u (Patch size in x (rectangular plate, central patch)) is taken as 100 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A13. v (Patch size in y (rectangular plate, central patch)) is taken as 100 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A14. r_ring (Ring radius (circular plate, ring load)) is taken as 150 mm. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A15. defl_ratio (Deflection limit as span/ratio (span = diameter of a circular plate, shorter side of a rectangle)) is taken as 250. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
