1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Friction and fitting losses through series pipe segments at a given flow, the static head, the system curve, the duty point against a pump curve entered as points, the affinity-law shift for a speed or impeller change, and the NPSH available at the pump suction.
Design flow from the heat carried, when a heat load is given in place of a flow: Q = Φ/(ρ c_p ΔT). Mean velocity in the pipe: v = Q/(πD²/4). Reynolds number, which decides laminar or turbulent flow: Re = ρvD/μ. Friction factor for laminar flow (Re < 2300): f = 64/Re. Friction factor for turbulent flow, Colebrook–White by fixed-point iteration (at most 100 steps) from the Swamee–Jain value, which also cross-checks it: 1/√f = −2 log10(ε/3.7D + 2.51/(Re√f)). Head lost in the pipe and its fittings: h_f = f (L + L_eq)/D · v²/2g + K v²/2g. System curve, with friction scaled from the design flow as Q² (a closed circuit has Σdz = 0, the rise in the flow being recovered in the return): H_sys(Q) = Σdz + h_out + Σh_f(Q). Water held in the pipes: V_pipe = Σ π/4·D²·L. Duty point, where the pump and system curves cross (found by bisection): H_pump(Q) = H_sys(Q). Pump affinity laws for a change of speed n: Q ∝ n; H ∝ n². For a change of impeller diameter D, by the choice: a trimmed impeller of the same casing, Q ∝ D and H ∝ D² (the default, the usual practice for a trim); or a geometrically similar pump scaled in all dimensions, Q ∝ D³ and H ∝ D². Speed and diameter combine as Q ∝ n·D^a, H ∝ n²·D², a = 1 or 3. Net positive suction head available, to compare with what the pump requires: NPSH_a = p_atm/ρg + z_s − h_f,suction − p_v/ρg, with p_v from the water correlation or, for a custom fluid, from the vapour pressure entered. The friction h_f,suction is that of the segments marked 'suction' only. Shaft torque for the motor: T = P/ω with P the larger of the design-flow shaft power and the duty-point shaft power, at the speed n_pump; when a speed or diameter ratio shifts the duty, the shifted duty power at the shifted speed n_pump·n₂/n₁.
  Ref: Crane Technical Paper 410 — Flow of fluids through valves, fittings and pipe
  Ref: Çengel & Cimbala, Fluid Mechanics Ch. 8 — Internal flow: friction factor, minor losses, pump duty
  Ref: CIBSE Guide C Section 4 — Flow of fluids in pipes and ducts (principles)
  Ref: Kell, J. Chem. Eng. Data 20(1), 1975 — Density of liquid water

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Steady, incompressible, fully developed flow; all segments carry the full design flow in series. (Darcy–Weisbach.)
  A2. Fitting losses scale with velocity head (K method) or add pipe length (equivalent-length method); both may be used on one segment. (Crane TP-410 practice.)
  A3. The system curve scales friction losses with Q² from the design flow (turbulent assumption); the static part is independent of flow. (H_sys = H_static + k Q².)
  A4. Water properties are from published correlations at atmospheric pressure (0–100 °C); any other fluid is entered with its own density and viscosity and their provenance. (Kell 1975; Vogel fit; Hyland & Wexler 1983.)
  A5. h_out (Pressure head required at the outlet) is taken as 0 m. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A6. v_max (Velocity limit (warning)) is taken as 3 m/s. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A7. n_ratio (Speed ratio n₂/n₁ (affinity)) is taken as 1. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A8. D_ratio (Impeller diameter ratio D₂/D₁ (affinity; the law is chosen below)) is taken as 1. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A9. D_law (Law for the diameter ratio: a trimmed impeller of the same casing (Q ∝ D, H ∝ D²) or a geometrically similar pump (Q ∝ D³, H ∝ D²)) is taken as impeller trim (Q ∝ n·D, H ∝ n²·D²). (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A10. eta_pump (Pump efficiency at duty (for shaft power)) is taken as 0.7. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A11. p_atm (Atmospheric pressure (NPSH)) is taken as 101.325 kPa. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A12. z_s (Static head at the pump suction (+ flooded, − lift)) is taken as 0 m. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A13. The specific heat of water is taken as 4186 J/(kg·K), a rounded constant for 10–90 °C. (Assumed constant; enter cp_load to use another value.)
