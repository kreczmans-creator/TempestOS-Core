Title: U-values and room heat loss
Author: unknown    Authored: 2026-10-27
Checker: J. Reviewer    Checked: 2026-10-27
Issued: 2026-10-27    Issue: HTG-001    Client: Client Co

1. Scope and method
Calculates the U-values of built-up elements and the steady-state design heat loss of one room. It gives the fabric and ventilation loss, the room load for sizing emitters, the heat loss coefficient and an optional check against target U-values.
Element U-value: U = 1/(R_si + Σ d/λ + R_g + R_se), or the user's U override for ground floors and glazing, or the ground floor record U_g for a floor without layers or an override Element fabric loss: Φ_i = A·U·f·(T_int − θ_adj), with θ_adj = θ_e where the element faces outside Thermal bridges: Φ_tb = y_tb·Σ(A·f)_ext·(T_int − θ_e) on the elements facing outside Fabric total: Φ_T = ΣΦ_i + Φ_tb Ventilation loss: q_v = n·V; Φ_V = 0.34·q_v·(T_int − θ_e), or (T_int − T_supply) through an MVHR supply, plus 0.34·n_inf·V·(T_int − θ_e) infiltration with MVHR Room load: Φ_HL = Φ_T + Φ_V (CIBSE DHDG method: × (1 + additional allowance)) Heat loss coefficient: HLC = Φ_HL/(T_int − θ_e); loss per floor area Φ_HL/A_floor U-value check: U against the target U of each element (the global target applies to built-up elements facing outside only; an element's own target applies wherever it faces)

3. Assumptions
  A1. Steady state: every temperature is constant, with no solar or internal gains, no thermal mass and no dynamic simulation. (Design heat loss, not an annual energy estimate.)
  A2. Layers are homogeneous and in series, with one-dimensional heat flow. (BS EN ISO 6946:2017 homogeneous-layer calculation.)
  A3. Bridged layers such as timber studs, mortar joints and fixings are not corrected for. (BS EN ISO 6946:2017 homogeneous-layer calculation.)
  A4. A thermal-bridge allowance y_tb is added instead of correcting bridged layers. (Stated.)
  A5. A blank Rsi is the conventional BS EN ISO 6946 value for the element's heat-flow direction. (Small conventional BS EN ISO 6946 set (three values), not a table of design data.)
  A6. The conventional Rsi is 0.13 m²K/W horizontal, 0.10 upward and 0.17 downward. (Small conventional BS EN ISO 6946 set (three values), not a table of design data.)
  A7. A blank Rse is 0.04 m²K/W for an element facing outside. (Small conventional BS EN ISO 6946 set, not a table of design data.)
  A8. Where an element faces another space, a blank Rse is the same as Rsi. (Small conventional BS EN ISO 6946 set, not a table of design data.)
  A9. The global target U applies to built-up elements facing outside only. (Scope of the target check: an internal partition or floor is checked only against a target entered on its own row.)
  A10. Ground-floor and glazing U-values are entered by the user as an override. (Stated limit.)
  A11. A ground floor U-value record fills U_g for a floor element without layers or an override. (Stated limit: the record's value is the user's, from its table.)
  A12. The calculator does not model ground heat transfer. (Stated limit.)
  A13. The air constant 0.34 Wh/(m³·K) is the conventional rounded ρ·c_p of air. (Derived: about 1.2 kg/m³ × 1005 J/(kg·K) ÷ 3600, not read from a table.)
  A14. Design temperatures, conductivities, surface resistances and the thermal-bridge allowance are the user's own records. (No table from a standard or a manufacturer is shipped.)
  A15. method (Calculation method (changes the citations; the DHDG option adds the allowance below)) is taken as BS EN 12831-1 elemental. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A16. y_tb (Thermal-bridge allowance on the area facing outside) is taken as 0 W/(m²·K). (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A17. f_add (Additional allowance on the room load (CIBSE DHDG method only)) is taken as 0 %. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A18. Conventional BS EN ISO 6946 surface resistances (R_si 0.13 horizontal, 0.10 upward, 0.17 downward; R_se 0.04 outside, or the same as R_si where the element faces another space; m²K/W) are used where blank, for: Cavity wall. (Small conventional set; enter your own where the construction needs other values.)

1. Scope and method
Calculates the friction and fitting losses of series pipe segments at a given flow, the system curve and the duty point against a pump curve. It also gives the affinity-law shift for a speed or impeller change, and the NPSH.
Design flow and velocity: Q = Φ/(ρ c_p ΔT) when a heat load is given in place of a flow; v = Q/(πD²/4) Reynolds number and friction factor: Re = ρvD/μ; f = 64/Re for laminar flow (Re < 2300); Colebrook–White 1/√f = −2 log10(ε/3.7D + 2.51/(Re√f)) for turbulent flow, by fixed-point iteration (at most 100 steps) from the Swamee–Jain value, which also cross-checks it Head lost in a segment: h_f = f (L + L_eq)/D · v²/2g + K v²/2g System curve: H_sys(Q) = Σdz + h_out + Σh_f,lam·(Q/Q₀) + Σh_f,other·(Q/Q₀)², with a laminar segment's friction scaled as Q and every other friction and fitting loss as Q² (a closed circuit has Σdz = 0) Water held in the pipes: V_pipe = Σ π/4·D²·L Duty point: H_pump(Q) = H_sys(Q), every crossing found by a scan and refined by bisection, the stable one nearest the design flow reported; pump affinity laws Q ∝ n·D^a, H ∝ n²·D², with a = 1 for a trimmed impeller (the default) and a = 3 for a geometrically similar pump Net positive suction head available: NPSH_a = p_atm/ρg + z_s − h_f,suction − p_v/ρg, with h_f,suction that of the 'suction' segments at the larger of the design flow and the duty flow Shaft torque for the motor: T = P/ω, with P the larger of the design-flow and duty-point shaft power at the speed n_pump, or the shifted duty power at the shifted speed n_pump·n₂/n₁

1. Scope and method
Calculates the torque, speed and power a motor needs to move a load through a mechanism, and checks a candidate motor against them. A continuously running rotary load is sized from its steady torque, speed and run-up time.
Linear mechanism: k = p/(2π i) (screw), D/(2 i) (belt), D_p/(2 i) (rack), in metres per motor radian; J_refl = m k²/η; T_load = F k/η, with F = m g (sin θ + μ cos θ) + F_p Rotary mechanism through a gearbox: J_refl = J_L/(i² η); T_load = T_L/(i η) Move profile: trapezoidal v_pk = s/(t_move − t_acc), a = v_pk/t_acc; S-curve (sine acceleration, same peak speed) a_pk = (π/2) a_trap Peak torque: T_acc = J_total α; T_pk = T_acc + T_load; T_dec = −T_acc + T_load; the check is SF·max(|T_pk|, |T_dec|) ≤ T_peak; braking power P_brake = max(0, −T_dec·ω_pk) Thermal duty: T_rms = √(Σ T_i² t_i / t_cycle) over the whole cycle, the dwell at T_hold = m g sin θ·k for a linear mechanism not held by a brake; P_rms = ω_pk √{[t_acc·(2/3)(T_load² + T_acc²) + T_load² t_const]/t_cycle}, and for the S-curve the acceleration term is t_acc·[(3/4) T_load² + (5/16) T_acc²] Output side and design torque: T_alt = (T_pk − T_dec)/2 (0 in continuous duty); T_out = T·i·η and n_out = n_pk/i for a rotary mechanism; F_ax_pk = F_load + m a_pk for a ball screw; T_design on the basis T_basis (load peak max(|T_pk|, |T_dec|), T_peak, SF_c·T_rated or T_lim) Continuous rotary duty: ω = 2π n_L i/60, n_pk = n_L i; T_load = T_L/(i η), T_rms = T_load; T_pk = J_total ω/t_start + T_load; P_cont = T_load ω, P_rms = P_cont Rated power of the candidate motor: P_rated = T_rated·2π n_rated/60 (without n_rated there is no P_rated); the check is P_cont ≤ P_rated, u_power = P_cont/P_rated

3. Assumptions
  A1. The transmission is rigid. (Servo-sizing practice.)
  A2. The mechanism efficiency applies to the driving direction and is also used in the deceleration phase. (Servo-sizing practice.)
  A3. This is conservative for the peak and slightly pessimistic for the RMS torque. (Servo-sizing practice.)
  A4. The friction force is Coulomb friction on the moved mass. (Load model.)
  A5. Viscous and breakaway friction are not modelled unless included in F_p. (Load model.)
  A6. In continuous duty the load torque is constant at the stated speed. (Run-up idealisation for pumps and fans.)
  A7. The motor accelerates the whole inertia linearly to speed in t_start while carrying the full load torque, which is conservative. (Run-up idealisation for pumps and fans.)
  A8. Continuous duty ignores the move distance, move time, acceleration fraction, dwell and profile. (Run-up idealisation for pumps and fans.)
  A9. The S-curve profile has sine-shaped acceleration over the same acceleration time. (Jerk-limited profile idealisation.)
  A10. The peak acceleration of the S-curve profile is π/2 times the trapezoidal value. (Jerk-limited profile idealisation.)
  A11. profile (Motion profile) is taken as trapezoidal. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
