1. SCOPE AND METHOD
U-values of built-up elements from their layers and surface resistances, and the steady-state design heat loss of one room: fabric loss through each element to its adjacent temperature, a thermal-bridge allowance, ventilation loss (through the outside temperature or an MVHR supply temperature), the room load for sizing emitters, the heat loss coefficient and the loss per floor area; optionally checked against target U-values.
Element U-value: U = 1/(R_si + Σ d/λ + R_g + R_se), or the user's U override for ground floors and glazing Element fabric loss: Φ_i = A·U·f·(θ_int − θ_adj), θ_adj = θ_e where the element faces outside Thermal bridges: Φ_tb = y_tb·Σ(A·f)_ext·(θ_int − θ_e) on the elements facing outside Fabric total: Φ_T = ΣΦ_i + Φ_tb Ventilation flow: q_v = n·V Ventilation loss: Φ_V = 0.34·q_v·(θ_int − θ_e), or (θ_int − θ_supply) through an MVHR supply, plus 0.34·n_inf·V·(θ_int − θ_e) infiltration with MVHR Room load: Φ_HL = Φ_T + Φ_V (CIBSE DHDG method: × (1 + additional allowance)) Heat loss coefficient: HLC = Φ_HL/(θ_int − θ_e); loss per floor area Φ_HL/A_floor U-value check: U against the target U of each element (the global target applies to built-up elements facing outside only; an element's own target applies wherever it faces)
  Ref: BS EN ISO 6946 homogeneous layers and surface resistances (clause numbers to be checked against the owner's copy) — Building components — thermal resistance and thermal transmittance
  Ref: BS EN 12831-1 cl. 6.3.2 (clause number to be checked against the owner's copy) — Heating systems in buildings — design heat load: transmission heat loss
  Ref: BS EN 12831-1 cl. 6.3.3 (clause number to be checked against the owner's copy) — Heating systems in buildings — design heat load: ventilation heat loss

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Steady state: every temperature is constant, with no solar or internal gains, no thermal mass and no dynamic simulation. (Design heat loss, not an annual energy estimate.)
  A2. Layers are homogeneous and in series (one-dimensional flow); no bridged layers (timber studs, mortar joints, fixings) are corrected for: a thermal-bridge allowance y_tb is added instead. (BS EN ISO 6946 homogeneous-layer calculation.)
  A3. A blank Rsi is the conventional BS EN ISO 6946 value for the element's heat-flow direction (0.13 m²K/W horizontal, 0.10 upward, 0.17 downward) and a blank Rse is 0.04 m²K/W for an element facing outside (no adjacent temperature, or one at or below θ_e), or the same as Rsi where it faces another space. (Small conventional BS EN ISO 6946 set (three values), not a table of design data.)
  A4. The global target U applies to built-up elements facing outside only: an internal partition or floor has no outside target, so it is checked only against a target entered on its own row. (Scope of the target check.)
  A5. Ground-floor U-values and glazing U-values are entered by the user (override); the calculator does not model ground heat transfer. (Stated limit.)
  A6. The air constant 0.34 Wh/(m³·K) is the conventional rounded ρ·c_p of air (about 1.2 kg/m³ × 1005 J/(kg·K) ÷ 3600); it is derived, not read from a table. (Derived.)
  A7. Design temperatures, conductivities, surface resistances and the thermal-bridge allowance are user records from the user's own licensed copies or manufacturers' data; none is embedded. (No table from a standard or a manufacturer is shipped.)
  A8. method (Calculation method (changes the citations; the DHDG option adds the allowance below)) is taken as BS EN 12831-1 elemental. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A9. y_tb (Thermal-bridge allowance on the area facing outside) is taken as 0 W/(m^2*K). (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A10. f_add (Additional allowance on the room load (CIBSE DHDG method only)) is taken as 0 %. (Sheet default value; no project figure given.)
  A11. Conventional BS EN ISO 6946 surface resistances (R_si 0.13 horizontal, 0.10 upward, 0.17 downward; R_se 0.04 outside, or the same as R_si where the element faces another space; m²K/W) are used where blank, for: Cavity wall. 1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Friction and fitting losses through series pipe segments at a given flow, the static head, the system curve, the duty point against a pump curve entered as points, the affinity-law shift for a speed or impeller change, and the NPSH available at the pump suction.
Design flow from the heat carried, when a heat load is given in place of a flow: Q = Φ/(ρ c_p ΔT). Mean velocity in the pipe: v = Q/(πD²/4). Reynolds number, which decides laminar or turbulent flow: Re = ρvD/μ. Friction factor for laminar flow (Re < 2300): f = 64/Re. Friction factor for turbulent flow, Colebrook–White by fixed-point iteration (at most 100 steps) from the Swamee–Jain value, which also cross-checks it: 1/√f = −2 log10(ε/3.7D + 2.51/(Re√f)). Head lost in the pipe and its fittings: h_f = f (L + L_eq)/D · v²/2g + K v²/2g. System curve, with friction scaled from the design flow as Q² (a closed circuit has Σdz = 0, the rise in the flow being recovered in the return): H_sys(Q) = Σdz + h_out + Σh_f(Q). Water held in the pipes: V_pipe = Σ π/4·D²·L. Duty point, where the pump and system curves cross (found by bisection): H_pump(Q) = H_sys(Q). Pump affinity laws for a change of speed n or impeller diameter D: Q ∝ n D³; H ∝ n² D². Net positive suction head available, to compare with what the pump requires: NPSH_a = p_atm/ρg + z_s − h_f,suction − p_v/ρg.

Title: Motor and actuator sizing
Author: unknown    Authored: 2026-10-26

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Torque, speed and power a motor needs to move a load through a mechanism on a point-to-point cycle: the load reflected to the motor shaft, the acceleration from the motion profile, peak torque during acceleration, RMS torque over the cycle (thermal duty), peak speed, inertia ratio, and a check of a candidate motor's rated and peak torque and speed with a safety margin. A rotary load that runs continuously (a pump, a fan) is sized instead from its steady torque and speed and a run-up time.
Motor-to-load ratio of a linear mechanism, in metres per motor radian: k = p/(2π i) (screw); D/(2 i) (belt); D_p/(2 i) (rack). Load inertia reflected to the motor through a linear mechanism: J_refl = m k²/η. Load torque at the motor through a linear mechanism: T_load = F k/η, with F = m g (sin θ + μ cos θ) + F_p. Reflected inertia and torque of a rotary load through a gearbox: J_refl = J_L/(i² η); T_load = T_L/(i η). Move profile, trapezoidal 1/3–1/3–1/3 (adjustable): v_pk = s/(t_move − t_acc); a = v_pk/t_acc. Move profile, S-curve (sine acceleration, same peak speed): a_pk = (π/2) a_trap. Torque to accelerate the total inertia, and the peak torque: T_acc = J_total α; T_pk = T_acc + T_load. Alternating torque amplitude of a point-to-point cycle about T_load, for a shaft's fatigue: T_alt = (T_pk − T_dec)/2 (0 in continuous duty). Torque behind the ratio on the output shaft of a rotary mechanism: T_out = T·i·η (mean, alternating and peak), n_out = n_pk/i. Peak axial thrust of a ball screw on its support bearings: F_ax_pk = F_load + m a_pk (0 for other mechanisms). RMS (thermal) torque over the whole cycle, dwell included: T_rms = √(Σ T_i² t_i / t_cycle). RMS mechanical power over the cycle, the thermal-equivalent continuous power a supply sees (regeneration counts as power; the instantaneous peak P_pk is not a continuous demand), trapezoidal profile: P_rms = ω_pk √{[t_acc·(2/3)(T_load² + T_acc²) + T_load² t_const]/t_cycle}; for the S-curve the acceleration term becomes t_acc·[(3/4) T_load² + (5/16) T_acc²]. Continuous rotary duty (a pump or fan), speed at the motor: ω = 2π n_L i/60; n_pk = n_L i. Continuous rotary duty, load torque and RMS torque: T_load = T_L/(i η); T_rms = T_load. Peak torque during the run-up, with full load throughout (conservative): T_pk = J_total ω/t_start + T_load. Continuous power: P_cont = T_load ω; P_rms = P_cont. Rated shaft power of the candidate motor, from its rated torque and its rated speed n_rated (not the running speed), the shaft power its supply must be sized for; without n_rated there is no P_rated and the sheet warns: P_rated = T_rated·2π n_rated/60.
  Ref: Hughes & Drury, Electric Motors and Drives Ch. on motor and drive selection — Load torque, inertia referred to the motor, duty cycles
  Ref: Manufacturer servo-sizing guides (Kollmorgen, Parker) — Reflected inertia, RMS torque, inertia ratio practice

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Rigid transmission; the mechanism efficiency applies to the driving direction and is also used in the deceleration phase (conservative for peak, slightly pessimistic for RMS). (Servo-sizing practice.)
  A2. The friction force is Coulomb friction on the moved mass; viscous and breakaway friction are not modelled unless included in F_p. (Load model.)
  A3. Continuous duty: the load torque is constant at the stated speed and the motor accelerates the whole inertia linearly to speed in t_start while carrying the full load torque (conservative); the move distance, move time, acceleration fraction, dwell and profile are ignored. (Run-up idealisation for pumps and fans.)
  A4. The S-curve profile has sine-shaped acceleration over the same acceleration time, so its peak acceleration is π/2 times the trapezoidal value. (Jerk-limited profile idealisation.)
