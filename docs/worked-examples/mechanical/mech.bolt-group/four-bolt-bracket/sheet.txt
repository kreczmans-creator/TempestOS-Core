1. SCOPE AND METHOD
A pattern of bolts in a plane under in-plane forces (with eccentricity, so torsion), out-of-plane moments and axial load: the shear and tension in every bolt by the elastic (area-weighted) method, the governing bolt, and an optional lever-model prying addition.
Centroid of the bolt group, each bolt weighted by its area: x_c = Σ A x / Σ A (y_c likewise). Torsion about the centroid from the eccentric load: T = F_y (e_x − x_c) − F_x (e_y − y_c) + T_z. Polar moment of the bolt areas about the centroid: J = Σ A r². Shear on each bolt, its direct share plus the torsion (vector sum): v = A (F/ΣA) + T·A·r/J. Tension on each bolt, its direct share plus the moments: t = A N/ΣA + M_x A y'/Σ A y'² + M_y A x'/Σ A x'². Tension with prying, when the distances a and b are given: t_p = t (1 + b/a). Combined shear and tension, circular interaction (elastic method, Shigley-style): (v/V_R)² + (t_p/T_R)² ≤ 1. Combined shear and tension, EN 1993-1-8 linear form (each is also checked alone; the highest governs): v/V_R + t_p/(1.4 T_R) ≤ 1.
  Ref: EN 1993-1-8:2005 §3.6, §3.8 — Design resistance of individual fasteners; elastic distribution in a bolt group
  Ref: AISC Steel Construction Manual Part 7 — Eccentrically loaded bolt groups — elastic method

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. The connected parts are rigid; bolt forces vary linearly with distance from the centroid (elastic method). (Conservative compared with plastic / instantaneous-centre methods.)
  A2. Resistances are per bolt as entered, with their source (a design-code capacity or a manufacturer value). (No code capacity is computed here.)
  A3. Prying uses a simple lever: the bolt tension rises by the factor (1 + b/a). (An optional, conservative first estimate.)
