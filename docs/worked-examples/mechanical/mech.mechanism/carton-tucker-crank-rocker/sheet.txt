1. SCOPE AND METHOD
A single-degree-of-freedom planar mechanism driven at constant speed: a four-bar linkage or an offset slider-crank (both circuits, position, velocity and acceleration over a sweep of the input angle, Grashof class, transmission angle against a minimum, toggle and dead-centre positions, loop-closure residual, input torque by virtual work), or a cam with a translating follower. Rigid links, no clearance, no friction.
Four-bar: a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} = d + c·e^{jθ₄}; ℓ = |A − O₄|, cos γ = (c² + ℓ² − b²)/(2cℓ), θ₄ = φ ∓ γ (φ the direction O₄→A; open: −, crossed: +); θ₃ from B − A Four-bar velocity and acceleration: differentiated loop, 2×2 system with det = b·c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄); ω₃ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₄ − θ₂)/(b·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)), ω₄ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₃ − θ₂)/(c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)); α from the second derivative at constant ω₂ Grashof: s + l < p + q (crank-rocker, double-crank or double-rocker by the shortest link), = at the change point, > non-Grashof; transmission angle cos μ = (b² + c² − ℓ²)/(2bc) folded to 0 … 90°; toggle where ℓ = b + c or |b − c| Slider-crank: x = r·cosθ + σ·√(l² − (r·sinθ − e)²) (σ = +1 open, −1 crossed); dx/dθ = −r·sinθ − r·cosθ·(r·sinθ − e)/(x − r·cosθ); dead centres where crank and rod are collinear; μ = 90° − |rod angle to the slide line| Input torque by virtual work: T_in·ω_in = Σ F·v; four-bar T_in = (T_L + J·α₄)·ω₄/ω₂; slider T_in = (m·a + F_L)·dx/dθ Loop closure: |a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} − c·e^{jθ₄} − d| (slider: |r·e^{jθ} + l·e^{jθ₃} − (x + j·e)|) asserted below 1e-9 mm at every step Cam, per segment with x = (θ − θ₀)/β: rise s = s₀ + h·f(x), return s = s₀ − h·f(x); cycloidal f = x − sin(2πx)/(2π); 3-4-5 f = 10x³ − 15x⁴ + 6x⁵; 4-5-6-7 f = 35x⁴ − 84x⁵ + 70x⁶ − 20x⁷; modified trapezoid from its acceleration pieces (C_v = 2, C_a = 8π/(π + 2), C_j = 32π²/(π + 2)) Cam SVAJ at constant ω: v = ω·s′, a = ω²·s″, j = ω³·s‴ (′ = d/dθ); peak coefficients C_v = max f′, C_a = max |f″|, C_j = max |f‴|, so v_pk = C_v·h·ω/β Pressure angle (roller, offset e, R_p = R_b + R_r): φ = arctan[(s′ − e)/(√(R_p² − e²) + s)], which for e = 0 is arctan[s′/(R_p + s)]; the base circle that holds φ ≤ φ_max has R_p,req = √(s₀,req² + e²) with s₀,req = max(|s′ − e|/tan φ_max − s) Radius of curvature of the pitch curve P(θ) = Rot(−θ)·(e, √(R_p² − e²) + s): ρ = |P′|³/(P′ × P″), convex where positive; undercut where ρ_min ≤ R_r (FAIL), none where the curve is nowhere convex; flat-faced follower ρ = R_b + s + s″ must stay positive and the face must reach |s′| Cam follower force F = m·a + k·s + F_pre + F_ext (the follower leaves the cam where F < 0); input torque by virtual work T = F·s′ (T·ω = F·v)
  Ref: Norton, Design of Machinery Ch. 4, 6, 7 — Position, velocity and acceleration analysis; transmission angle; Grashof condition
  Ref: Uicker, Pennock and Shigley, Theory of Machines and Mechanisms Ch. 4–6 — Loop-closure position analysis; velocity and acceleration of planar linkages

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Rigid links, a planar mechanism with one degree of freedom, no joint clearance and no friction; the input turns at a constant speed. (Addendum B §B6 M9 limits.)
  A2. The position is the closed-form solution of the vector loop; the open and crossed circuits are the two branches (θ₄ = φ − γ and φ + γ for the four-bar, σ = +1 and −1 for the slider) and the sweep stays on the chosen branch. Angles at which the linkage cannot be assembled, or at a toggle where the velocity system is singular, are left out of the table and counted. (Norton, Design of Machinery, position analysis of the four-bar and the offset slider-crank.)
  A3. Input torque is by virtual work from the output load and the inertia of the output element only (four-bar: the output link's moment of inertia about its pivot; slider: the slider mass); the crank and the coupler or connecting rod are massless. (No link-inertia or gravity terms: add the link masses to the output element for a first allowance.)
  A4. The peaks (speeds, accelerations, torque, transmission angle, swing) are those of the continuous motion over the swept range, found by a dense scan refined by golden section; the printed table (steps N, up to 3600) only samples it. (Peaks do not depend on the table's step count.)
  A5. Cam: a translating follower on a cam turning at constant speed; the follower mass, a linear return spring (rate and preload at the base circle, for example linked from M7) and a constant external load make the force the cam must apply, F = m·a + k·s + F_pre + F_ext; contact is lost where it falls below zero. The segment table must add to 360° and close; pressure angle and undercut are checked for the roller follower (R_p = R_b + R_r), the positive profile radius for the flat-faced one. (Norton, Design of Machinery, cam design: the four classical laws, pressure angle and radius of curvature.)
  A6. Follower force (four-bar: the tangential force at the output pin, (T_L + J·α₄)/c; slider: the rod force on the slider, m·a + F_L) is the force the mechanism must apply to the output element. (Rigid body, frictionless.)
  A7. steps (Intervals N of the table (N + 1 rows, a whole number, at most 3600; the peaks do not depend on it)) is taken as 72. (5° table step.)
  A8. mu_req (Minimum transmission angle required (linkages)) is taken as 40 deg. (Default minimum transmission angle; set it from your design rule.)
  A9. load_dir (External load: constant direction, or always opposing the output motion) is taken as Opposes motion. (The flap resists the blade in whichever direction it moves.)
  A10. T_load (External load torque on the output link (four-bar), positive against ω₄) is taken as 4 N*m. (Example flap-folding torque at the rocker shaft; measure it on the station.)
  A11. J_out (Moment of inertia of the output link about its pivot (four-bar)) is taken as 0.012 kg*m^2. (Example: rocker arm and blade about the rocker pivot; take it from the CAD mass properties.)
