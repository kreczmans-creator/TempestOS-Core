1. Scope and method
Calculates the position, velocity, acceleration, input torque and forces of a one-degree-of-freedom planar four-bar, slider-crank or cam mechanism. The input turns at constant speed, and the links are rigid, with no clearance and no friction.
Four-bar position: a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} = d + c·e^{jθ₄}, with ℓ = |A − O₄|, cos γ = (c² + ℓ² − b²)/(2cℓ) and θ₄ = φ ∓ γ (φ the direction O₄→A; open: −, crossed: +); θ₃ from B − A Four-bar velocity and acceleration: differentiated loop, 2×2 system with det = b·c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄); ω₃ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₄ − θ₂)/(b·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)), ω₄ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₃ − θ₂)/(c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)); α from the second derivative at constant ω₂ Grashof class and transmission angle: s + l < p + q (crank-rocker, double-crank or double-rocker by the shortest link), = at the change point, > non-Grashof; transmission angle cos μ = (b² + c² − ℓ²)/(2bc) folded to 0 … 90°; toggle where ℓ = b + c or |b − c| Slider-crank position: x = r·cosθ + σ·√(l² − (r·sinθ − e)²) (σ = +1 open, −1 crossed); dx/dθ = −r·sinθ − r·cosθ·(r·sinθ − e)/(x − r·cosθ); dead centres where crank and rod are collinear; μ = 90° − |rod angle to the slide line| Input torque and forces: T_in·ω_in = Σ F·v by virtual work; four-bar T_in = (T_L + J·α₄)·ω₄/ω₂, tangential pin force P = (T_L + J·α₄)/c and coupler force P/sin μ; slider T_in = (m·a + F_L)·dx/dθ, slide-direction force F_x = m·a + F_L, rod force F_x/cos θ₃ and guide reaction F_x·tan θ₃; RMS torque and mean power over the sweep by Simpson's rule Assembly limits and loop closure: four-bar toggles at cos θ₂ = (d² + a² − ℓ²)/(2ad), ℓ = b + c or |b − c|; slider sin θ = (e ± l)/r; the sweep is cut at them and the unbounded peaks are not reported; closure |a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} − c·e^{jθ₄} − d| (slider: |r·e^{jθ} + l·e^{jθ₃} − (x + j·e)|) below 1e-9 mm at every step Cam motion: per segment with x = (θ − θ₀)/β, rise s = s₀ + h·f(x), return s = s₀ − h·f(x); cycloidal f = x − sin(2πx)/(2π); 3-4-5 f = 10x³ − 15x⁴ + 6x⁵; 4-5-6-7 f = 35x⁴ − 84x⁵ + 70x⁶ − 20x⁷; modified trapezoid from its acceleration pieces (C_v = 2, C_a = 8π/(π + 2), C_j = 32π²/(π + 2)); SVAJ at constant ω: v = ω·s′, a = ω²·s″, j = ω³·s‴ (′ = d/dθ), so v_pk = C_v·h·ω/β Cam contact: roller pressure angle φ = arctan[(s′ − e)/(√(R_p² − e²) + s)] with R_p = R_b + R_r, base circle R_p,req = √(s₀,req² + e²) with s₀,req = max(|s′ − e|/tan φ_max − s); pitch-curve radius ρ = |P′|³/(P′ × P″), undercut where ρ_min ≤ R_r; flat-faced profile radius ρ = R_b + s + s″ above zero; follower force F = m·a + k·s + F_pre + F_ext (the follower leaves the cam where F < 0), input torque T = F·s′, roller normal force F/cos φ

3. Assumptions
  A1. The mechanism is rigid and planar, has one degree of freedom, and has no joint clearance or friction. (Scope limits of the model.)
  A2. The input turns at a constant speed. (Scope limits of the model.)
  A3. The position is the closed-form solution of the vector loop, on the chosen circuit. (Open and crossed are the two branches: θ₄ = φ − γ and φ + γ for the four-bar, σ = +1 and −1 for the slider.)
  A4. The sweep stays on the chosen circuit and follows it by continuity through a change point. (Only a change point, with all four links collinear (θ₂ = 0 or π), flips the branch.)
  A5. A linkage with one change point per turn repeats every 720°, and a sweep of more than one turn warns. (A kite, for example 40/40/100/100, is such a linkage.)
  A6. The table leaves out angles where the linkage cannot be assembled or sits at a toggle. (It counts them, and at a toggle the velocity system is singular.)
  A7. The input torque comes from the output load and the inertia of the output element only. (The crank and the coupler or connecting rod are massless: add their mass to the output element for a first allowance.)
  A8. The crank, the coupler and the connecting rod are massless two-force members. (Rigid body, frictionless.)
  A9. The peaks are those of the continuous motion over the swept range, not of the printed table. (A dense scan refined by golden section finds them, so they do not depend on the step count of the table (up to 3600).)
  A10. At an assembly limit the sweep is cut, and the unbounded peaks, torque and forces are not reported. (The limit angles are listed: ℓ = b + c or |b − c| (four-bar), |r·sinθ − e| = l (slider).)
  A11. A change-point four-bar is continued through its singular position on the same branch. (Its speeds and torques next to the singular position are not reported.)
  A12. The cam drives a translating follower at constant speed. (Norton, Design of Machinery, cam design: the four classical laws, pressure angle and radius of curvature.)
  A13. The follower force is m·a + k·s + F_pre + F_ext, and contact is lost below zero. (The spring rate and the preload are those at the base circle, and the segment table must add to 360° and close.)
  A14. Pressure angle and undercut are checked for the roller follower, and the profile radius for the flat-faced one. (The roller has the prime radius R_p = R_b + R_r.)
  A15. The output speed is a kinematic proxy at constant input speed, not the speed of an actuated cylinder. (For motor sizing, use the peak input_torque for the peak check and T_rms_in with n_in for the thermal sizing. The mean power is P_mean.)
  A16. steps (Intervals N of the table (N + 1 rows, a whole number, at most 3600; the peaks do not depend on it)) is taken as 72. (5° table step.)
  A17. load_dir (External load: constant direction, or always opposing the output motion) is taken as Opposes motion. (The flap resists the blade in whichever direction it moves.)
  A18. T_load (External load torque on the output link (four-bar). Positive acts in the −θ₄ direction, resisting positive ω₄; with 'Opposes motion' its sign is applied against the actual direction of ω₄ (a negative value then assists)) is taken as 4 N·m. (Example flap-folding torque at the rocker shaft; measure it on the station.)
  A19. J_out (Moment of inertia of the output link about its pivot (four-bar)) is taken as 0.012 kg·m². (Example: rocker arm and blade about the rocker pivot; take it from the CAD mass properties.)
  A20. mu_req (Minimum transmission angle required (linkages)) is taken as 40 °. (Default minimum transmission angle; set it from your design rule.)
