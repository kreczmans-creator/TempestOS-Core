# v0.73.0 — Cross-Domain Engineering Fundamentals

This release closes another major set of fundamentals by connecting mechanical
engineering to controls, electronics, safety, reliability, environmental
loads and configuration management.

## Controls and feedback

The foundation now covers the basic closed-loop control architecture,
PID behaviour, stability/response considerations and control-loop failure
modes. The treatment explicitly includes actuator saturation, feedback failure,
delay, sampling and mechanical non-idealities.

## Electronics interfaces

Mechanical systems are connected to their electrical interfaces through power,
signals, feedback, grounding, cabling, connector and EMI/EMC considerations.

## Safety and reliability

Hazard/risk reasoning, FMEA/FMECA, fault-tree analysis, single-point failures,
fail-safe behaviour, fault containment and reliability fundamentals are now
represented as reusable engineering methods.

## Environmental engineering

Temperature, humidity, vibration, shock, ingress, corrosion and combined
environmental exposure are treated as design inputs and verification drivers.

## Configuration and change control

Requirements, CAD, drawings, BOMs, software, materials, models, procedures and
verification evidence are treated as controlled configuration items. Change
impact analysis and baseline release discipline are explicitly represented.

## Cross-domain worked cases

Five integrated cases connect these fundamentals:
- closed-loop electromechanical axis;
- power/signal interface;
- safety architecture;
- environmental qualification;
- controlled engineering change.

## Boundary

The foundation provides engineering reasoning and workflow structure. It does
not substitute for applicable safety standards, EMC/environmental qualification,
reliability demonstration, or product-specific certification.
