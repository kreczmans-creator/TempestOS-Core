Title: Pipe expansion, loops and anchor forces (screening)

1. Scope and method
Screening only — not a piping stress analysis. Thermal growth of a straight run between anchors, the fully restrained stress and force, the leg an offset leg or a U-loop (two guided cantilevers) needs to absorb the growth within the owner's allowable displacement-stress range Sa and whether the available leg is long enough (an offset leg or Z-bend also loads its run or runs with the leg's own growth), the force on the anchors and the largest root moment of the guided-cantilever model, the screening ratio D·Y/(L − U)² against the owner's K1 (PASS, or the line needs a formal stress analysis), and, for an axial bellows, the anchor force P·A_eff plus its spring force. No allowable stress, K1 or code table ships.
Pipe section: d_i = OD − 2·t; A_m = π/4·(OD² − d_i²); I = π/64·(OD⁴ − d_i⁴); Z = I/(OD/2) Temperature change: ΔT = T_operate − T_install, or as entered; the movement uses |ΔT| and its sign gives the direction Run that grows: L_tot = L, or L + L2 for a Z-bend; thermal growth ΔL = α·L_tot·|ΔT| Fully restrained pipe: σ_r = E·α·|ΔT| (E at the installation temperature); F_r = E_r·α·|ΔT|·A_m (E_r = E_hot, else E) Movement one leg must absorb: Δ = ΔL for an offset leg or Z-bend; Δ = ΔL/2 for a U-loop (two identical legs in series, the cross leg a rigid guide) Guided cantilever, required leg: L_req = √(3·E·OD·Δ/Sa), the leg at which the bending stress M/Z reaches Sa; PASS when L_req ≤ the available leg H Force and moment of the available leg: F_leg = 12·E_r·I·Δ/H³ (acts along the run); M_leg = 6·E_r·I·Δ/H² (= F_leg·H/2); σ_leg = 3·E·OD·Δ/H² = Sa·(L_req/H)² Offset leg or Z-bend, the runs bend for the leg's growth Δ_leg = α·|ΔT|·H: the runs act in series under one force F_run = 12·E_r·I·Δ_leg/(L³ + L2³) (L2 = 0 for an L-bend) acting along the leg; run i takes the share L_i³/(L³ + L2³) of Δ_leg, with root stress σ_i = 3·E·OD·Δ_i/L_i² and moment M_i = F_run·L_i/2; each run is checked as its own cantilever, PASS when √(σ_i/Sa) ≤ 1 (for an L-bend the run needed is L_req,run = √(3·E·OD·Δ_leg/Sa)) Force on the anchors: the two components are perpendicular, F_leg along the run and F_run along the leg, so the force is √(F_leg² + F_run²); the moment reported is the largest root moment of the legs and runs in the guided-cantilever model Flexibility screening: OD·Y/(L_dev − U)² ≤ K1 with Y = α·|ΔT|·U, the free growth between the anchors; offset leg or Z-bend L_dev = L_tot + H, U = √(L_tot² + H²); U-loop L_dev = L + 2·H, U = L (so Y = ΔL); PASS, or the line needs a formal stress analysis Axial bellows (no leg or loop, no leg check, no screening): anchor force = P·A_eff + k·ΔL; the movement is checked against the bellows' rated axial movement when given

3. Assumptions
  A1. Screening only — not a piping stress analysis. The result tells whether a simple leg or loop is plausible and what it puts on its anchors; it does not replace a flexibility analysis of the real routing (elbow flexibility, intensification, torsion, branches, restraint friction, supports, sustained and occasional loads). (Stated scope.)
  A2. A guided cantilever: the available leg is straight, uniform, fixed against rotation at both ends and loaded only by the movement it absorbs (stiffness 12·E·I/H³, end moment F·H/2). Elbows are taken as rigid and the leg as slender: the beam model is not valid for legs shorter than about ten diameters. (Beam theory.)
  A3. No stress-intensification factor and no elbow flexibility are applied: the stress at the elbow root of a real bend is higher than the beam stress M/Z, by the intensification factor of the code, so a leg that passes here can still exceed Sa at the elbow. This is the main reason the result is a screen and not a stress analysis. (Stated limit.)
  A4. A U-loop is two identical legs in series, each a guided cantilever taking half the growth, the cross leg a rigid guide; the force along the line is the same F in both legs. The moment M is the moment at the leg roots (the corners of the loop); it reaches an anchor only where the leg ends at the anchor (an offset leg). The growth of the loop's own legs, which the cross leg has to absorb, is not checked. (Loop model.)
  A5. An offset leg is anchored at both ends: the run L bends for the growth of the leg (α·|ΔT|·H) as a second guided cantilever and is checked against the same Sa. A Z-bend (second run L2) has the two runs in series under one force, each taking its share of the leg's growth in proportion to its flexibility, L_i³, and each checked as its own cantilever; the leg takes the growth of both runs. The force on the anchors is the resultant of the force along the run (the leg's) and the force along the leg (the runs'), which are perpendicular. (Offset-leg and Z-bend model.)
  A6. anchor_moment is the largest root moment of the legs and runs in the guided-cantilever simplification (M = F·L/2 with each end fixed against rotation). It is not the moment on a frame anchor, which depends on the whole frame (the stiffness of the other legs, the elbow rotations, the anchor's own fixity); a formal analysis is needed before an anchor is sized for moment. (Stated limit.)
  A7. The material properties are single values: α the mean coefficient between the install and operating temperatures; E the modulus at the installation (reference) temperature, used for the stress check, as the piping codes do (ASME B31.3 para. 319.3.2 and 319.5, numbers to be checked against the owner's copy); E_hot, when given, is used only for the reactions on the anchors. Take them from a W3 material applied at the right temperature. (Material library; code convention to be checked.)
  A8. Sa and K1 are the owner's records from the owner's own copy of the piping code (or the design basis), with their provenance; no allowable stress, K1 or code constant ships. The sheet applies the relation to the numbers entered and does not know which code, material or load case they belong to. (No table from a standard is shipped.)
  A9. No friction, weight, spring-hanger or cold-spring effect is included in the leg force; the anchor force adds the friction of the sliding supports, μ·w·L, only when μ and the weight per metre are entered (a warning says so when they are not). The axial bellows anchor force is the pressure thrust P·A_eff plus the spring force k·ΔL at the governing pressure entered (the design or the test pressure, whichever governs; a bellows' compression and extension ratings can differ): guide friction and any other bellows load come from the manufacturer's data. (Stated limit.)
  A10. Layout: the offset leg of 6 m takes the whole movement of 42 mm as a guided cantilever fixed against rotation at both ends, and the run of 50 m bends for the leg's growth of 5.04 mm. Screening only — not a piping stress analysis. (Guided-cantilever model.)
