Title: Glaser condensation (interstitial and surface)

1. Scope and method
Interstitial condensation in a layered wall by the Glaser method over twelve monthly climate steps: the temperature and saturation pressure at every layer interface, the vapour-pressure line with its tangent construction at the condensation planes, the condensate on each interface month by month carried forward from the first month of condensation (drying when the weather turns), the annual balance, the peak accumulation against the owner's limit, and the surface temperature factor f_Rsi against its minimum at the critical surface humidity.
Layer resistance and diffusion thickness: R_j = d_j/λ_j; s_d,j = μ_j·d_j Total resistance and interface temperature: R_T = R_si + Σ R_j + R_se; θ_k = θ_i − (θ_i − θ_e)·(R_si + Σ_(j≤k) R_j)/R_T Saturation pressure (Magnus): p_sat = 610.5·exp(17.269·θ/(237.3 + θ)) over water (θ ≥ 0 °C); p_sat = 610.5·exp(21.875·θ/(265.5 + θ)) over ice (θ < 0 °C) Outside vapour pressure: p_e = RH_e·p_sat(θ_e) Inside vapour pressure: p_i = RH_i·p_sat(θ_i), or p_e + f_Δp·Δv(θ_e)·R_v·(T_i + T_e)/2 with the moisture excess Δv linear between two user points and constant beyond them (R_v = 461.5 J/(kg·K)) and the safety factor f_Δp on the pressure difference (a record, default 1.10; clause wording to be checked against the owner's copy of ISO 13788) Vapour flux through a layer: g = δ0·Δp/Δs_d with δ0 = 2×10⁻¹⁰ kg/(m·s·Pa) Tangent construction: the line from p_i to p_e is bent onto the saturation line at the interfaces it would rise above (and at wet interfaces), by the smallest-slope rule from each contact point Condensation at an interface: g_c = g_in − g_out; Mc accumulates g_c·t over 12 months from the first month of condensation (t = the row's days, else the calendar days of the month, January to December order), a wet interface (held at saturation) drying at g_c < 0 until nothing is left Refinement check: the same year is run again with every layer split into 20 equal sub-layers of the same λ and μ (inside the model, not counted against the 30-layer limit), because the saturation line is curved inside a layer and the interface-only model can miss the condensate that forms between two interfaces; the annual balance passes only when it passes both ways, and the sheet warns when the two runs differ (a verdict flips, or Mc_end or the wall peak differ by more than 10 %) Annual balance and peak: PASS when no condensate remains after the 12 months (as entered and in 20 sub-layers per layer); Mc_max = the most held on any interface, checked against the user's limit Wall U-value and its cross-check: U = 1/R_T; when the heat-loss calculation's U-value U_ref is given, the sheet warns if |U − U_ref|/U_ref exceeds 2 % (the two calculations then describe different constructions) Surface factor: f_Rsi = 1 − R_si,f/R_T,f with R_si,f = 0.25 m²K/W by default for an opaque element (conventional, a record; 0.13 for glazing); f_Rsi,min = the largest monthly (θ_si,min − θ_e)/(θ_i − θ_e) with p_sat(θ_si,min) = p_i/φ_crit; PASS when f_Rsi ≥ f_Rsi,min

3. Assumptions
  A1. Steady state in each month: one-dimensional diffusion of water vapour through homogeneous layers in series, with the monthly mean climate; no air movement, no liquid transport, no moisture storage, no solar or rain gain. (The Glaser method.)
  A2. Material properties (λ, μ) are constants of the layer, not functions of its moisture or temperature; condensate does not change them. (The Glaser method.)
  A3. Condensation is checked at the interfaces between layers; the saturation line is joined straight between the interface values, which can miss condensate that forms inside a layer. The annual balance is therefore also run with every layer split into 20 sub-layers and passes only when both pass; the limit on the condensate per interface is checked on the interfaces as entered. (Stated limit; refinement check (bs.glaser-01). Whether ISO 13788 requires sub-division is to be checked against the owner's copy.)
  A4. The Magnus saturation pressure is used over water at and above 0 °C and over ice below, and the water-vapour permeability of air is δ0 = 2×10⁻¹⁰ kg/(m·s·Pa); both are closed-form physics, not data of a standard. (Derived.)
  A5. Where a month's length is blank it is the calendar days of the month, taking the climate rows as January to December (February 28 days); enter the days where the record starts in another month. A wet interface that dries within a month is taken as dry from the next month; the month's rates are those of the wet state at its start. (Monthly steps.)
  A6. The monthly climate, the humidity-class points, the surface resistances, the critical humidity and the limit on the condensate are the user's records from the user's own licensed copies; no climate data, humidity-class table, material table or limit of any standard is shipped. (No table from a standard is shipped.)
  A7. Month length: 12 row(s) of the climate gave no days, so the calendar days of the month are used with the rows taken as January to December (February 28 days). If the record starts in another month, enter the days of each row. (Calendar months.)
