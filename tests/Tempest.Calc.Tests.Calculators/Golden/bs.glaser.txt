Title: Glaser condensation (interstitial and surface)

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Interstitial condensation in a layered wall by the Glaser method over twelve monthly climate steps: the temperature and saturation pressure at every layer interface, the vapour-pressure line with its tangent construction at the condensation planes, the condensate on each interface month by month carried forward from the first month of condensation (drying when the weather turns), the annual balance, the peak accumulation against the owner's limit, and the surface temperature factor f_Rsi against its minimum at the critical surface humidity.
Layer resistance and diffusion thickness: R_j = d_j/λ_j; s_d,j = μ_j·d_j Total resistance and interface temperature: R_T = R_si + Σ R_j + R_se; θ_k = θ_i − (θ_i − θ_e)·(R_si + Σ_(j≤k) R_j)/R_T Saturation pressure (Magnus): p_sat = 610.5·exp(17.269·θ/(237.3 + θ)) over water (θ ≥ 0 °C); p_sat = 610.5·exp(21.875·θ/(265.5 + θ)) over ice (θ < 0 °C) Outside vapour pressure: p_e = RH_e·p_sat(θ_e) Inside vapour pressure: p_i = RH_i·p_sat(θ_i), or p_e + Δv(θ_e)·R_v·T_i with the moisture excess Δv linear between two user points and constant beyond them (R_v = 461.5 J/(kg·K)) Vapour flux through a layer: g = δ0·Δp/Δs_d with δ0 = 2×10⁻¹⁰ kg/(m·s·Pa) Tangent construction: the line from p_i to p_e is bent onto the saturation line at the interfaces it would rise above (and at wet interfaces), by the smallest-slope rule from each contact point Condensation at an interface: g_c = g_in − g_out; Mc accumulates g_c·t over 12 months from the first month of condensation, a wet interface (held at saturation) drying at g_c < 0 until nothing is left Annual balance and peak: PASS when no condensate remains after the 12 months; Mc_max = the most held on any interface, checked against the user's limit Surface factor: f_Rsi = 1 − R_si/R_T; f_Rsi,min = the largest monthly (θ_si,min − θ_e)/(θ_i − θ_e) with p_sat(θ_si,min) = p_i/φ_crit; PASS when f_Rsi ≥ f_Rsi,min
  Ref: ISO 13788 cl. 5 (clause number to be checked against the owner's copy) — Hygrothermal performance of building components and elements — internal surface temperature and surface condensation
  Ref: ISO 13788 cl. 6 (clause number to be checked against the owner's copy) — Hygrothermal performance of building components and elements — interstitial condensation (Glaser method)
  Ref: EN 15026 whole standard (named as the recommended method for timber and breathable builds) — Hygrothermal performance of building components — assessment of moisture transfer by numerical simulation

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Steady state in each month: one-dimensional diffusion of water vapour through homogeneous layers in series, with the monthly mean climate; no air movement, no liquid transport, no moisture storage, no solar or rain gain. (The Glaser method.)
  A2. Material properties (λ, μ) are constants of the layer, not functions of its moisture or temperature; condensate does not change them. (The Glaser method.)
  A3. Condensation is checked at the interfaces between layers only; a thick layer where the plane would lie inside it should be split into thinner layers. The saturation line is joined straight between the interface values. (Stated limit.)
  A4. The Magnus saturation pressure is used over water at and above 0 °C and over ice below, and the water-vapour permeability of air is δ0 = 2×10⁻¹⁰ kg/(m·s·Pa); both are closed-form physics, not data of a standard. (Derived.)
  A5. Where a month's length is blank it is 365/12 days. A wet interface that dries within a month is taken as dry from the next month; the month's rates are those of the wet state at its start. (Monthly steps.)
  A6. The monthly climate, the humidity-class points, the surface resistances, the critical humidity and the limit on the condensate are the user's records from the user's own licensed copies; no climate data, humidity-class table, material table or limit of any standard is shipped. (No table from a standard is shipped.)
