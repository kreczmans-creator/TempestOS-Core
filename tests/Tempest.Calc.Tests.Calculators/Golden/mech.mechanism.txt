Title: Mechanism kinematics: four-bar, slider-crank, cam

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
A single-degree-of-freedom planar mechanism driven at constant speed: a four-bar linkage or an offset slider-crank (both circuits, position, velocity and acceleration over a sweep of the input angle, Grashof class, transmission angle against a minimum, toggle and dead-centre positions, loop-closure residual, input torque by virtual work), or a cam with a translating follower. Rigid links, no clearance, no friction.
Four-bar: a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} = d + c·e^{jθ₄}; ℓ = |A − O₄|, cos γ = (c² + ℓ² − b²)/(2cℓ), θ₄ = φ ∓ γ (φ the direction O₄→A; open: −, crossed: +); θ₃ from B − A Four-bar velocity and acceleration: differentiated loop, 2×2 system with det = b·c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄); ω₃ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₄ − θ₂)/(b·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)), ω₄ = ω₂·a·sin(θ₃ − θ₂)/(c·sin(θ₃ − θ₄)); α from the second derivative at constant ω₂ Grashof: s + l < p + q (crank-rocker, double-crank or double-rocker by the shortest link), = at the change point, > non-Grashof; transmission angle cos μ = (b² + c² − ℓ²)/(2bc) folded to 0 … 90°; toggle where ℓ = b + c or |b − c| Slider-crank: x = r·cosθ + σ·√(l² − (r·sinθ − e)²) (σ = +1 open, −1 crossed); dx/dθ = −r·sinθ − r·cosθ·(r·sinθ − e)/(x − r·cosθ); dead centres where crank and rod are collinear; μ = 90° − |rod angle to the slide line| Input torque by virtual work: T_in·ω_in = Σ F·v; four-bar T_in = (T_L + J·α₄)·ω₄/ω₂; slider T_in = (m·a + F_L)·dx/dθ Loop closure: |a·e^{jθ₂} + b·e^{jθ₃} − c·e^{jθ₄} − d| (slider: |r·e^{jθ} + l·e^{jθ₃} − (x + j·e)|) asserted below 1e-9 mm at every step
  Ref: Norton, Design of Machinery Ch. 4, 6, 7 — Position, velocity and acceleration analysis; transmission angle; Grashof condition
  Ref: Uicker, Pennock and Shigley, Theory of Machines and Mechanisms Ch. 4–6 — Loop-closure position analysis; velocity and acceleration of planar linkages

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Rigid links, a planar mechanism with one degree of freedom, no joint clearance and no friction; the input turns at a constant speed. (Addendum B §B6 M9 limits.)
  A2. The position is the closed-form solution of the vector loop; the open and crossed circuits are the two branches (θ₄ = φ − γ and φ + γ for the four-bar, σ = +1 and −1 for the slider) and the sweep stays on the chosen branch. Angles at which the linkage cannot be assembled, or at a toggle where the velocity system is singular, are left out of the table and counted. (Norton, Design of Machinery, position analysis of the four-bar and the offset slider-crank.)
  A3. Input torque is by virtual work from the output load and the inertia of the output element only (four-bar: the output link's moment of inertia about its pivot; slider: the slider mass); the crank and the coupler or connecting rod are massless. (No link-inertia or gravity terms: add the link masses to the output element for a first allowance.)
  A4. The peaks (speeds, accelerations, torque, transmission angle, swing) are those of the continuous motion over the swept range, found by a dense scan refined by golden section; the printed table (steps N, up to 3600) only samples it. (Peaks do not depend on the table's step count.)
  A5. Follower force (four-bar: the tangential force at the output pin, (T_L + J·α₄)/c; slider: the rod force on the slider, m·a + F_L) is the force the mechanism must apply to the output element. (Rigid body, frictionless.)
