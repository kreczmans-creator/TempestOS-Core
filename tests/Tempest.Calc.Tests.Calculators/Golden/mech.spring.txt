Title: Helical springs: compression, extension, torsion

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
A cylindrical helical spring of round wire, cold-coiled, with a linear characteristic: compression (rate, working points, solid length by end type, stress at the working points and at solid, buckling, Goodman fatigue, surge frequency), extension (the same with the initial tension and the hook stresses) or torsion (rate with the legs, curvature-corrected bending stress, wound diameter and body length).
Index and rate: C = D/d; k = G·d⁴/(8·D³·n) (compression, extension); k_φ = E·d⁴/(64·D·n_a), n_a = n + (l₁ + l₂)/(3π·D) (torsion) Spring stress: τ_k = k_c·8·F·D/(π·d³), k_c = (C + 0.5)/(C − 0.75) (Bergsträsser) or (4C − 1)/(4C − 4) + 0.615/C (Wahl); torsion σ = q·32·M/(π·d³) with q = (C + 0.07)/(C − 0.75) (EN 13906-3, the default) or K_i = (4C² − C − 1)/(4C·(C − 1)) (Shigley) Solid length (compression): n_t = n + e_n; EN 13906-1 form L_s = d_max·(n_t + e_s) with (e_n, e_s) = (0, 1) plain, (1, 0) plain and ground, (2, 1.5) squared (closed, unground), (2, 0) squared and ground, and the minimum coil clearance S_a = (0.0015·D²/d + 0.1·d)·n checked at the working length (L₂ ≥ L_s + S_a); Shigley form L_s = d·(n_t + e_s) with e_s = 1 for squared; pitch p = (L₀ − a·d)/(n + b) Extension spring hook stresses: F = F₀ + k·s; hook bending σ_A = F·[K_A·16·D/(π·d³) + 4/(π·d²)], K_A = (4C₁² − C₁ − 1)/(4C₁·(C₁ − 1)), C₁ = 2r₁/d; hook torsion τ_B = K_B·8·F·D/(π·d³), K_B = (4C₂ − 1)/(4C₂ − 4), C₂ = 2r₂/d Buckling (compression): λ_eff = ν·L₀/D; y_cr = L₀·C₁′·[1 − √(1 − C₂′/λ_eff²)], C₁′ = E/(2(E − G)), C₂′ = 2π²(E − G)/(2G + E); stable at any deflection when λ_eff² < C₂′ Fatigue (Goodman, as M4): 1/n_f = a/S_e + m/S_u with a = (x₂ − x₁)/2, m = (x₂ + x₁)/2 of the stresses at the two working points; S_u = f_su·R_m in shear, R_m in bending; R_m typed or R_m = A/d^m from a W3 material (A in MPa, d in mm) Surge frequency: f_n = (d/(2π·n·D²))·√(G/(2ρ)); warning when f_n/f_op < 13 Torsion body: D_w = D·n/(n + φ/2π); L_w = d·(n + 1 + φ/2π)
  Ref: EN 13906-1 Calculation of compression springs — Rate, stress correction, solid length, buckling, fatigue, surge
  Ref: EN 13906-2 Calculation of extension springs — Rate, initial tension, hook stress
  Ref: EN 13906-3 Calculation of torsion springs — Rate, bending stress with curvature correction
  Ref: Shigley's Mechanical Engineering Design 10th ed., Ch. 10 — Helical springs: end geometry, buckling, critical frequency, extension and torsion springs

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Linear characteristic: the rate is constant between the working points; no progressive or conical spring, no variable pitch. (Cold-coiled round wire up to 20 mm.)
  A2. Active coils n are as entered; end coils are accounted for only in the solid length (and the pitch) by the end type; the hook and leg flexibility is in the rate only for the torsion legs. (Shigley 10th ed. Ch. 10, geometry of the ends.)
  A3. Solid length: the EN 13906-1 form (default) uses the maximum wire diameter d_max, adds the 1.5 d of an unground squared end and checks the minimum coil clearance S_a at the working length L₂; plain ends have no EN row and keep the geometric form. The Shigley form (nominal d, no clearance) is selectable. (EN 13906-1 relations written in own notation, to be checked against the owner's copy; Shigley 10th ed. Table 10-1.)
  A4. Torsion bending stress: the EN 13906-3 factor q = (C + 0.07)/(C − 0.75) is the default; Shigley's K_i = (4C² − C − 1)/(4C(C − 1)) is selectable and about 1 % lower at C = 8. (EN 13906-3 relation written in own notation, to be checked against the owner's copy.)
  A5. R_m(d) and the endurance strength are the user's inputs from the user's own copy of the wire standard and design reference, with no default. The permissible stress fractions f_τ, f_σ and f_su do default (0.45, 0.75, 0.67), to generic textbook fractions that the user replaces with the licensed EN 13906 value. R_m(d) can instead be taken from the curve R_m = A/d^m of a W3 material (revision-pinned; A in MPa for d in mm, the diameter range of the fit is the user's to respect). No wire-strength table and no permissible-stress diagram is shipped. (No wire-strength table or permissible-stress diagram is shipped.)
  A6. The stress correction factor k_c is applied to the working and the solid stress alike (conservative for the static check). (Choose 'None (uncorrected)' for the uncorrected static stress; the fatigue stresses (Goodman) always carry the curvature factor, the Bergsträsser one when 'None' is chosen.)
  A7. Extension: hook fatigue and the hook shape beyond its two radii are not assessed; the initial tension is a force the user enters. (Shigley 10th ed. Ch. 10, extension springs.)
  A8. Surge is the first natural frequency of a spring with both ends fixed; a spring with one end free has half of it. (Shigley 10th ed. Ch. 10, critical frequency.)
