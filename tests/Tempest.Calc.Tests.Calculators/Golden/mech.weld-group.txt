Title: Weld group (elastic line method)

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
A group of straight fillet welds in a plane under in-plane forces (with eccentricity, so torsion), an out-of-plane force and out-of-plane moments: the resultant force per unit length at every weld end, the critical point, the throat stress against the design strength and the leg size required.
Welds as lines of unit throat: L_w = Σ l, centroid x_c = Σ l·x_m/L_w Unit second moments: I_ux = Σ [l(y_m − y_c)² + l·Δy²/12], I_uy likewise, J_u = I_ux + I_uy Torsion about the centroid: T = F_y(e_x − x_c) − F_x(e_y − y_c) + M_z At each weld end: direct F/L_w, torsional T·r/J_u (perpendicular to r), out-of-plane F_z/L_w + M_x·y′/I_ux + M_y·x′/I_uy; vector sum f_r Throat: a = 0.707·s, τ = f_r,max/a; required leg s_req = f_r,max/(0.707·f_w)
  Ref: Shigley's Mechanical Engineering Design 11th ed., Ch. 9, Tables 9-1 and 9-2 — Welds treated as lines: torsional and bending properties
  Ref: Blodgett, Design of Welded Structures 1966, §7.4 — Treating the weld as a line
  Ref: EN 1993-1-8:2005 cl. 4.5.3.3 — Simplified method for design resistance of fillet welds (resistance input)

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Elastic, static; the connected parts are rigid and the weld stress varies linearly with distance from the centroid. (Conservative against the instantaneous-centre method.)
  A2. Fillet welds only, all of one leg size; the throat is 0.707·s. (Equal-leg fillet.)
  A3. The design strength of the throat comes from S4 (EN 1993-1-8 simplified method) or a user allowable with its source. (No resistance table ships.)
