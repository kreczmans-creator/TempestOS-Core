Title: Weld group (elastic line method)

1. Scope and method
Calculates the force per unit length at every end of a fillet weld group, the throat stress and the leg size required. It covers one leg size, with in-plane forces and torque, an out-of-plane force and moments.
Welds as lines of unit throat: L_w = Σ l, centroid x_c = Σ l·x_m/L_w Unit second moments: I_ux = Σ [l(y_m − y_c)² + l·Δy²/12], I_uy likewise, product I_uxy = Σ [l(x_m − x_c)(y_m − y_c) + l·Δx·Δy/12], J_u = I_ux + I_uy Torsion about the centroid: T = F_y(e_x − x_c) − F_x(e_y − y_c) + M_z At each weld end: direct F/L_w, torsional T·r/J_u (perpendicular to r), out-of-plane F_z/L_w + [(M_x·I_uy − M_y·I_uxy)·y′ + (M_y·I_ux − M_x·I_uxy)·x′]/(I_ux·I_uy − I_uxy²) with F_z's eccentricity added to M_x and M_y (this reduces to M_x·y′/I_ux + M_y·x′/I_uy only for a group symmetric about a centroidal axis); vector sum f_r Throat and weld stress: a = 0.707·s, τ = f_r,max/a; required leg s_req = f_r,max/(0.707·f_w)

3. Assumptions
  A1. The load is static, the weld is elastic and the connected parts are rigid. (Conservative against the instantaneous-centre method.)
  A2. The weld stress varies linearly with distance from the centroid. (The elastic line method.)
  A3. The welds are fillet welds of one leg size, and the throat is 0.707·s. (Equal-leg fillet.)
  A4. The design strength of the throat comes from EN 1993-1-8 (simplified method) or a user allowable with its source. (No resistance table ships.)
