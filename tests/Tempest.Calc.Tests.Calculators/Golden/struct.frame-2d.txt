Title: Beam and 2D frame analysis

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
Linear elastic analysis of a plane frame or continuous beam by the direct stiffness method: reactions, member end forces, axial force, shear, bending moment and deflection along every member with their extremes and positions, for each load combination and as an envelope. Global x to the right, y up; moments and couples anticlockwise positive; sagging moments positive along a member from node i to node j.
Member stiffness, three freedoms per node, Euler–Bernoulli members: axial EA/L and bending EI/L³ terms. End releases (pins) are taken out by static condensation; supports remove freedoms or add spring stiffness. Span loads become equivalent nodal loads, the exact fixed-end forces. Displacements, by banded LDLᵀ after a reverse Cuthill–McKee ordering (a non-positive pivot is reported as a mechanism): K u = F. Internal forces and deflections follow from the member end actions and Macaulay functions of the span loads. Extremes are found at stations along each member, plus the zero-shear and zero-slope points found by bisection.
  Ref: Hibbeler, Structural Analysis Ch. 16 — Plane frame analysis using the stiffness method
  Ref: Roark's Formulas for Stress and Strain Ch. 8, Table 8.1 — Shear, moment, slope and deflection formulas for elastic straight beams
  Ref: BS EN 1990 6.4.3 — Combinations of actions (user-entered factors)

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Linear elastic, small displacements, first order; no shear deformation, no P–Δ, no buckling. (Euler–Bernoulli direct stiffness.)
  A2. Members are prismatic between nodes and connect rigidly unless a release is stated; a release transmits no moment at that end. (Static condensation of the released rotation.)
  A3. Distributed loads act perpendicular to the member unless marked as gravity loads, which act in global −y per metre of member length. (Load table convention.)
