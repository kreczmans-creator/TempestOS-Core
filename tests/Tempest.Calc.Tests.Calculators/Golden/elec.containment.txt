Title: Containment fill, bend radius and cable pulling

1. Scope and method
The fill of a conduit, trunking or tray by the cables in it against the space factor you enter, the bend radius of the route against the multiple of the largest cable diameter you enter, and the pull of the cables through a route of straight lengths and bends in both directions: the tension along the route, the sidewall pressure at each bend, and the pull direction that passes every check (the lower maximum tension when both do or neither does). Space factor, bend multiple, friction coefficient and pulling limits are your own records; no cable or conduit factor method is offered.
Cable area: A_cab = Σ n_i·π·d_i²/4 (n_i cables of overall diameter d_i) Containment area: A_int = π·D²/4 (conduit, internal diameter D) or W·H (trunking or tray, internal width × depth) Containment fill: fill = A_cab/A_int; utilisation u_fill = fill/f_max, f_max the space factor from your record Bend radius: R_min = n_bend·d_max, d_max the largest cable diameter, n_bend from your record; each bend u_b = R_min/R Cable weight: w = Σ n_i·m_i (kg/m); force per metre w·g·w_c, g = 9.80665 m/s², w_c your weight-correction factor (1 = full weight) Straight length: T₂ = T₁ + w·g·w_c·μ·L Tension round a bend in the horizontal plane, the cable resting on the containment floor (the default): T₂ = T₁·cosh(μ_e·θ) + √(T₁² + (w·g·w_c·R)²)·sinh(μ_e·θ), μ_e = w_c·μ, θ in radians, R the bend radius: the cable's weight bears on the floor round the bend as well as the tension, so a heavy cable on a large radius gains tension even from T₁ = 0 Tension round a bend, capstan option (no weight term): T₂ = T₁·e^(w_c·μ·θ), the equation for a weightless pull; it is T₂ = T₁·e^(μ_e·θ) and equals the weighted form when w·g·w_c·R is small against T₁ Sidewall pressure at a bend: SWP = T₂/R (N/m) Pulling direction: the route is run in the listed order and in reverse; the direction that passes every tension and sidewall pressure check is recommended, and when both or neither pass, the one of lower maximum tension (equal tension: lower SWP) Cable limit: load shared equally, so each cable carries T/N (N cables); T_cab = k_n·N·min(T_max,i), k_n your derating for more than 3 cables (1 if none); u_T = max_i (T/N)/(k_n·T_max,i) = T/T_cab Grip limit: u_grip = T/T_grip, T_grip the limit of the grip or pulling eye (your record); sidewall pressure u_P = SWP_max/P_lim

3. Assumptions
  A1. Cables are circular and counted by overall diameter; there is no packing or cable and conduit factor method (the jam-ratio warning is the only jamming check). The space factor is yours, from your own record. (Fill by geometry; the factor is a user record.)
  A2. Every cable is pulled together as one bundle on one pulling head with the load shared equally, so each cable carries T/N and the weakest cable governs: the cable limit is N times the lowest maximum pulling tension, optionally derated by your factor for more than 3 cables. The grip or pulling-eye limit is a separate limit you enter. (Equal sharing; unequal sharing is not modelled.)
  A3. Straight lengths are horizontal and the cable weight bears on the containment over the full length; bends are taken in the plane of the route. Inclines, vertical runs and pulley or roller reductions are not modelled; fold them into μ. A cradled or triangular weight correction is entered as w_c, which scales the weight on the straight lengths and the friction in the bends (the contact force of a cradled or triangular arrangement is w_c times that of a flat one), so T₂ = T₁ + w·g·w_c·μ·L and, at a bend, μ_e = w_c·μ in the cosh/sinh (or capstan) form. A bend in a vertical plane has the weight along the cable rather than on the floor: take the capstan option and fold the rise into the route. (Equivalent friction w_c·μ at the bends; blank w_c means 1.)
  A4. The coefficient of friction is one constant for the whole route; static and running friction are not distinguished. (A user record with its source.)
  A5. Sidewall pressure is the tension leaving the bend over the bend radius, for the bundle as a whole. Enter the cable maker's per-cable limit unchanged: the bundle T/R against it is conservative for a triangular arrangement and roughly equal for a cradled one. Do not multiply the limit by the number of cables. (SWP = T₂/R.)
  A6. The bend radius uses the individual cable diameter; for a triplexed or bundled assembly, enter the assembly diameter from the cable maker as the overall diameter. (Cable data sheet.)
  A7. The tension at the start of the pull is the same in both directions. With the horizontal-bend (weight) model a heavy cable gains tension at a bend even from T₁ = 0; with the capstan option a bend at the very start of a pull with no start tension adds nothing, so enter the pulling rope or drum tension when it matters. (Weight term for a horizontal bend; capstan equation as the option.)
