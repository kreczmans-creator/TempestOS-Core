Title: Containment fill, bend radius and cable pulling

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
The fill of a conduit, trunking or tray by the cables in it against the space factor you enter, the bend radius of the route against the multiple of the largest cable diameter you enter, and the pull of the cables through a route of straight lengths and bends in both directions: the tension along the route, the sidewall pressure at each bend, and the direction of lower maximum tension. Space factor, bend multiple, friction coefficient and pulling limits are your own records; no cable or conduit factor method is offered.
Cable area: A_cab = Σ n_i·π·d_i²/4 (n_i cables of overall diameter d_i) Containment area: A_int = π·D²/4 (conduit, internal diameter D) or W·H (trunking or tray, internal width × depth) Fill: fill = A_cab/A_int; utilisation u_fill = fill/f_max, f_max the space factor from your record Bend radius: R_min = n_bend·d_max, d_max the largest cable diameter, n_bend from your record; each bend u_b = R_min/R Weight: w = Σ n_i·m_i (kg/m); force per metre w·g, g = 9.80665 m/s² Straight length: T₂ = T₁ + w·g·μ·L Bend: T₂ = T₁·e^(μθ) (capstan equation, θ in radians) Sidewall pressure at a bend: SWP = T₂/R (N/m) Direction: the route is run in the listed order and in reverse; the direction of lower maximum tension is recommended (equal tension: lower SWP) Limits: u_T = T_max/T_lim, T_lim = Σ n_i·T_max,i or your override; u_P = SWP_max/P_lim
  Ref: BS 7671:2018 (as amended) reg. 522.8 — Other mechanical stresses on wiring systems (damage during installation, bending radius)
  Ref: BS EN 61386-1 general — Conduit systems for cable management: general requirements
  Ref: Beer and Johnston, Vector Mechanics for Engineers: Statics Ch. 8 — Friction: belt friction (the capstan equation)

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Cables are circular and counted by overall diameter; there is no packing, jamming or cable and conduit factor method. The space factor is yours, from your own record. (Fill by geometry; the factor is a user record.)
  A2. Every cable is pulled together as one bundle on one pulling head, the load shared equally; the tension limit is the sum of the cable limits unless you override it with the limit of the weakest link. (Common pulling practice; override available.)
  A3. Straight lengths are horizontal and the cable weight bears on the containment over the full length; bends are taken in the plane of the route. Inclines, vertical runs, cradled or triangular weight corrections and pulley or roller reductions are not modelled; fold them into μ if needed. (Stated limitation of the straight-length relation T₂ = T₁ + w·g·μ·L.)
  A4. The coefficient of friction is one constant for the whole route; static and running friction are not distinguished. (A user record with its source.)
  A5. Sidewall pressure is the tension leaving the bend over the bend radius, for the bundle as a whole; enter the limit in the same terms (for a single cable it is the cable's own limit). (SWP = T₂/R.)
  A6. The tension at the start of the pull is the same in both directions; a bend at the very start of a pull with no start tension adds nothing, so enter the pulling rope or drum tension when it matters. (Capstan equation.)
