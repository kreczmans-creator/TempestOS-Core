Title: Pipe pressure drop and pump duty

1. Scope and method
Calculates the friction and fitting losses of series pipe segments at a given flow, the system curve and the duty point against a pump curve. It also gives the affinity-law shift for a speed or impeller change, and the NPSH.
Design flow and velocity: Q = Φ/(ρ c_p ΔT) when a heat load is given in place of a flow; v = Q/(πD²/4) Reynolds number and friction factor: Re = ρvD/μ; f = 64/Re for laminar flow (Re < 2300); Colebrook–White 1/√f = −2 log10(ε/3.7D + 2.51/(Re√f)) for turbulent flow, by fixed-point iteration (at most 100 steps) from the Swamee–Jain value, which also cross-checks it Head lost in a segment: h_f = f (L + L_eq)/D · v²/2g + K v²/2g System curve: H_sys(Q) = Σdz + h_out + Σh_f,lam·(Q/Q₀) + Σh_f,other·(Q/Q₀)², with a laminar segment's friction scaled as Q and every other friction and fitting loss as Q² (a closed circuit has Σdz = 0) Water held in the pipes: V_pipe = Σ π/4·D²·L Duty point: H_pump(Q) = H_sys(Q), every crossing found by a scan and refined by bisection, the stable one nearest the design flow reported; pump affinity laws Q ∝ n·D^a, H ∝ n²·D², with a = 1 for a trimmed impeller (the default) and a = 3 for a geometrically similar pump Net positive suction head available: NPSH_a = p_atm/ρg + z_s − h_f,suction − p_v/ρg, with h_f,suction that of the 'suction' segments at the larger of the design flow and the duty flow Shaft torque for the motor: T = P/ω, with P the larger of the design-flow and duty-point shaft power at the speed n_pump, or the shifted duty power at the shifted speed n_pump·n₂/n₁

3. Assumptions
  A1. Flow is steady, incompressible and fully developed, and all segments carry the full design flow in series. (Darcy–Weisbach.)
  A2. Fitting losses scale with velocity head (K method) or add pipe length (equivalent-length method). (Crane TP-410 practice.)
  A3. Both fitting methods may be used on one segment. (Crane TP-410 practice.)
  A4. The system curve scales turbulent and transitional friction and all fitting losses with Q² from the design flow. (H_sys = H_static + k₁ Q + k₂ Q².)
  A5. The friction factor is held at its design-flow value on the system curve. (H_sys = H_static + k₁ Q + k₂ Q².)
  A6. The friction of a laminar segment scales with Q, and the static part is independent of flow. (H_sys = H_static + k₁ Q + k₂ Q².)
  A7. Water properties are from published correlations at atmospheric pressure, 0 to 100 °C. (Kell 1975; Vogel fit; Hyland & Wexler 1983.)
  A8. Any other fluid is entered with its own density and viscosity and their provenance. (Stated.)
