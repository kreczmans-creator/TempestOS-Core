Title: Enclosure heat dissipation and fan sizing

1. Scope and method
Calculates the heat load of a control enclosure, its natural temperature rise and the airflow for a target rise. It also gives the fan duty point and the cooling-unit capacity needed when the limit cannot be met.
Heat dissipated inside the enclosure, plus any solar gain: P = Σ P_i n_i d_i (+ α I A_sun). Effective cooling surface, each face weighted by how freely it sheds heat: A_eff = Σ f_face × A_face. Temperature rise with natural cooling, one coefficient over the exposed faces (the IEC TR 60890 tables are not used): ΔT = P/(h_conv·A_eff). Air flow a fan must move to hold the target rise: V = P/(ρ c_p ΔT_target), with ρ = p(z)/(287.055 T) and c_p = 1006 J/(kg·K). Fan duty point, where the fan curve meets the system resistance (found by bisection): p_fan(V) = p_sys (V/V_ref)². Cooling unit capacity, when the surfaces alone cannot reject the heat: P_cool = P − h_conv·A_eff·(T_max − T_amb); with T_max at or below T_amb the term is a gain, P_cool = P + h_conv·A_eff·(T_amb − T_max), and no ventilation airflow or fan duty is given. Sealed enclosure (sealing temperature T_seal entered, no breather): Δp = p_site·|T_int/T_seal − 1|, the pressure difference across the walls when the air warms or cools from the sealing temperature at constant volume, in absolute temperatures (a rise when T_int > T_seal, a suction when below); a breather or relief element limits it, dp_seal = min(Δp, dp_relief); the force on a cover of area A_cover is F_seal = dp_seal·A_cover

3. Assumptions
  A1. Heat leaves the enclosure through its exposed faces at one overall coefficient h_conv. (Plain exposed-area method of manufacturer practice, not an IEC TR 60890 verification; h_conv is a reference value the user may edit.)
  A2. The coefficient covers natural convection and radiation to surroundings at the ambient temperature. (Plain exposed-area method of manufacturer practice, not an IEC TR 60890 verification; h_conv is a reference value the user may edit.)
  A3. Covered faces dissipate nothing. (Plain exposed-area method of manufacturer practice, not an IEC TR 60890 verification; h_conv is a reference value the user may edit.)
  A4. Forced ventilation removes the full heat load with air entering at ambient and leaving at the target rise. (Manufacturer fan-sizing practice.)
  A5. Natural dissipation is neglected for the airflow, which is conservative. (Manufacturer fan-sizing practice.)
  A6. A sealed enclosure (T_seal entered) is a closed, rigid, leak-free volume of dry air at the site pressure when closed. (Ideal gas at constant volume (derived).)
  A7. The pressure difference follows the ideal-gas relation at constant volume, from the sealing temperature to T_int. (Ideal gas at constant volume (derived).)
  A8. T_int is the internal temperature with natural dissipation, without a cooling unit. (Ideal gas at constant volume (derived).)
  A9. A breather, a seal that leaks or a flexing wall reduces the difference, so the result is an upper bound. (Ideal gas at constant volume (derived).)
  A10. A pressure-compensation element is modelled only as a cap at its cracking pressure, with no flow resistance. (Ideal gas at constant volume (derived).)
  A11. The site pressure is the standard atmosphere at the altitude. (Ideal gas at constant volume (derived).)
  A12. The fan curve is linear between the entered points. (Duty-point idealisation.)
  A13. The system resistance scales with airflow squared. (Duty-point idealisation.)
  A14. The effective surface is the plain exposed-area method of manufacturer practice: each face area weighted by how freely it sheds heat, one overall coefficient h. This is not a verification of temperature rise to IEC TR 60890 or BS EN 61439-1 cl. 10.10 (neither the surface factors nor the standard's temperature-rise correlation is used). (Stated on every run so the result is not read as an assembly verification.)

4. Working
  [18] P_loads = Σ P_i × n_i × d_i
      = 2 item(s)
      = 0.3 kW
        Internal heat load
  [19] P_solar = alpha_sun * I_sun * A_sun
      = 0.5 × 0 W/m² × 0 m²
      = 0 kW
        Solar gain
  [20] P_total = P_loads + P_add + P_solar
      = 0.3 kW + 0 kW + 0 kW
      = 0.3 kW
        Total heat to remove
  [21] A_eff = f_top * W * D + f_bottom * W * D + f_front * W * H + f_rear * W * H + f_left * H * D + f_right * H * D    « plain exposed-area method (manufacturer practice), not an IEC TR 60890 verification
      = 1 × 0.6 m × 0.3 m + 0 × 0.6 m × 0.3 m + 1 × 0.6 m × 0.8 m + 0 × 0.6 m × 0.8 m + 1 × 0.8 m × 0.3 m + 1 × 0.8 m × 0.3 m
      = 1.14 m²
        Effective dissipating surface
  [22] dT_nat = P_total / (h_conv * A_eff)
      = 0.3 kW / (5.5 W/(m²·K) × 1.14 m²)
      = 47.847 K
        Temperature rise with natural dissipation
  [23] T_int = T_amb + dT_nat
      = 35 degC + 47.847 K
      = 82.847 degC
        Internal temperature with natural dissipation
  [24] dT_allow = T_max - T_amb
      = 50 degC − 35 degC
      = 15 K
        Allowed rise
  [25] u_nat = dT_nat / dT_allow
      = 47.847 K / 15 K
      = 3.1898
        Natural dissipation utilisation
  [26] P_nat_max = h_conv * A_eff * dT_allow
      = 5.5 W/(m²·K) × 1.14 m² × 15 K
      = 0.09405 kW
        Heat the enclosure dissipates naturally at the limit
  [27] P_cool = max(0 W, P_total - P_nat_max)
      = max(0 W, 0.3 kW − 0.09405 kW)
      = 0.20595 kW
        Cooling-unit capacity needed if ventilation is not used
  [28] p_site = 101325 (1 − 2.25577e-5 z)^5.2559
      = z = 0 m
      = 101.32 kPa
        Site pressure
  [29] rho_air = p_site / (287.055 J/(kg*K) * T_amb)
      = 101.32 kPa / (287.055 J/(kg*K) × 35 degC)
      = 1.1455 kg/m³
        Air density at ambient and altitude
  [30] V_req = P_total / (rho_air * 1006 J/(kg*K) * dT_allow)
      = 0.3 kW / (1.1455 kg/m³ × 1006 J/(kg*K) × 15 K)
      = 62.481 m³/h
        Airflow for forced ventilation at the allowed rise (natural dissipation neglected)
  [31] V_req_net = max(0 m^3/h, (P_total - P_nat_max) / (rho_air * 1006 J/(kg*K) * dT_allow))
      = max(0 m^3/h, (0.3 kW − 0.09405 kW) / (1.1455 kg/m³ × 1006 J/(kg*K) × 15 K))
      = 42.893 m³/h
        Airflow crediting natural dissipation
