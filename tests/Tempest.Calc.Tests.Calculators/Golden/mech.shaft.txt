Title: Shaft design

1. Scope and method
A stepped (optionally hollow) shaft on two bearings carrying radial loads in two planes and a torque with a steady (mean) part and an optional alternating part: bending moment, slope and deflection along it, the fatigue and yield safety factors at the sections the user marks, slopes at the bearings, deflection at the loads, the first lateral critical speed and parallel keys.
Shaft bending moment: the shaft as an S2 beam (pin at A, roller at B) in each plane; M = √(M_y² + M_z²) Stress at a section: σ_a = K_f·32M_a·d/(π(d⁴ − d_i⁴)), τ_m = K_fs·16T_m·d/(π(d⁴ − d_i⁴)), τ_a = K_fs·16T_a·d/(π(d⁴ − d_i⁴)); K_f = 1 + q(K_t − 1) Endurance limit: S_e = k_a·k_b·k_c·k_d·k_e·k_f·S_e1, S_e1 (the rotating-beam limit S_e′) = 0.5·S_ut (S_ut ≤ 1400 MPa, else 700 MPa); k_a = a·S_ut^b, k_b = 1.24d^−0.107 or 1.51d^−0.157, k_e = 1 − 0.08·z_R Fatigue (DE-Goodman, bending fully reversed, torque T_m ± T_a): 1/n_f = √(σ_a² + 3τ_a²)/S_e + √3·τ_m/S_ut; yield n_y = S_y/√((σ_a)² + 3τ_pk²), τ_pk from the larger of T_m + T_a and the peak torque T_pk when given Critical speed: Rayleigh ω = √(g·Σm·y/Σm·y²), y signed (an overhung mass lifts the span, which lowers the quotient); Dunkerley 1/ω² = Σ m_i·δ_ii (lower bound, used for the ratio to the operating speed) Weight loads (with w_masses): F_y,i = −m_i·g at each node, m_i the carried mass plus the shaft's own mass split to the node; without it the loads table is the whole load Parallel key, on the peak torque T_k = the larger of T_m + T_a and T_pk: τ = 2T_k/(d·w·L), σ = 4T_k/(d·h·L); required length L = max(2T_k·n_target/(d·w·0.577·S_y,key), 4T_k·n_target/(d·h·S_y,min)), S_y,min the lowest of the key, the shaft and (when entered) the hub; a shoulder is checked on both sides and the lower n_f governs

3. Assumptions
  A1. Linear elastic, two simple bearing supports (pin at A, roller at B); bending fully reversed by rotation; torque a steady part T_m plus an alternating part T_a about it (fully alternating when T_m = 0), both acting between the torque-in and torque-out positions. (Shigley 10th ed. §7-4.)
  A2. No torsional vibration; only the first lateral mode, by Rayleigh with Dunkerley as a lower bound. (Shigley 10th ed. §7-6.)
  A3. K_t and K_ts come from user records or published fit equations; no stress-concentration chart is reproduced. (No stress-concentration chart is shipped; the factors are entered or derived.)
  A4. At a shoulder both sides are checked, each on its own diameter and bore, and the lower fatigue safety factor governs; k_c = 1 because the stresses are combined by von Mises. (Shigley 10th ed. §6-9, §7-4.)
  A5. The loads table is the whole load: a carried mass adds no weight to the reactions, the moment or the deflection, and neither does the shaft's own weight, unless w_masses is on (which adds m·g at the nodes in the vertical plane). (Stated limitation; w_masses adds the weights.)
  A6. Loads, sections and steps closer together than 1/200 of the shaft length share one node. (Keeps the beam model well conditioned.)
  A7. The torque leaves at the shaft end (400 mm): x_T2 is blank. (Every section beyond the torque entry carries the torque; enter x_T2 for a torque taken off part-way.)
