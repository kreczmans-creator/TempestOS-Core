Title: Insulation thickness (ISO 12241 formula route)

1. SCOPE AND METHOD
The insulation thickness on a pipe (cylinder) or a flat surface that meets a heat-flow limit, a limit on the surface temperature of a hot surface and an anti-condensation requirement for a cold one, each solved separately and the governing one reported; and for a chosen thickness the heat flow, the surface temperature and PASS/FAIL per criterion. Steady state, one layer, no thickness table of any standard.
Pipe resistance per metre: R = ln(D_e/D_i)/(2π·λ) + 1/(h_se·π·D_e), D_e = D_i + 2t; with the inner film and pipe wall R_oth = 1/(h_i·π·D_p) + ln(D_i/D_p)/(2π·λ_w) added in series Flat resistance per square metre: R = t/λ + 1/h_se (+ 1/h_i + t_w/λ_w) Heat flow: q = (θ_i − θ_a)/R, positive from the process to the ambient Surface temperature: θ_se = θ_a + q/(h_se·π·D_e) (pipe), θ_a + q/h_se (flat) Surface coefficient: h_se as given, or h_se = h_c + h_r with h_r = ε·σ·(T_s² + T_a²)(T_s + T_a); h_c from free convection to air (laminar or turbulent, the larger), forced convection (cross-flow cylinder or flat plate) and their cube-law blend; solved with θ_se, which it depends on Heat-loss limit: the thickness beyond which |q| ≤ q_max; surface-temperature limit (hot): θ_se ≤ θ_se,max; anti-condensation (cold): θ_se ≥ θ_dew + margin, θ_dew entered (for example from B2) or from the Magnus relation at θ_a and RH Solve: each criterion's thickness by scanning t from 0 to 1 m for the last broken point and bisecting the bracket after it; governing t = max over the selected criteria Critical diameter: D_crit = 2λ/h_se; below it, insulation raises the heat flow until D_e passes D_crit
  Ref: ISO 12241 formula route (clause numbers to be checked) — Thermal insulation for building equipment and industrial installations: calculation rules
  Ref: Incropera, Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer 7th ed., §7.2, §7.4, §9.6, §9.9 (to be checked) — External forced, free and mixed convection correlations
  Ref: ISO 13788 cl. 6 (clause number to be checked against the owner's copy) — Magnus saturation vapour pressure (shared with B7)

3. ASSUMPTIONS
  A1. Steady one-dimensional heat flow through a single homogeneous layer of constant conductivity; no thermal bridges at supports, no joints, no moisture in the insulation. λ is taken at the value entered: use the value at the mean insulation temperature shown on the sheet (optionally noted in 'Temperature the conductivity applies at'). (Formula route of the standard.)
  A2. With the correlation, h_se = h_c + h_r. Radiation: h_r = ε·σ·(T_s² + T_a²)(T_s + T_a) to surroundings at the ambient air temperature. Free convection to air: Holman's simplified relations at atmospheric pressure, the larger of the laminar and turbulent forms (horizontal cylinder 1.32(ΔT/D)^0.25 and 1.24ΔT^(1/3); vertical surface 1.42(ΔT/L)^0.25 and 1.31ΔT^(1/3)). Forced convection: Churchill–Bernstein for a cylinder in cross-flow, the laminar/mixed flat-plate relations along a plate; air properties from fits at the film temperature, Pr = 0.71; blended with free convection as (h_nat³ + h_forced³)^(1/3). Indicative, not the standard's own correlation. (Incropera 7th ed. §7.2, §7.4, §9.6 free convection and §9.9 mixed convection (section numbers to be checked against the owner's copy); the free-convection constants are Holman's, Heat Transfer; the standard's own coefficients are not reproduced.)
  A3. The dew point is entered (for example from B2) or is the Magnus relation at θ_a and RH, the same closed form B7 uses. Below 0 °C the relation is over ice, so RH is then taken relative to ice, whereas meteorological RH is usually relative to water: enter the dew point for sub-zero air. A margin above it is the user's. (Derived; B7 shares the function.)
  A4. Pipes are treated as horizontal for free convection and flat surfaces as vertical; air properties are fits (conductivity within about 3 %, viscosity within about 4 % over 250–400 K), Pr = 0.71. (Stated.)
  A5. The required thickness is the least thickness beyond which the criterion holds for every thicker layer up to 1 m, which is what matters when thin insulation on a small pipe raises the heat flow (critical diameter). (Stated.)
  A6. No thickness table is used or shipped: not BS 5422, not the tables of any standard. The conductivity is the user's input, from a W3 material or the maker's data. (Addendum B §B5.)

4. WORKING
  [10] t_q = t such that |q| ≤ 30 W/m for every thicker layer    « ISO 12241
      = scan 0 … 1000 mm, then bisection
      = 28.552 mm
        Required thickness, heat-loss limit
  [11] t_se = t such that θ_se ≤ 45 °C for every thicker layer    « ISO 12241
      = scan 0 … 1000 mm, then bisection
      = 5.3259 mm
        Required thickness, surface-temperature limit
  [12] thickness = max over the selected criteria    « ISO 12241
      = t_q = 28.552 mm, t_se = 5.3259 mm
      = 28.552 mm
        Governing required thickness (heat-loss limit)
  [14] D_e = D_i + 2t
      = 100 mm + 2 × 30 mm
      = 160 mm
        Outer diameter of the insulation, chosen thickness
  [15] R_ins = ln(D_e/D_i)/(2π·λ)    « ISO 12241
      = ln(160/100)/(2π × 0.04)
      = 1.8701 m·K/W
        Resistance of the insulation
  [16] R_se = 1/(h_se·π·D_e)    « ISO 12241
      = 1/(10 × π × 0.16 m)
      = 0.19894 m·K/W
        Resistance of the outer surface
  [17] R = R_oth + R_ins + R_se    « ISO 12241
      = 0 + 1.8701 + 0.19894
      = 2.069 m·K/W
        Total resistance per metre
  [18] q_per_m = |θ_i − θ_a| / R    « ISO 12241
      = 60 K / 2.069
      = 28.999 W/m
        Heat flow per metre of pipe (heat loss, magnitude)
  [19] theta_se = θ_a + q/(h_se·π·D_e)    « ISO 12241
      = 20 °C + 28.999 / 5.0265
      = 25.769 degC
        Outer surface temperature
  [20] theta_m = (θ_i + θ_se)/2
      = (80 + 25.769)/2
      = 52.885 degC
        Mean temperature of the insulation: take λ at this temperature
  [21] D_crit = 2λ/h_se at the bare surface    « ISO 12241
      = 2 × 0.04 / 10
      = 8 mm
        Critical diameter at the bare-surface h_se: below it, insulation raises the heat flow
  [22] u_q = |q| / q_max
      = |q| ≤ 30 W/m
      = 0.96664
        Utilisation, heat-loss limit
  [23] u_se = (θ_se − θ_a)/(θ_se,max − θ_a)
      = θ_se ≤ 45 °C
      = 0.23077
        Utilisation, surface-temperature limit
